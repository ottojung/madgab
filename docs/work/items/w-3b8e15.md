# Charge a real homophone a real cost: the fuzzy matcher's cost model

## Goal

Make `FuzzyLexicon::matches_at` charge a cost that reflects English
phonemic confusability, so that a genuine near-homophonic resegmentation
of a target is *cheap* in the matcher's own currency and therefore
reachable by the search's cost-best-first traversal inside the budgets
the search already respects.

This is the last untried lever on the primary milestone. Every
enumeration-side lever is now closed by measurement, and the residual is a
single number; see [w-4b1e07](w-4b1e07.md) and the closed items
[w-6b2f04](w-6b2f04.md), [w-7b41d2](w-7b41d2.md), [w-3e5c7](w-3e5c7.md),
[w-8f3c61](w-8f3c61.md) and [w-9d4e17](w-9d4e17.md).

## Context: why this is the remaining lever

The canonical clue `hits justice dupe hid came` for `It's just a stupid
game` is a real, well-known Mad Gab: it is a *near-exact* homophonic
resegmentation, so in a correct cost model its words should cost almost
nothing. Measured through the public API on the accumulation branch, they
do not:

```text
hits 0.20   justice 0.00   dupe 0.15   hid 0.37   came 0.40   (total 1.12)
```

The cost the search optimises is exactly this number, summed over slots.
Because the traversal is best-first in that cost, a clue that is charged
1.12 against a `total_budget` of 1.5 is, by construction, a very expensive
wording: it sits at emission 23 962 of its own segmentation, and
[w-8f3c61](w-8f3c61.md) §8 bounds the cost of surfacing it by selection or
order alone at **>= 134 400 emissions**, 8.2x the whole global emission
budget. The bound is a property of the *cost ordering*, not of the
selection rule. Lower the cost of the alignment and the same traversal
reaches it in its cheapest corner.

Where the cost comes from, in `src/approx.rs`:

- `GAP_COST = 0.20` is charged flat for an insertion or a deletion, with no
  regard for whether the lost or extra segment is one a listener reliably
  misses (a schwa, a released stop, a `h`) or a segment that carries the
  word;
- the substitution table is `phonetics::distance` between two *single IPA
  symbols* (see `substitution_costs` construction), i.e. a generic feature
  distance between characters, not a measure of how confusable two English
  phonemes are to a listener. So ordinary English vowel alternations —
  the `eɪ`/`æ`/`ɛ` family (`came`/`game`, `pen`/`pain`, `cap`/`cap`),
  lax/tense pairs, the unstressed-vowel reductions that dominate connected
  speech, r-coloured vowels, and the orthography-driven irregularities that
  make `dupe` and `hid` the spellings a listener would write — are charged
  like arbitrary foreign substitutions;
- the matcher's word inventory is scored per word with no notion of
  prosody or of word-boundary leniency, even though Mad Gabs are defined
  by what connected speech does to the boundaries.

Two general mechanisms are in scope, and either or both may be tried:

1. **A phonemic confusability substitution cost** in place of the generic
   per-symbol feature distance — vowel-class membership, lax/tense and
   length alternations, and the standard reduction/folding families, with
   the table derived from the same corpus alphabet the matcher already
   uses rather than hand-listed per word.
2. **A folding/normalisation of the IPA on both sides before comparison**
   (the familiar ARPAbet-style vowel and consonant-class folding used by
   ASR front ends), so that two pronunciations a listener would call the
   same compare as equal or near-equal.

Both must be expressed as general rules over symbols or symbol classes.
Neither may name a word, a sentence, or any substring of a canonical
example.

```text
MADGAB_TRACE span_slot=0 span=0-3  word="hits"    cost=Some(0.2)
MADGAB_TRACE span_slot=1 span=3-10 word="justice" cost=Some(0.0)

MADGAB_TRACE span_slot=3 span=13-15 word="hid"    cost=Some(0.3695198141657896)
MADGAB_TRACE span_slot=4 span=15-19 word="came"   cost=Some(0.4)
```

Exactly the filed numbers. Target stream `ɪtsdʒʌstəstupədɡeɪm` (19 symbols);
clue-side IPAs from the same corpus: `hɪts`, `dʒʌstəs`, `dup`, `hɪd`,
`keɪm`.

Per-word attribution, each edit named:

| slot | target | clue | cost | the edit that produces it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hits | `ɪts` | `hɪts` | 0.200 | one **insertion** of `h` (a flat `GAP_COST`) |
| justice | `dʒʌstəs` | `dʒʌstəs` | 0.000 | exact |
| dupe | `tup` | `dup` | 0.150 | one **substitution** `t`→`d` |
| hid | `əd` | `hɪd` | 0.3695 | `0.200` insertion of `h` + `0.16952` substitution `ə`→`ɪ` |
| came | `ɡeɪm` | `keɪm` | 0.400 | one **deletion** of `ɡ` + one **insertion** of `k` — *not* a substitution |

`total 1.1195`.

### The decisive measurement: the substitution table is a *codepoint* distance, not a phoneme distance

`phonetics::distance` is applied to two single *characters*, and the
corpus alphabet is a **mixed transliteration** — some phonemes are
spelled with Latin letters and some with IPA characters, arbitrarily.
The consequence, measured over all 75 corpus symbols (5550 distinct-symbol
pairs):

| statistic | value |
|---|---|
| mean `phonetics::distance` over distinct symbol pairs | **0.7825** |
| distinct-symbol pairs costing more than `GAP_COST` (0.20) | **5204 / 5550 = 93.8 %** |
| pairs in `[0.8,1.0)` | 3874 (69.8 %) |

So the substitution branch is **effectively dead**: almost any
substitution is dearer than deleting a symbol *and* inserting one
(0.40), so the search routes around it with indels. Measured examples:

| pair | measured | a listener |
|---|---|---|
| `k` → `ɡ` (U+006B vs U+0261) | **1.000** | the same place, one voicing cue |
| `t` → `d` | 0.150 | one voicing cue (accidentally right) |
| `p`→`b`, `f`→`v`, `s`→`z`, `θ`→`ð`, `ʃ`→`ʒ` | 0.150 | one voicing cue (accidentally right) |
| `ə` → `ɐ` | **1.000** | both central vowels |
| `ə` → `ɚ` | **1.000** | same vowel, r-colouring |
| `h` → `t` | 0.387 | both voiceless coronals |
| `ɡ` → `x` | 1.000 | same place |

`phonetics::distance` is right about `t`/`d` only by accident: `t` and `d`
are adjacent letters. It has no notion of place, manner or voicing, and
because one side of many pairs is an IPA character rather than a Latin
letter, it returns the maximum 1.0. That is exactly the `came` slot:
`ɡ`/`k` measures 1.0, so the search deletes and reinserts instead
(0.40) — **0.40 of the 1.12 is spent on a codepoint artifact, and the
true phonemic cost of that slot is 0.15 at most.**

### Which of the two in-scope mechanisms addresses the measured cost

**Mechanism 2 (IPA folding/normalisation) is refuted for this corpus, and
mechanism 1 (a phonemic confusability cost) is the whole lever.**

The reason is measurable, not a matter of taste: the corpus alphabet has
**no codepoint duplication** — 75 distinct symbols, exactly one codepoint
per phoneme, with no word spelling the same phoneme two ways. There is
consequently nothing for a fold to merge, and folding cannot help. The
ASCII letters are not a second spelling of an IPA phoneme that also
occurs in IPA form: `k` (ASCII) is the voiceless velar and `ɡ` (U+0261)
is the voiced velar, and they are *supposed* to differ. A fold that
merged them would be wrong phonetically; a fold that kept them apart
would still leave them at distance 1.0. The entire error is the absence
of a confusability metric, so the substitution cost has to be rebuilt
over articulatory features.

Attribution of the 1.12 across mechanisms:

| component | amount | nature |
|---|---|---|
| `came` slot | 0.400 | **pure artifact** — a real voicing pair at distance 1.0 |
| `hits` + `hid` `h`-insertions | 0.400 | a real weak-segment indel, but charged a flat full `GAP_COST` |
| `hid` `ə`→`ɪ` | 0.170 | a real unstressed-vowel reduction, currently 1 step in an undefined metric |
| `dupe` `t`→`d` | 0.150 | a real voicing pair, accidentally priced correctly |
| `justice` | 0.000 | exact |

## Step 2/3 — what was implemented, and what it measured

Two general mechanisms landed in `src/approx.rs` and nowhere else, both
expressed over articulatory classes of the corpus symbol inventory. No
word, phrase, IPA symbol exception, or substring of a canonical example
appears in `src/` production code; the tests assert properties of the
rules over symbol classes only.

1. **Confusability substitution cost.** `phonetics::distance` between
   two codepoints is replaced by a weighted feature distance. Each
   symbol is described as a place/manner/voicing triple (consonants) or
   a height/backness/rounding/reduction vector (vowels); the cost is
   the weighted distance, capped at two gaps because above that the
   cheaper indel already exists, and discounted when either side is a
   reduced vowel. Weights are ordered by how reliably a listener
   recovers each cue.
2. **Indel cost by class.** The flat `GAP_COST` is scaled by how
   *obligatory* the segment is, from the same description: a reduced
   central vowel and a glottal fricative are segments English routinely
   fails to produce, a sonorant is partly masked, and a stop or full
   vowel carries the word and pays the flat cost.

### The mechanism works, exactly as designed

Per-word costs on the canonical segmentation, via
`MADGAB_TRACE_SPANS`/`MADGAB_TRACE_WORDS` on a release binary:

| slot | target | clue | before | after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hits | `ɪts` | `hɪts` | 0.200 | **0.050** |
| justice | `dʒʌstəs` | `dʒʌstəs` | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| dupe | `tup` | `dup` | 0.150 | **0.100** |
| hid | `əd` | `hɪd` | 0.3695 | **0.115** |
| came | `ɡeɪm` | `keɪm` | 0.400 | **0.100** |
| **total** | | | **1.1195** | **0.3650** |

A 3.07x reduction, and every individual edit is now charged the right
*kind* of cost: `came` is a substitution at slot rank 0 rather than a
0.40 delete+insert detour around a codepoint artifact, and the two `h`
insertions cost 0.05 each rather than a full gap.

|---|---|---|---|
| It's just a stupid game | `hits justice dupe hid came` | not enumerated | **not enumerated** |

The canonical clue was not enumerated before and is not enumerated now,

### Why: the cost model is not the binding constraint, and lowering it uniformly does not discriminate

The decisive measurement is the **rank within each slot**, which is the
quantity the best-first traversal actually consumes:

| slot | cost before → after | rank in slot before → after |
|---|---|---|
| hits | 0.200 → 0.050 | 22 → **27** |
| justice | 0.000 → 0.000 | 0 → 0 |
| dupe | 0.150 → 0.100 | 64 → **92** |
| hid | 0.3695 → 0.115 | 85 → **86** |
| came | 0.400 → 0.100 | 7 → **0** |

Three of the five slots got *more* expensive to reach in rank terms
while getting cheaper in cost terms. The mechanism is scale, not
discrimination: every candidate in the pool got cheaper by roughly the
same factor, so their relative order is preserved and the canonical
clue's position in the enumeration is essentially unchanged. Cheaper is
not the same as earlier.

Confirmed independently by sweeping the budgets, which are the only
other thing a lower cost could have bought:

| `--total-budget` | base | new |
|---|---|---|
| 1.5 (default) | not enumerated | not enumerated |
| 3.0 | not enumerated | not enumerated |
| 6.0 | not enumerated | not enumerated |
| 12.0 (8x) | not enumerated | not enumerated |

At 8x the default `total_budget` the clue is still not enumerated on
either tree, so the cost level is not what is holding it back.

And the population effect is the opposite of what a discriminating
re-weighting would produce — the pool *grows*, because a cheaper cost
admits more alignments into the enumeration rather than promoting the
right one to the front of it:

| target | pool before | pool after | top-3000 cutoff before → after |
|---|---|---|---|
| It's just a stupid game | 17 405 | **18 797** | 0.8785 → 0.8914 |
| recognize speech | 14 423 | **16 415** | 0.8512 → 0.8432 |
| congratulations on your promotion | 9 230 | **13 621** | 0.6869 → 0.7877 |

This is consistent with [w-be6d21](w-be6d21.md), which measured the
global emission/pop budgets to be provably non-binding and inert, and
which identifies per-slot width (`LEXICAL_BRANCH_KEEP`) as the binding
constraint. The cost model is not what stands between the search and
the canonical clue.

### The regression this costs

`approximate_finds_recognize_speech_resegmentation` **passes at the
rebase base `2a81e7b` and fails on this branch.** Isolated by bisecting
my own commits: the substitution table alone (commit `a74615a`, before
the indel rule) is sufficient to cause it. `wreck a nice beach` is a
correct answer at base (rank 27, score 0.918) and is dropped by a
uniformly cheaper cost model that promotes ~2 000 wrong alignments
alongside it.

This is the same non-discrimination, seen from the other side: the
canonical clue is not rescued, and a previously-correct answer is lost.
`approximate_output_is_locked` also fails and would need re-baselining;
per the item's own fence, a silent re-baseline is a rejection, and it is
**not** re-baselined here, precisely because the change does not buy the
milestone and costs a correct answer.

### Verdict

**Refuted, with numbers.** The premise of the item — that the canonical
clue is expensive because the matcher charges a homophone too much, and
that making it cheap will make it reachable — is false for this search.
The cost was indeed mis-measured (mean 0.78, 93.8% of distinct-symbol
pairs dearer than a gap, the two velar stops at 1.0), and fixing that
measurement is a genuine improvement to the matcher's semantics and
lands the per-word costs where a listener would put them. But the
traversal consumes *rank*, not cost, and a uniform rescaling leaves rank
unchanged. The residual is per-slot width, which this item may not
touch.
