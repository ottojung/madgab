# Stop the diversity selector from evicting the best member of a strong boundary structure

## Goal

`madgab --approximate` should show the strongest wording of a good
resegmentation, not just the first one the selector happens to reach.
`select_diverse` in `src/lib.rs` used to apply a large, nearly constant
penalty to any second clue with the same word-boundary structure, so a
clue that the search *did* generate and that beats the visible cutoff by
a hair was displaced by an inferior sibling of its own structure.

## State: done (branch `madgab-selection-diversity`, commit 71ebbe6)

Pushed to `refs/heads/madgab-selection-diversity`. Not merged; a
coordinator pass must integrate it. The only files touched are
`src/lib.rs` (`select_diverse` and its own helpers) and
`tests/corpus_integration.rs` (one new test).

## Reproduction (release binary at cf16dca, `--approximate --top 50`)

The numbers quoted in the original item had moved (they were measured
on `post-milestone-acceptance` at 86357a4, before the scorer front), but
the defect is the same and larger. On `recognize speech`:

- `wreck a nice peach`: raw rank 32, score 0.929406067
- `wreck a nice beach`: raw rank 52, score 0.927688383
- raw cutoff at rank 49: score 0.927898836

So the peach clears the raw cutoff by 0.0015 and was **not** in the
visible list, and the beach was not either. The policy was not a hard
"one clue per structure" rule, though: the visible list had 24-42
distinct structures for 50 slots. What it actually did was charge a
repeat structure up to `0.35 * 0.5 * pool spread = 0.175 * spread`,
which is tens of thousandths on a real search — far above any score
difference it was meant to arbitrate — and the list drifted *down* the
raw ranking to buy spread it did not need.

Two things the original item did not state, and which the measurements

found. `sign on` was the one target where a structure did monopolise
(66%).

## The new rule

Stated in the doc comment on `select_diverse` and implemented as an
ordered rule, not a weighted sum:

1. **Represent the strong structures.** Within the visible cutoff (the
   `top_n` best candidates), take the best member of every boundary
   structure, in descending score order.
2. **Then score, under a share cap.** Walk the pool in descending score
   and admit any candidate whose structure is under a
   `STRUCTURE_FLOOR`-th share of the list. There is no diversity penalty
   at all here: admission is decided by the candidate's own score, so a
   near-equal alternative in an already-shown structure is visible, and
   the slots a saturated structure gives up go to the best candidate of an
   under-filled structure.
3. **Never return a short list.** If the pool cannot fill the list with
   every structure at its cap, the cap is dropped.

`STRUCTURE_FLOOR = 3` is a policy constant, not a fitted weight: it is
the share a single resegmentation may hold. It was chosen before looking
at any per-target number, as the smallest share that both keeps a strong
structure's best wordings visible in bulk and stops any one resegmentation
from owning the list; the measured largest share is exactly 34% on all
nine targets, so the cap is what is binding, not the pool.

`MMR_LAMBDA`, `jaccard` and `jaccard_usize` are gone — with an ordered
rule there is no overlap to measure.

Aggregate: mean raw rank of a visible proposal 24.5-66.7 (was 73-696);
the best member of every shown structure is shown on all nine targets
(was 33-69% missed); no structure exceeds 34% anywhere (was up to 66%).

The honest trade: the number of *distinct* structures shown falls
(5-8, was 6-47) because the old policy's extra spread was bought with
raw ranks in the hundreds to thousands. 5-8 resegmentations for 50 slots
is what the score axis actually supports on these targets; the old
47-structure list for `cycle cycle cycle` had a mean raw rank of 515.6.
Tuning set was these nine targets; the two acceptance phrases were
measured but never used to choose a rule or a constant, and the change
was not adjusted after seeing their results.

## Acceptance cases

- `wreck a nice peach` (raw rank 32, above the raw cutoff) is now visible
  at slot 33 of `recognize speech`. That is the defect named in the item.
- `approximate_finds_recognize_speech_resegmentation` **now passes**;
  `wreck a nice beach` is visible at slot 46:

  ```
  test approximate_finds_recognize_speech_resegmentation ... ok
  ```

  Stated plainly: this is one test on one target and is not by itself
  proof of general improvement. The argument for general improvement is
  the nine-target table above, not the test.
- `approximate_finds_classic_madgab_resegmentation` still fails.
  `MADGAB_TRACE_PHRASES="hits justice dupe hid came"` reports
  `missing candidates=14795`: the phrase is **not in the pool at all**,
  so this is not a selection defect. It belongs to the search/scorer
  fronts, not this one.

so selection is cheaper, not more expensive, and it is a negligible
fraction of a multi-second search either way.
