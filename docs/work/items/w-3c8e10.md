# Stop the diversity selector from evicting the best member of a strong boundary structure

## Goal

`madgab --approximate` should show the strongest wording of a good
resegmentation, not just the first one the selector happens to reach.
Today `select_diverse` in `src/lib.rs` applies a large, nearly constant
penalty to any second clue with the same word-boundary structure, so a
clue that the search *did* generate and that beats the visible cutoff by a
hair is displaced by an inferior sibling of its own structure.

## Context

Residual of [w-4b1e07](w-4b1e07.md); independent of the content-word
front ([w-9c2d51](w-9c2d51.md)), which is changing the score itself.

Measured on `post-milestone-acceptance` at 86357a4, release binary,
`--approximate --top 50`:

- `recognize speech` takes 5.2 s of search. The visible list is dominated
  by a few boundary structures, and Antonina agent `a1b2c303` measured
  `wreck a nice peach` at score 0.9234 raw rank 18 while
  `wreck a nice beach` sits at 0.9214 raw rank 27 — same boundary
  structure, so the diversity penalty evicts the weaker-but-still-excellent
  one, and the milestone regression
  `approximate_finds_recognize_speech_resegmentation` fails on that
  tie-break alone. `a1b2c303` correctly refused to chase it by tuning
  weights, because that is phrase-specific tuning.
- The selector's policy is a hard "one clue per structure" effect: with
  `MMR_LAMBDA = 0.35`, weight `0.5` on cut-Jaccard, and the penalty scaled
  by the *full pool* score spread, a repeat structure costs up to
  `0.175 * spread`. Against a real search the spread is tens of
  thousandths, so the penalty is far larger than the score differences it
  is supposed to arbitrate. The fix is to make the penalty *ordered*: a
  repeat of an already-picked structure should be priced against the
  actual score gap it would have to overcome, and repeated structures
  should be allowed to contribute a bounded second member when that member
  is close in score, so the visible list is not one-per-structure by
  accident.
- This is a *policy* defect, not a scoring one, and it is general: it
  affects every target, and any fix must be justified by proposal quality
  measured over a spread of targets, not by the two canonical examples.

## Completion criteria

- The diversity policy no longer has the property that a second clue from
  an already-picked boundary structure is effectively unreachable, while
  still preventing a single structure from monopolising the list. The
  policy is stated in the doc comment as a rule, not as a tuned constant
  fitted to a case.
- Structural diversity is not regressed: the existing general test at
  `src/lib.rs` (`select_diverse` unit tests) still passes, and for a
  spread of targets no single boundary structure occupies most of the
  visible proposal list.
- A new regression test at the `select_diverse` unit boundary expresses
  the property "a near-equal-scoring alternative in an already-picked
  structure can be selected, while a far-worse one is not", using a
  synthetic pool, not a real target.
- Improvement is justified by measurements over a spread of real targets
  recorded in this item, explicitly not tuned against `recognize speech`
  or `It's just a stupid game`.
- `cargo test --release --lib` and
  `cargo test --release --test corpus_integration` are green apart from
  the pre-existing acceptance regressions, and search wall clock does not
  regress (baseline on this commit: `recognize speech` 5.2 s search,
  `It's just a stupid game` 7.0 s search).
- No phrase-specific hard-coding for the two canonical examples.
- Work is committed on a focused branch and integrated into
  `post-milestone-acceptance`, never into `main`.

sibling that used to evict it is gone, and what remains is the general
MMR redundancy penalty against the other boundary-identical survivors the
front itself identified (`wreck a nice peach`, `rica nice beach`,
`reckon i.'s beach`).

Do not read that as a licence to tune the penalty until the phrase appears.
The general property to fix is the one already written in the Completion
criteria: a near-equal-scoring alternative inside an already-picked
structure must be selectable, while a far-worse one must not, and no single
structure may occupy most of the visible list. The new
`approximate_proposals_are_predominantly_content_words` test in
`tests/corpus_integration.rs` also asserts that no resegmentation occupies
more than half of the visible list, so a fix that simply drops the
redundancy term wholesale will fail a test that did not exist when this
item was written.

Wall-clock baseline for the "does not regress" criterion is unchanged and
re-measured during this pass: 4.7 s wall for `recognize speech`
(3.9 s search), 5.9 s wall for `It's just a stupid game` (5.8 s search).
