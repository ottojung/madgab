## Scope fence: what this front must not do

[w-6b2f04](w-6b2f04.md) owns the **global budget across segmentations** and
the enumeration loop that spends it. To keep the two fronts composable
rather than contending:

- Do **not** implement a global emission/pop counter, a per-segmentation
  share, round-robin budget division, structure-aware budget allocation, or
  shared-prefix/trie work across segmentations. Those are `6b2f04`'s
  mechanisms 1, 2 and 3. If your audit concludes one of them is the only
  thing that works, write that in this item as a finding and stop.
- Do **not** change `LEXICAL_BRANCH_KEEP`, `LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION`
  or `LEXICAL_HEAP_POP_LIMIT`; the measurements above price those.
- Do **not** move `axes::*` weights, the scoring functions, or the
  `approximate_output_is_locked` baseline.
- Do **not** touch `approximate_finds_classic_madgab_resegmentation` or relax
  any assertion.

A mechanism that lives in a *different* place — for example the composition
or ordering of the per-span shortlist that feeds each slot, or the
retention/ordering of segmentations entering the enumeration, or a pruning
that removes work rather than reallocating it — is in scope, provided you
first show by measurement that it changes recall for a spread of real
targets and not just for the canonical pair.

### 2. The mechanism audit: every in-scope lever is refuted by measurement

The scope fence allows three places. Each was tested by **forcing the
canonical alignment to the front of every slot** — the strongest possible
version of "compose or order the per-span shortlist" — and observing whether
it is then enumerated. If that oracle-forced variant does not enumerate the
clue, no real reordering scheme can.

**In-scope lever (a): composition/ordering of the per-span shortlist feeding
each slot. REFUTED — and this is the decisive experiment of the pass.**

Instrumented result on the canonical segmentation
`[(0,3),(3,10),(10,13),(13,15),(15,19)]`, with all five canonical words moved to
index 0 of their slot and the per-segmentation emission/pop caps lifted for
that one segmentation only (emission 200000, pop 20,000,000):

```text
MADGAB_FORCE fired spans=[(0, 3), (3, 10), (10, 13), (13, 15), (15, 19)]
             ["hits@0", "justice@0", "dupe@0", "hid@0", "came@0"]
MADGAB_RANK  canonical reached at emission 23962 (pop 28930)
MADGAB_TRACE raw phrase="hits justice dupe hid came" rank=28573 score=0.819901291
```

So the canonical alignment is at **emission 23962 and pool rank 28573** of
*its own segmentation* even when it is the single best-scoring choice in all
five slots simultaneously. `LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION = 64` stops
at 64. This is a much stronger negative than "the cap binds": it says the
canonical alignment is roughly **375x deeper** than the emission budget even
under a per-slot oracle, and the real cap is 10-deep at slot 3, so under the
real configuration it is deeper still. **No ordering of the per-span shortlist
can reach it, because ordering only decides *which* 10 of 160 are tried, and
the clue is not in the best 23962 of that segmentation's product under any
ordering at all.** This also retires the framing in the "established facts"
section above that the clue "requires the fan-out to reach rank 99 at slot 3":
reaching rank 99 at slot 3 is necessary but nowhere near sufficient, and the
walk rank was never the binding quantity.

**In-scope lever (b): retention/ordering of segmentations entering the
enumeration. REFUTED.** The canonical segmentation is *retained*, at rank 151
of 256 (measured above), and the loop at `src/lib.rs:907` iterates **all**
retained segmentations unconditionally. Nothing prunes, defers or reorders
segmentations ahead of it. There is no retention gap to close: the segmentation
is already being enumerated, and (a) shows its own product search is what
misses.

**In-scope lever (c): pruning that removes work rather than reallocating it.
REFUTED.** The two pruning mechanisms on this path are the span-lattice
shortlist fill (`SPAN_SHORTLIST = 160`, and all five words survive it — measured
above) and the `prune_partials` beam retention. Both are already *non-binding*
for this clue: nothing about this target is being pruned away. A pruning change
can only remove work, and the problem is not that too much work is being done
— it is that the emission budget stops before this clue in a product of
23962-better candidates. There is nothing to prune.

### 3. Why the answer is a budget, and that budget is `6b2f04`'s

The measurement localises the blocker exactly: the canonical clue is
**retained, reachable, and affordable** (running cost 1.1195 <= `total_budget`
1.5), and is lost **solely** to how many combinations the per-segmentation
enumeration is allowed to emit before moving on. That is a global
emission/pop budget, a per-segmentation share, and a structure-aware
allocation question — mechanisms 1 and 2 of [w-6b2f04](w-6b2f04.md), which owns
them. Consistent with the scope fence, this front does not implement them, and
per the fence's own instruction this is recorded here as a finding.

The useful contribution of the refutation is that it **sharpens `6b2f04`'s
target**: the number that matters is not `LEXICAL_BRANCH_KEEP`, not the
shortlist, and not the segmentation retention. It is that a uniform
`LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION = 64` per segmentation is a ~64-deep
window onto a product whose relevant member sits 23962 deep. Any fix must make
the *depth* of the enumeration adaptive to something — a structure's
under-representation, a global budget divided across segmentations — rather
than widening any single list. Mechanism 2 (structure-aware allocation) is
now measurably the more promising of `6b2f04`'s two: the canonical clue does
not need more *breadth* in any slot, it needs to be enumerated at *all*.
