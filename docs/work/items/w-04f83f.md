# Make the canonical Mad Gab alignment competitive under the clue score

## Goal

`madgab --approximate` should be able to propose
`hits justice dupe hid came` for `It's just a stupid game` because the
clue score ranks that kind of alignment competitively, not because a
retention rule was loosened until it appeared.

## Context

The last remaining blocker of the itinerary's primary milestone, after
[w-3c8e10](w-3c8e10.md) delivered the first canonical case. The decisive
measurement is in [w-6d1f45](w-6d1f45.md): the alignment is reachable
only at an unbounded search cost, and under the real final scorer it
scores **0.819901** against a visible top-10 cutoff of **0.929514** for
that target. It is 0.11 below the cut, so no amount of reachability work
puts it in the output; the lever is the objective.

Why it loses, from the same measurement: its edit cost of 1.12 eats a
quarter of the similarity axis' weight (`0.25 * (1 - cost / 4)`), and its
boundary novelty is 2/6. Every other axis it wins or ties. Meanwhile the
visible list for that target is still determiner salad
(`it justice too bad aim`, `eat justice too pad aim`, ...), which is
exactly the population the closed-class axis
([w-9c2d51](w-9c2d51.md), `CLOSED_CLASS_WEIGHT = 0.15`) was meant to
suppress and demonstrably has not.

The general question, stated without reference to any phrase: **does the
clue score rank a well-aligned multi-syllable English phrase above a
function-word salad that merely matches the phones?** A real Mad Gab
answer is a phrase a person can say and a solver can recognise. If the
objective cannot tell those apart, the objective is wrong, and the fix
belongs in a general axis, a normalisation, or the way axes are combined
— not in a retention constant.

## Completion criteria

- The reason the current score under-rates well-aligned, sayable clues is
  identified by decomposing the score per axis, for a spread of real
  targets and not only the two acceptance phrases.
- The fix is general: an axis, a normalisation, or a combination rule
  that helps other inputs. **No phrase-specific hard-coding**, no special
  case for any word, substring or word sequence of the acceptance
  examples — including inside tests, except as the regression assertion
  itself.
- A regression test at the library or executable boundary expresses the
  property ("a sayable, well-aligned phrase outranks a function-word
  salad at comparable alignment"), and a measurement over a spread of
  targets shows the property holds generally rather than on one input.
- `cargo test --release --lib`, `--test corpus_integration` and
  `--test exact_determinism` are green apart from the known acceptance
  regression this item is trying to close, which must then pass.
- Wall clock does not regress against the current figures
  (`--approximate --top 20`: 2.0 s search for `It's just a stupid game`,
  419 ms corpus load).
- Work is on a focused branch, integrated into `post-milestone-acceptance`
  by a coordinator, never into `main`.
