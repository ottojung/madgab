# Make the canonical Mad Gab alignment competitive under the clue score

## Goal

`madgab --approximate` should be able to propose
`hits justice dupe hid came` for `It's just a stupid game` because the
clue score ranks that kind of alignment competitively, not because a
retention rule was loosened until it appeared.

## Context

The last remaining blocker of the itinerary's primary milestone, after
[w-3c8e10](w-3c8e10.md) delivered the first canonical case. The decisive
measurement is in [w-6d1f45](w-6d1f45.md): the alignment is reachable
only at an unbounded search cost, and under the real final scorer it
scores **0.819901** against a visible top-10 cutoff of **0.929514** for
that target. It is 0.11 below the cut, so no amount of reachability work
puts it in the output; the lever is the objective.

Why it loses, from the same measurement: its edit cost of 1.12 eats a
quarter of the similarity axis' weight (`0.25 * (1 - cost / 4)`), and its
boundary novelty is 2/6. Every other axis it wins or ties. Meanwhile the
visible list for that target is still determiner salad
(`it justice too bad aim`, `eat justice too pad aim`, ...), which is
exactly the population the closed-class axis
([w-9c2d51](w-9c2d51.md), `CLOSED_CLASS_WEIGHT = 0.15`) was meant to
suppress and demonstrably has not.

The general question, stated without reference to any phrase: **does the
clue score rank a well-aligned multi-syllable English phrase above a
function-word salad that merely matches the phones?** A real Mad Gab
answer is a phrase a person can say and a solver can recognise. If the
objective cannot tell those apart, the objective is wrong, and the fix
belongs in a general axis, a normalisation, or the way axes are combined
— not in a retention constant.

## Completion criteria

- The reason the current score under-rates well-aligned, sayable clues is
  identified by decomposing the score per axis, for a spread of real
  targets and not only the two acceptance phrases.
- The fix is general: an axis, a normalisation, or a combination rule
  that helps other inputs. **No phrase-specific hard-coding**, no special
  case for any word, substring or word sequence of the acceptance
  examples — including inside tests, except as the regression assertion
  itself.
- A regression test at the library or executable boundary expresses the
  property ("a sayable, well-aligned phrase outranks a function-word
  salad at comparable alignment"), and a measurement over a spread of
  targets shows the property holds generally rather than on one input.
- `cargo test --release --lib`, `--test corpus_integration` and
  `--test exact_determinism` are green apart from the known acceptance
  regression this item is trying to close, which must then pass.
- Wall clock does not regress against the current figures
  (`--approximate --top 20`: 2.0 s search for `It's just a stupid game`,
  419 ms corpus load).
- Work is on a focused branch, integrated into `post-milestone-acceptance`
  by a coordinator, never into `main`.

### What I tried on the objective, and why I reverted it

I built a general `ANSWER_QUALITY` axis to replace the three miscalibrated
lexical terms (`FAMILIARITY` 0.10, `SHAPE` 0.05, `CLOSED_CLASS` 0.15):
a monotone answer-word length factor times the existing convex
closed-share discount, one weight, weights still summing to 1. It is
reverted, and here is the measurement that killed it, because it is the
useful part.

The tension is structural, not a tuning problem. Take
`wreck a nice beach` (cost 0.55, one function word in four) and
`wreck egg nice peach` (cost 0.40, none). Same resegmentation, same
syllables, same novelty, edit cost within 0.15. They differ only in one
function word against one content word. Every setting I tried that
satisfies `approximate_proposals_are_predominantly_content_words` demotes
`wreck a nice beach` out of the visible list, and every setting that keeps
it fails that test:

```text
CLOSED_CLASS_QUALITY  len(3 letters)  wreck a nice beach   content-share test
0.5                   0.85             rank 5   (in)        pass
0.25                  0.85             rank 92  (out)       pass
0.0                   0.85             rank 246 (out)       pass
0.5                   1.0              rank 165 (out)       (fails)
```

**A bag-of-words score has no way to know that `a nice` is idiomatic and
`eggs eyes` is not.** So the closed-class axis is not a lever that can be
turned up: it is weak *and* unfixable by strengthening, and the item's
framing — the salad survives because that axis is too weak — is only half
right. Turning the objective up also perturbs the whole pool, which is how
`wreck a nice beach` fell out, and it moved the needed words' shortlist
ranks the wrong way (`hid` 85 → 129 with a 3-letter penalty; → 96 once
that was dropped).

I kept exactly the part of the investigation that survives: the finding
above, recorded here rather than in a comment, because it is what stops the
next pass repeating the sweep.

### What I did land: the diversity policy was reading a corrupted structure

One real, general defect, found while decomposing, fixed in **adfc7c4**.

`select_diverse` is supposed to cap how much of the visible list one
resegmentation may hold and to represent the strong structures first. Both
were no-ops. A clue's boundary structure was re-derived by summing its
words' own IPA lengths, which equals the search's alignment only when every
alignment is phonetically exact. Under an approximate alignment a clue
word consumes a run of the *target's* phonemes, so the accumulated offsets
drift and every wording of one resegmentation counted as its own structure.
With no structure ever repeated, the share cap could not bind.

Measured on `--top 300` at adaa135: **all 300 candidates were wordings of
a single resegmentation** (`it`/`justice`/`too`/`*`/`*`), and the cap let
that one structure keep 50 of 50 visible slots.

`Clue` now carries the offsets the search aligned it at, as `cuts`, and both
the policy and the integration test read them. No score, weight or
retention constant moves; the candidate pool is unchanged, and the visible
list differs only because the slots a saturated structure gives up now go
to the best candidates of other resegutations. `Clue.cuts` is a new public
field, so the wasm JSON gains a key (additive).
