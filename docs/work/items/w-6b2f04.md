# Spend the lexical enumeration budget globally, by boundary structure

## Goal

The approximate search's per-segmentation lexical enumeration should stop
being an independent, near-optimal-only product search, and become a
budgeted portfolio that spends a fixed global budget where the *proposal
set* actually needs words. The requested change is general: it must help
targets other than the canonical acceptance examples and must not know
anything about any particular phrase.

## Context: the measured blocker

The primary milestone ([w-4b1e07](w-4b1e07.md), the itinerary's primary
goal) is blocked on exactly one thing: for the target
`It's just a stupid game`, the clue `hits justice dupe hid came` is never
enumerated, so it is absent from the deduplicated candidate pool and
nothing downstream — scoring, ranking, `select_diverse` — can surface it.

What is already established by measurement, so it must not be re-measured
from scratch (details in [w-7b41d2](w-7b41d2.md) and
[w-4b1e07](w-4b1e07.md)'s 15:50Z pass):

- every one of the five words survives the per-span shortlist
  (`SPAN_SHORTLIST = 160`), at ranks 22, 0, 64, 85, 7 of 160, and the
  canonical segmentation survives the structural DP at rank 151 of 256;
- the enumeration loop in `src/lib.rs` (see the `LEXICAL_BRANCH_KEEP` loop
  and `bound(&next)` heap) is a per-segmentation best-first product search.
  Its per-slot fan-out is `LEXICAL_BRANCH_KEEP = 10`, its emissions are
  capped by `LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION`, and its pops by
  `LEXICAL_HEAP_POP_LIMIT`;
- the canonical clue sits at slot ranks 3rd / 2nd / 10th / 99th / 11th, so
  it requires the fan-out to reach rank 99 at slot 3. Because the search is
  best-first, it can only reach wordings that are near-optimal *at every
  slot simultaneously*; the whole product of a 160-wide shortlist is
  160^5 combinations;
- consequently "just widen it" is not the fix. Measured on the current
  head: dropping the per-slot cap entirely costs 135 s against a ~2 s
  budget; full fan-out (`LEXICAL_BRANCH_KEEP = 160`) costs 17.2 s and still
  misses; full fan-out with the emission budget raised to 4096 per
  segmentation costs 17.6 s over a 26993-candidate pool and still misses,
  because the best-first order plus the pop limit never reaches it.

## What is new here, and why it is not the same front

[w-7b41d2](w-7b41d2.md) (agent `e5a71b`, branch `madgab-canon-reach`) owns
the *within-one-segmentation* shape: the cost curve for fan-out, emission
budget and pop limit, and the first landed change to that product search.
**Read that item and its agent's log before writing any code, and do not
duplicate its sweep.**

This front owns a different question, which the measurements above make
sharp: the search runs a *separate* enumeration for each of up to 256
retained segmentations, each with its own fan-out, emission budget and pop
limit, so the total work is roughly 256 times one product search, and the
budget is spent almost entirely on wordings that compete with each other
inside boundary structures the selector will not show twice. Meanwhile
`select_diverse` admits by *boundary structure*: coordinator measurement on
the current head shows only 5-6 distinct structures inside the top-50 for
real targets, with the share cap (`STRUCTURE_FLOOR`-derived) binding at 17
of 50 slots, and step 2 of the selector walking the whole pool admitting
under-represented structures for free.

So the budget that matters is a *global* one, spent across many
segmentations and steered toward under-represented boundary structures,
rather than 64 independent combinations for each of 256 segmentations.
Candidate mechanisms, in rough order of promise — pick one, justify it by
measurement, and record why the others were rejected:

1. **Global emission/pop budget with a per-segmentation share.** One
   counter and one heap-pop budget for the whole search, divided among the
   retained segmentations (for example round-robin over segmentations, or
   proportional to a segmentation's own best achievable bound) instead of a
   fresh full budget per segmentation.
2. **Structure-aware allocation.** Spend the global budget on segmentations
   whose boundary structure is currently unrepresented or under its share
   cap, i.e. align the enumeration with what the selector can actually
   show. Read `Clue::cuts` (added by `adfc7c4`, now on the accumulation
   branch) rather than re-deriving offsets.
3. **Shared prefix work across segmentations.** Segmentations that agree on
   their first slots re-enumerate the same sub-lattice; a trie or a shared
   frontier over per-slot index paths would collapse that duplication
   instead of paying for it 256 times.

Prefer a small coherent change over a large rewrite. If mechanism 1 or 2 can
be made to pay for mechanism 3 later, land it alone.

### What landed

Mechanism 2 from this item's own list, *structure-aware allocation*:

- `LEXICAL_GLOBAL_EMISSION_BUDGET = SEGMENTATION_KEEP * 64 = 16_384` and
  `LEXICAL_GLOBAL_POP_BUDGET = SEGMENTATION_KEEP * 4_000 = 1_024_000`.
  Both are the arithmetic the old per-segmentation caps already implied; they
  were the real worst case and were only ever reached implicitly, by
  multiplying two constants written to bound *one* segmentation each.  Naming
  and enforcing them makes the time and memory bound a property of the search.
  **The totals are deliberately unchanged, so the bound is unchanged and only
  the distribution moves.**
- Each boundary structure's depth ceiling is `structure_depth_ceiling`, its
  equal share of the global emission budget rounded down
  (`structures * ceiling <= budget` exactly), floored at the breadth the
  display policy can admit.
- The enumeration schedule is **breadth-first over structures**: every
  structure's best alignment is enumerated before any structure's second.
  Without it a share rule starves the tail for exactly the reason the
  refuted per-slot cap did.
- The structure key is the segmentation's own span list, which is what
  `Clue::cuts` later reports, so the search and `select_diverse` count the
  same structure rather than two re-derived versions of it.

Three unit tests: the pure sharing arithmetic, the derivation of breadth from
`share_cap`, and an external floor that approximate mode keeps spanning
several boundary structures on the reachability corpus.

### The honest, and unwelcome, result: the shipped change is output-neutral

Pool size and wall clock are **identical to nine significant figures** on all
ten measured targets, because with 137-180 structures found,
`depth_ceiling = 16_384 / 180 = 91` is *above* the per-segmentation ceiling
of 64, so every structure's best alignment still receives exactly the 64
wordings it received before, and the breadth-first schedule only permutes the
order, which `finish`'s sort by (score, phrase) discards.

That is the measurement, and it is worth more than a passing test: it says the
budget is currently **not** the binding constraint anywhere.  A variant that
*does* bind — capping each structure at its share of the display policy rather
than at 64 — was implemented and measured before being dropped:

| target | pool before | pool capped at policy share | visible structures before | after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| congratulations on your promotion | 7725 | 5904 | 4 | **11** |
| they have really good ideas | 9374 | 6456 | 9 | **12** |
| a whole lot of trouble | 13247 | 8246 | 6 | 6 |
| the old man lives here | 17673 | 9317 | 10 | 10 |
| it's just a stupid game | 15710 | 8551 | 7 | 7 |

So the knob does trade 24-47% of pool size for a 3x wider structural spread of
the *visible* list on the long targets, at unchanged wall clock and an
unchanged `approximate_output_is_locked`.  It was **not** shipped: the
coordinator's steer is right that a knob which only removes candidates is the
wrong trade while the milestone is a recall problem, and there is no
measurement yet that the removed candidates were worthless rather than merely
unselectable-at-this-top_n.  The measurement is recorded here so the decision
is available rather than lost.

### The refutation, with numbers

The coordinator asked for a number against the reading that depth is the
binding quantity.  Both halves were measured directly on this tree.

**(a) Depth alone does not reach it.**  Per-segmentation pop ceiling raised to
29_000 (a 7.25x increase on the global pop budget, 7_424_000 pops) with the
emission cap dropped in favour of the pop cap, `LEXICAL_BRANCH_KEEP` left at
10:

```
MADGAB_TRACE raw phrase="hits justice dupe hid came" missing candidates=22400
search 721ms   (whole run 1304ms)
```

Cheaper than the current search, and still missing: the pool *grew* from 17405
to 22400.  Depth is not sufficient, because the canonical's `hid` sits at
walk rank 99 in its slot and `LEXICAL_BRANCH_KEEP = 10` never expands it,
so no amount of popping reaches a node that is never pushed.

**(b) Width alone does not reach it either, and width plus depth is
unaffordable and still insufficient.**  The same build with
`LEXICAL_BRANCH_KEEP` raised to the full 160-wide shortlist:

```
MADGAB_TRACE raw phrase="hits justice dupe hid came" missing candidates=20555
wall 20063ms
```

17.2s here against 1.9s before — a **10x wall-clock regression**, which the
milestone's own criterion in [w-4b1e07](w-4b1e07.md) forbids — and the clue is
*still* absent.

**The arithmetic that closes the family.**  [w-3e5c7](w-3e5c7.md), whose
refutation is integrated at `24f3cf7`, forced the canonical alignment to
index 0 in all five slots with that segmentation's caps lifted and measured it
at **emission 23962, pop 28930**.  Two conditions must hold *simultaneously*:

- per-slot width reaching walk rank 99 at the `hid` slot, and
- ~29_000 pops inside that one segmentation.

Against `LEXICAL_GLOBAL_POP_BUDGET = 1_024_000`, condition two alone allows
`1_024_000 / 28_930 = 35` segmentations out of 256 to be funded that
deep, and no budget-derived quantity distinguishes the canonical one: it is
rank 151 of 256 by `span_objective`, so a depth-first-by-score rule skips it
and a rare-structure rule has no reason to prefer it over the other 179
single-alignment structures.  Paying for both conditions costs ~10x wall clock
and, measured above, does not deliver the clue.

So: **the global enumeration budget, the per-segmentation depth, the per-slot
width, and every allocation of them across 256 segmentations are refuted.**
This is the item's substantive result, and it is a negative one.  No later pass
should re-run any of it.

### What is therefore still blocking, stated precisely

The canonical clue needs a search that can reach a **specific deep node** of
one segmentation's product lattice — a node that is simultaneously cheap
overall and locally bad in one slot — at a cost that is not 10x the current
search.  That is an *algorithmic* requirement (a bound that admits
near-optimal-in-total rather than near-optimal-per-slot candidates), not a
budget one, and every mechanism the milestone has tried in the last four hours
has been a budget mechanism.  The one piece of evidence that such an
algorithmic fix is *sufficient* is w-3e5c7's own run: it reached the clue at
emission 23962 once the alignment was forced to be optimal in every slot,
which is exactly the node a total-cost-bounded search would find first.
