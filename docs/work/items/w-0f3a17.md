# The canonical clue is outside the enumerated lattice, not late in its order

## Goal

Make the per-segmentation lexical traversal **enumerate** wordings that are
deep in a single slot, so that `Hits Justice Dupe Hid Came` can be reached
for `It's just a stupid game` at all. General mechanism, general benefit; no
phrase-specific case, no hard-coding of either canonical sentence, clue or
word.

## Why this item exists

This is the successor front named by the priced negative in
[w-5b1e93](w-5b1e93.md) § "Pass 2026-09-27T08:00Z". Nine passes of steering
on emission **order** ended with arithmetic: over 14 real targets, the number
of wordings of a segmentation standing in front of a deep-in-one-slot wording
is 14,411 to 934,472 under the exact score — 323x to 14,601x the
per-segmentation allowance of 64 and 1.3x to 57x the whole 16,384 emission
budget. Reordering within the enumerated product cannot fix that, and a
jointly admissible Pareto bound makes it *worse* on 6 of the 14.

Two measured facts from the same front say the real gap is upstream of order:

- **`LEXICAL_BRANCH_STAGE_0` is not the production opening width.**
  `affordable_opening_width` returns **7** at depth 5.
- Therefore the canonical alignment's per-slot indices — **3 / 2 / 10 / 99 /
  11** against the 160-wide shortlist — have their 10, 99 and 11 **never
  pushed**. The clue is not late in the enumeration; it is **outside the
  enumerated lattice**.

So the question this item opens is: *which* candidates enter the lattice at
all — the per-slot shortlist width, `affordable_opening_width`'s derivation,
and the tuples the coverage reserve spends its budget on — not the order in
which the traversal emits them.

1. **At every depth ≥ 3 the deepest index the traversal pushes is exactly

So the item's `3 / 2 / 10 / 99 / 11` is the same tuple read against a different

indices 0-6, so 3 of the 5 coordinates are in the lattice and 2 are not, and
the reserve cannot supply the missing pair either: its shape classes keep
index 0 in every slot outside the deep subset, so with
`EMIT_PROFILE_MAX_DEEP = 3` the best it can ever emit is *three* non-zero
coordinates. **The clue is outside the reserve's lattice too, at any width** —
so this is not a width defect at all.

## 3. The per-slot derivation, done exactly, and what it buys

`affordable_opening_width` returns the largest `w` with
`1 + w + ... + w^(d-1) <= pop_limit`, which is the count of *internal* nodes
a walk expands to reach its first leaf when **every** slot is `w` wide at
once. The item's step 2 asks for the same series evaluated with one slot
widened and the rest held at the baseline, so a slot pays for its own depth
instead of the worst case. Held to the same 4,000-pop budget:

| depth | baseline | per-slot derived width (160-wide lists) | internal nodes at that width |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 7 | **9 / 7 / 10 / 10 / 93** | 3,601 / 2,801 / 3,977 / 3,830 / 2,801 |
| 6 | 5 | **5 / 5 / 5 / 5 / 5 / 93** | 3,906 each, 2,801 for the last |

Two general facts, both now asserted in
`tests::the_opening_width_is_charged_the_all_slots_deep_worst_case`:

* a slot's depth is charged against the product of the slots **before** it,
  so the affordable width of a slot is non-decreasing in its position; and
* the **last** slot's depth is charged against *nothing*, because it is only
  read once the walk is at the last internal level — so the last slot can
  always be opened to its whole list width at the same internal-node cost as
  one alternative.

And the price of that, at `d = 5`: the derived widths are 9, 7, 10, 10, 93
against the shipped uniform 7. The clue needs 22 and 99 in slots 2 and 3. So
the per-slot derivation is **2-3 indices wider in three slots and still leaves
both deep coordinates unpushed**. It is a constant replacing a constant, which
criterion 2 explicitly refuses to count, and this pass declines to claim it.

The general reason is point 3 above: the width is derived from the *pop*
budget, and the traversal's binding constraint is its *emission* allowance.
The walk is best-first, so it spends its 64 wordings on the cost-best corner
and stops; a deep-in-one-slot tuple's bound is below all of them, so no width
at all brings it into the walk. Widening a slot cannot help, which is why the
widening ladder fires zero times at `d ≥ 3` on all 15 targets.

## 4. What is therefore still open, and where

The gate is `LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION` = 64 wordings per
segmentation, spent in descending-bound order, with
`LEXICAL_GLOBAL_EMISSION_BUDGET` saturated at 16,384/16,384 and
`LEXICAL_GLOBAL_POP_BUDGET` at 5-44%. Any mechanism that *places* a
deep-in-one-slot tuple into the lattice has to spend **emissions** on it, and
the only spend in the tree that does is the coverage reserve (16/segmentation,
corner-only shapes, at most 3 non-zero coordinates) and the adjacency operator
(8/segmentation, one slot at a time, retaining the best
`ADJACENCY_PER_SLOT` = 2 children per slot — so a rank-99 index is admitted
only if it is top-2 by bound in its own slot, which measurement says it is
not). This item's front is the width; the width is not the gate. The successor
front is **the coverage reserve's shape rule**, and it is a different question
from the one this item opened.
