# The canonical clue is outside the enumerated lattice, not late in its order

## Goal

Make the per-segmentation lexical traversal **enumerate** wordings that are
deep in a single slot, so that `Hits Justice Dupe Hid Came` can be reached
for `It's just a stupid game` at all. General mechanism, general benefit; no
phrase-specific case, no hard-coding of either canonical sentence, clue or
word.

## Why this item exists

This is the successor front named by the priced negative in
[w-5b1e93](w-5b1e93.md) § "Pass 2026-09-27T08:00Z". Nine passes of steering
on emission **order** ended with arithmetic: over 14 real targets, the number
of wordings of a segmentation standing in front of a deep-in-one-slot wording
is 14,411 to 934,472 under the exact score — 323x to 14,601x the
per-segmentation allowance of 64 and 1.3x to 57x the whole 16,384 emission
budget. Reordering within the enumerated product cannot fix that, and a
jointly admissible Pareto bound makes it *worse* on 6 of the 14.

Two measured facts from the same front say the real gap is upstream of order:

- **`LEXICAL_BRANCH_STAGE_0` is not the production opening width.**
  `affordable_opening_width` returns **7** at depth 5.
- Therefore the canonical alignment's per-slot indices — **3 / 2 / 10 / 99 /
  11** against the 160-wide shortlist — have their 10, 99 and 11 **never
  pushed**. The clue is not late in the enumeration; it is **outside the
  enumerated lattice**.

So the question this item opens is: *which* candidates enter the lattice at
all — the per-slot shortlist width, `affordable_opening_width`'s derivation,
and the tuples the coverage reserve spends its budget on — not the order in
which the traversal emits them.

### The latent defect, priced

The one provable weakness, which this measurement prices but does not
trigger: the fill loop truncates on a single score, and `quality` degrades
monotonically with phonetic distance, so the last slots of a full shortlist
are systematically the worst-sounding retained words. The band structure that
exists to prevent exactly this — four cost bands x (quality, familiarity,
rarity) x 4 — is applied only to the first
`16 + 16 + 4 + 48 + 16 = 100` slots, so **at least 60 of every full 160-wide
shortlist (37.5%) is an undifferentiated quality top-up carrying no band
guarantee.** Measured over **939 span edges**: candidates within 1.5x of their
span's cheapest — the same threshold the acceptance reachability test uses —
come to **7,056**, of which **623 (8.8%)** are dropped at every width, worst
dropped quality rank 255, and worst per-span share **24 of 56 (43%)** on the
ɛ-initial one-phoneme spans (`[8,9)` of `in the middle of the night`,
`[4,5)` of `a whole lot of trouble`, `[11,12)` of `we should have told her`).

The general fix is a **cost-stratified fill**: give the fill's 60 slots to
the four cost bands in proportion to band population and fill within a band
by `quality`. It keeps `SPAN_SHORTLIST = 160` — it changes *which* 160, not
how many — adds no scoring work, leaves the structural DP's branching and
every `SpanExtremes` extremum computed over the same 160 candidates, and can
only make the DP's `min_cost` bound tighter and still valid. It is checkable
at an API boundary: every non-empty cost band of a span contributes at least
one word to the shortlist, asserted over real targets with no phrase in the
assertion. **Not implemented here**: it is not what blocks the canonical
clue, it moves a production selection rule that two pool-reach guards were
re-pinned against, and the handoff above says not to contend on `src/lib.rs`
until the w-6d2af3 reviewer reports.

### The per-slot index, and the priced negative at depth 1

Added at the request of coordination pass coord-0c1d. First, the hazard:
**there are four per-slot orders, not one**, and they disagree by up to 143
positions. (1) admission order, which nothing reads; (2) shortlist order,
`sort_by(cmp_desc(quality))`, the order the truncation and the structural DP
see; (3) **traversal order**, `slots[k]` = `edge.matches` re-sorted by
`SlotAlt::contribution(word_count)` — familiarity, shape and closed class
divided by the slot count, `WORD_NOVELTY / word_count`, ties by word string —
and this is the one the walk indexes, `0..slots[k].len().min(cap)`; (4) the
six per-pass orders. `cap` bounds (3), not (2). Any later pass quoting "the
per-slot index" without naming the order is quoting an ill-defined number,
and `3 / 2 / 10 / 99 / 11` matches none of the four.

**Case 1, `recognize speech` -> `wreck a nice beach`** (depth 4, `cap = 10`):
traversal indices **1 / 0 / 0 / 2**, all four pushed, depth-1 `wreck` at
index 1 with shortlist index 32 and cost rank 0. Case 1 needs nothing.

**Case 2, `It's just a stupid game` -> `hits justice dupe hid came`**
(depth 5, `cap = 7`): traversal indices **7 / 0 / 13 / 99 / 11** against
shortlist indices 22 / 0 / 64 / 85 / 7.

**Priced negative, depth 1, with arithmetic.** The depth-1 needed tuple is
`hits`. Its rank under each of the six admission passes' own orderings is
**7** (cost), 72 (familiarity), 72 (rarity), 22 (quality); the cost pass is
the best of the six, so the **floor over all six is 7**. The walk reads
`0..min(len, cap)`, i.e. the test is `index < cap` = `7 < 7` = false. So
**no change to any of the six admission passes can admit `hits` at depth 1
within the production opening width; it misses by exactly one index.** The
same arithmetic holds for the whole clue — floors 7 / 0 / 3 / 85 / 7 against
`cap = 7` — so **not one of the five canonical tuples** can be brought
inside the opening width by re-specifying the admission rule. What admits
them is a width: `cap = 8` reaches `hits` (7), `12` also reaches `came`
(11), `14` also `dupe` (13), and **`100` is the first width at which the
canonical wording is fully enumerable** (`hid` at 99), against the derived
opening width of 7. **The width the clue needs is ~14x the width the budget
derives, and the shortlist is not what stands in the way.**

**One correction the sibling front needs.** Because `contribution` divides
the familiarity / shape / closed-class terms by the slot count, the traversal
order depends on the segmentation's depth: a wider slot is not further down
the same list, it is a differently-ordered list (measured disagreements:
`it` 143 -> 0, `wreck` 32 -> 1, `came` 7 -> 11, `hid` 85 -> 99). A widening
ladder argued in "shortlist index" units is arguing in the wrong units, and a
width derivation must be stated and re-derived in traversal-index units per
depth.
