# The selector's leftover slots are spent on depth, not on unrepresented
# boundary structures: close the fill-rank gate

## Goal

Make `select_diverse` admit the best candidate of an **unrepresented**
boundary structure *before* it spends a slot on a second or third member of a
structure that already has one, so the 50 slots it hands out are decided by
structural coverage rather than by the top of the score order. This is the
general property behind the last remaining blocker on the acceptance target
and it is stated without reference to any phrase.

## Why this is the gate, by measurement already taken

All of this was measured on accumulation head `8cc71c4` by agent `7b2d40` and
is recorded in [w-7b2d40](w-7b2d40.md) and [w-3f9c02](w-3f9c02.md) §"Why
selection cannot reach it either":

* for `It's just a stupid game` the pool is 18,065 candidates over 333
  distinct structures, but the visible list shows only **6** structures and
  the 50 slots are exhausted at global rank **101**;
* below-cutoff-but-visible candidates do exist (27 of them, at global ranks
  54-101), so being 0.0958 below the visible cutoff is **not** what hides a
  candidate;
* when the canonical alignment is injected at a score that gives it
  within-structure rank **2** under a cap of 17 — comfortably under the cap —
  it is **still invisible** at global rank 2,968;
* on all six of that item's targets there are **0** under-cap candidates
  invisible at or before the fill rank, and **4,609-4,869** invisible beyond
  it.

The last two lines are the finding. A candidate under its structure's cap that
sits deep in the pool is invisible, and the quantity that decides it is the
**fill rank**, not the cap, not the cutoff and not the score.

## The mechanism, in the code as it stands

`select_diverse` (`src/lib.rs:3021`) has three steps:

1. one representative per structure inside the score cutoff (fills only 5-6
   slots, because a saturated cutoff holds only 5-6 structures);
2. `for &i in &order { if picked.len() == top_n { break } ... }` — a single
   walk in **descending score order** over the whole pool, admitting anything
   under its structure's `share_cap`;
3. a cap-dropping fallback.

Step 2's own comment states the intent: *"the slots a structure gives up are
taken by the best candidate of an under-filled structure, which is exactly the
trade the policy is meant to make."* The implementation cannot make that trade
once the list is full. The walk is greedy in score, so the first six
structures to appear take every slot they are entitled to, `picked.len()`
reaches `top_n` at rank ~101, the loop breaks, and the ~4,600 under-cap
candidates that follow — including every candidate of a structure the list has
not seen yet — are never examined. The policy's structural half is therefore
decided by the top of the score order, which is what it exists to prevent.

The general property to fix: **coverage of structures must be bought before
depth within a structure.** Concretely, admit one representative per
structure over the whole pool (bounded by `top_n`, and by whatever walk budget
is needed to stay inside the existing wall clock) and only then spend
remaining slots on further members of structures that already have one. This
is a change of admission *order* inside one function, not a change of weights
and not a change of caps.

and admits the best member of every boundary structure until `top_n`

untouched, as are `STRUCTURE_FLOOR`, `share_cap` and every `axes::*` constant.

representatives or at the end of the pool, so it is one pass over a list step 2
was already walking. Measured walk depth: it reaches global rank 490-1812 of a
15,712-18,065 pool, i.e. 3-10% of the pool, and spends **50 of 50** slots on
coverage and 0 on depth on every one of the six targets, because every pool
holds 306-352 structures.
