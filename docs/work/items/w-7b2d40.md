# Establish what actually bounds a proposal's visibility, and fix that thing

## Goal

For the second canonical example, decide with measurement whether the binding
constraint on a proposal becoming *visible* is its **score** or its
**rank within its own boundary structure**, then fix that constraint in a way
that is general and helps other inputs.

Stated without reference to any phrase: `select_diverse` composes a global
score order with a per-structure share cap. A candidate can be far below the
global visible cutoff and still be admitted, because step 2 walks the **whole
pool**, not the cutoff. So "the candidate's score is 0.0958 below the visible
cutoff" does **not** by itself mean it can never be shown. What decides it is
how many better-scoring candidates share its boundary structure, i.e. its
**within-structure rank**, versus the cap
`cap = share_cap(top_n, available.len())` where `available` is the number of
distinct structures in the whole pool (`src/lib.rs:2919-2931`).

That is the question this item exists to settle by measurement, because two
records in this repository currently disagree about it (see "The conflict").

## The conflict, which is why this item exists

* [w-c1d3a7](w-c1d3a7.md) §8.3, from the `413113a` close-out, says of the
  canonical clue injected at its real score 0.819901291, raw rank 8,967 of
  17,553: "It is cut, by 0.0958", and concludes "`select_diverse` step 1 is
  bounded by the visible cutoff, and the clue is 0.0958 under it." Its
  recommended lever 3 is "the visible cutoff itself".
* [w-2e5b93](w-2e5b93.md) status note says the opposite: "the visible list is
  not decided by the score cutoff — `select_diverse` step 2 walks the whole
  pool and admits by boundary structure under a binding share cap (17 of 50
  slots for three real targets)".

Reading the code on this head settles the mechanism but not the numbers: step 2
is `for &i in &order` over the whole pool, gated only by
`counts[structure] < cap`. So a below-cutoff candidate **is** reachable, and
the binding quantity is the candidate's rank *inside its own structure*
relative to `cap`. Both prior claims are therefore incomplete: the first treats
step 1's bound as if it governed the whole list, the second does not name the
within-structure rank that actually does.

Coordinator note: this is a claim to be checked, not adopted. If measurement
shows the within-structure rank is already inside the cap for a real
population, this item closes as a refutation and says so with numbers.

### Criterion 2 — the second canonical clue, injected at its real alignment and score

`It's just a stupid game` → `hits justice dupe hid came`, injected with
`cuts [3, 10, 13, 15, 19]` (the alignment recorded in
[w-7b41d2](w-7b41d2.md)) and score `0.8199012907736722` (the real final score
recorded in [w-4b1e07](w-4b1e07.md) and [w-3e5c7](w-3e5c7.md)). Reported as
three separate numbers, because conflating them has cost this repository a
pass before:

```text
ENUMERATED = yes   (by injection; the search does not reach it on its own)
RANKED     = 8683 / 17231      gap to cutoff = -0.095790597
structure  = [3, 10, 13, 15]   49 members in the pool
within-structure rank = 42      41 same-structure siblings score higher
cap        = 17
VISIBLE    = no
```

**Which constraint it fails: neither of the two this item offered.** The
within-structure rank (42) does exceed the cap (17), and the score is below the
cutoff — but both of those are *inner* gates, and the measurement shows neither
is what stops it.

The decisive experiment: inject the same alignment at a score that puts it at
**within-structure rank 2, well under the cap of 17**:

```text
injected at 0.8802260016836312, cuts [3,10,13,15,19]:
  RANKED = 2294 / 17232   within-structure rank = 2   cap = 17   VISIBLE = no
```

Still invisible. So the cap is not the binding constraint, and neither is the
score-versus-cutoff comparison.

**The binding constraint is reachability: the global rank at which the list
fills.** `select_diverse` step 2 breaks the moment `picked.len() == top_n`, and
on these targets that happens at global rank 49-93 — a small multiple of
`top_n`, not anywhere near the 17,231-entry pool. Measured directly: on all six
targets there are **0** under-cap candidates (within-structure rank < cap) that
are invisible at or before the fill rank, and **4,598-4,869** that are invisible
beyond it. A candidate past the fill rank is invisible *whatever* its
within-structure rank, because no slot remains to compete for.

That reconciles the two prior records and corrects both:

* [w-c1d3a7](w-c1d3a7.md) §8.3's "cut, by 0.0958" is **not** the mechanism. On
  this very target **27** candidates below the cutoff are visible, at ranks
  54-93, with within-structure ranks 1 through 17. Being below the cutoff does
  not prevent visibility.
* [w-2e5b93](w-2e5b93.md)'s "not decided by the cutoff, admits by structure
  under a binding share cap" is **directionally right and still incomplete**:
  the cap binds, and it is saturated, but it decides who wins the slots that
  exist. It cannot create a slot.

### Method notes worth keeping

* The scratch harness reports **ENUMERATED / RANKED / VISIBLE** as three
  separate numbers, per the coordinator steer.
* One self-inflicted error is recorded because it nearly produced a wrong
  refutation: the trace's first field is the **pool index**, not the rank.
  Reading it as a rank made eight same-structure siblings appear to outrank a
  candidate they actually score below, and produced a within-structure rank of
  50 where the truth is 42. The harness now asserts the pool arrives in
  descending score order and derives rank from emission position.
* Scores are traced at `{:.17}` so that a candidate's own score cannot alias a
  neighbour's under rounding; at `{:.9}` the injected candidate compared as
  greater than itself.
* Build provenance: `cargo clean` then a full rebuild on the rebased scratch
  head, binary sha1 `3841af06440fd529722cb216d14821e9ebb2ebc6`, to rule out a
  failed build silently reusing a previous binary.
