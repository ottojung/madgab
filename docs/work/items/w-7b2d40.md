# Establish what actually bounds a proposal's visibility, and fix that thing

## Goal

For the second canonical example, decide with measurement whether the binding
constraint on a proposal becoming *visible* is its **score** or its
**rank within its own boundary structure**, then fix that constraint in a way
that is general and helps other inputs.

Stated without reference to any phrase: `select_diverse` composes a global
score order with a per-structure share cap. A candidate can be far below the
global visible cutoff and still be admitted, because step 2 walks the **whole
pool**, not the cutoff. So "the candidate's score is 0.0958 below the visible
cutoff" does **not** by itself mean it can never be shown. What decides it is
how many better-scoring candidates share its boundary structure, i.e. its
**within-structure rank**, versus the cap
`cap = share_cap(top_n, available.len())` where `available` is the number of
distinct structures in the whole pool (`src/lib.rs:2919-2931`).

That is the question this item exists to settle by measurement, because two
records in this repository currently disagree about it (see "The conflict").

## The conflict, which is why this item exists

* [w-c1d3a7](w-c1d3a7.md) §8.3, from the `413113a` close-out, says of the
  canonical clue injected at its real score 0.819901291, raw rank 8,967 of
  17,553: "It is cut, by 0.0958", and concludes "`select_diverse` step 1 is
  bounded by the visible cutoff, and the clue is 0.0958 under it." Its
  recommended lever 3 is "the visible cutoff itself".
* [w-2e5b93](w-2e5b93.md) status note says the opposite: "the visible list is
  not decided by the score cutoff — `select_diverse` step 2 walks the whole
  pool and admits by boundary structure under a binding share cap (17 of 50
  slots for three real targets)".

Reading the code on this head settles the mechanism but not the numbers: step 2
is `for &i in &order` over the whole pool, gated only by
`counts[structure] < cap`. So a below-cutoff candidate **is** reachable, and
the binding quantity is the candidate's rank *inside its own structure*
relative to `cap`. Both prior claims are therefore incomplete: the first treats
step 1's bound as if it governed the whole list, the second does not name the
within-structure rank that actually does.

Coordinator note: this is a claim to be checked, not adopted. If measurement
shows the within-structure rank is already inside the cap for a real
population, this item closes as a refutation and says so with numbers.

## Why this is the right next front

The two reach families are closed on measurement
([w-5f1c04](w-5f1c04.md), [w-be6d21](w-be6d21.md),
[w-c1d3a7](w-c1d3a7.md) §7: 2,048 segmentations, 56,697 candidates, 16.6 s,
clue still absent), and `c1d3a7` showed the clue's own alignment *is* in a
retained pool. So the residual is selection and score, in that order of
priority, and the itinerary's primary goal cannot move until one of them does.

## Completion criteria

1. **Measure, do not assume.** On a release build of this head, report for at
   least six targets — the two acceptance phrases plus at least four
   non-canonical ones — the number of distinct structures in the pool, the
   `cap` that follows from it, and the distribution of **within-structure
   score rank** over the visible list. State the measured share of pool
   candidates that are below the global visible cutoff and still visible; that
   number is the size of the effect this item is about.
2. For the second canonical example, report its within-structure rank and the
   cap, and say which of the two it fails. If neither, the premise of this item
   is wrong and it closes as a refutation.
3. Any change is justified by a stated general property, not by the target
   example. A change that moves a specific clue from invisible to visible must
   be shown to do the same *kind* of thing on the four non-canonical targets,
   with numbers in both directions.
4. **No phrase-specific hard-coding**: no special case for any word,
   substring, or word sequence of the acceptance examples, in `src/` or in
   `tests/`; no axis weight tuned until one clue lands; no test relaxed to
   accommodate the change.
5. `cargo test --release --lib`, `--test corpus_integration`,
   `--test exact_determinism` and `--test approx_determinism` are run and
   reported. `approx_determinism` is the guard that approximate mode stays
   run-to-run deterministic and that raising `top_n` never retracts a shown
   proposal; a selection change is exactly the change that can break either.
6. A re-baselined `approximate_output_is_locked` is acceptable and must name
   which structures moved and why, so a later pass can tell an intended
   selection change from drift.
7. Wall clock is not regressed against `--approximate --top 50` on the six
   targets.
8. Work lands on a focused branch, is reviewed, and is integrated into
   `post-milestone-acceptance`, never into `main`. Instrumentation belongs on a
   scratch branch and scratch worktree, never on the integration branch.
9. `cargo fmt`, `cargo clippy` and doctests do not exist on this host; see
   [../../environment-notes.md](../../environment-notes.md). Do not report them
   as satisfied.
