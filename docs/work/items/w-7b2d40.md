# Establish what actually bounds a proposal's visibility, and fix that thing

## Goal

For the second canonical example, decide with measurement whether the binding
constraint on a proposal becoming *visible* is its **score** or its
**rank within its own boundary structure**, then fix that constraint in a way
that is general and helps other inputs.

Stated without reference to any phrase: `select_diverse` composes a global
score order with a per-structure share cap. A candidate can be far below the
global visible cutoff and still be admitted, because step 2 walks the **whole
pool**, not the cutoff. So "the candidate's score is 0.0958 below the visible
cutoff" does **not** by itself mean it can never be shown. What decides it is
how many better-scoring candidates share its boundary structure, i.e. its
**within-structure rank**, versus the cap
`cap = share_cap(top_n, available.len())` where `available` is the number of
distinct structures in the whole pool (`src/lib.rs:2919-2931`).

That is the question this item exists to settle by measurement, because two
records in this repository currently disagree about it (see "The conflict").

## The conflict, which is why this item exists

* [w-c1d3a7](w-c1d3a7.md) §8.3, from the `413113a` close-out, says of the
  canonical clue injected at its real score 0.819901291, raw rank 8,967 of
  17,553: "It is cut, by 0.0958", and concludes "`select_diverse` step 1 is
  bounded by the visible cutoff, and the clue is 0.0958 under it." Its
  recommended lever 3 is "the visible cutoff itself".
* [w-2e5b93](w-2e5b93.md) status note says the opposite: "the visible list is
  not decided by the score cutoff — `select_diverse` step 2 walks the whole
  pool and admits by boundary structure under a binding share cap (17 of 50
  slots for three real targets)".

Reading the code on this head settles the mechanism but not the numbers: step 2
is `for &i in &order` over the whole pool, gated only by
`counts[structure] < cap`. So a below-cutoff candidate **is** reachable, and
the binding quantity is the candidate's rank *inside its own structure*
relative to `cap`. Both prior claims are therefore incomplete: the first treats
step 1's bound as if it governed the whole list, the second does not name the
within-structure rank that actually does.

Coordinator note: this is a claim to be checked, not adopted. If measurement
shows the within-structure rank is already inside the cap for a real
population, this item closes as a refutation and says so with numbers.

### Criterion 2 — the canonical clue on `8cc71c4`

Injected at its real alignment `cuts [3, 10, 13, 15, 19]` and real score
`0.8199012907736722`, three separate numbers:

```text
ENUMERATED = yes   (by injection; the search does not surface it on its own)
RANKED     = 9517 / 18066      gap to cutoff = -0.095790597
structure  = [3, 10, 13, 15]   49 members in the pool
within-structure rank = 41      40 same-structure siblings score higher
cap        = 17
VISIBLE    = no
```

**Which of the two it fails: neither, and the cap is positively refuted as the
binding constraint.** The rank (41) does exceed the cap (17), but that is not
what stops it. The control is decisive — the same alignment injected at a score
that places it at **within-structure rank 2, comfortably under the cap of 17**:

```text
injected at 0.8781284673865422, cuts [3,10,13,15,19]:
  RANKED = 2968 / 18066   within-structure rank = 2   cap = 17   VISIBLE = no
```

Under the cap, and still invisible. So no change to the share cap, to
`STRUCTURE_FLOOR`, or to the within-structure rule can surface this clue. That
is the refutation, and it is why this item closes rather than proposing a
selection fix.

**What actually bounds visibility is the fill rank**: the global rank at which
the 50 slots are exhausted, measured at 49-101 against pools of ~16k. Step 2
breaks the moment `picked.len() == top_n`, so a candidate past that rank is
invisible *whatever* its within-structure rank. Measured directly, on all six
targets: **0** under-cap candidates are invisible at or before the fill rank,
and **4,609-4,869** are invisible beyond it. The cap and the rank are real but
*inner* gates — they decide who wins slots that already exist, and cannot
create one.

This also settles the two conflicting prior records, and both are wrong as
written:

* [w-c1d3a7](w-c1d3a7.md) §8.3's "cut, by 0.0958 ... can therefore never be
  shown" is **refuted as a mechanism**: 27 candidates below the cutoff are
  visible on the canonical target (ranks 54-101, within-structure ranks 1-17),
  and 0-27 on the others. Being below the cutoff does not prevent visibility.
* [w-2e5b93](w-2e5b93.md)'s "not decided by the cutoff, admits under a binding
  share cap" is **directionally right, still incomplete**: the cap is genuinely
  binding and saturated, but it is not what excludes this clue, and it is not
  adjustable into excluding-or-including it.

### Why the residual is score, not selection

Ranking the canonical target's 333 structures by best-member score puts
`[3, 10, 13, 15]` at **#94**, best member 0.878128 at global rank 2,966 —
itself 0.0376 below the 0.915692 cutoff. A "represent more structures" policy
would have to reach the 94th-best structure to admit this one at all, and
`top_n` is 50. So even the most generous selection rule available cannot reach
it: **the best wording this resegmentation can produce does not clear the
cutoff either.** That is a scoring question, owned by
[w-2e5b93](w-2e5b93.md) and [w-04f83f](w-04f83f.md), and fenced out of this
item (`axes::*` retuning is w-2e5b93's, and a sweep was refuted once already).
Handed on as [w-3f9c02](w-3f9c02.md) rather than fixed here.

This is consistent with, and independent of, the adjacency front's reach
refutation: that alignment is in the pool but no bounded score-ordered
neighbourhood walk surfaces it, because it sits under ~1e4 better wordings of
the same structure. My measurement agrees on the ranking and adds the
selection-side fact it does not speak to: the cap is not the gate either.

### Method notes worth keeping

* The harness reports **ENUMERATED / RANKED / VISIBLE** as three separate
  numbers, and derives rank from *emission position* in the descending-score
  order, not from the trace's first field (which is the pool index). Getting
  that wrong once reported within-structure rank 50 where the truth is 41, and
  would have supported a wrong refutation. Scores are traced at `{:.17}` so a
  candidate cannot alias its own score under rounding.
* Build provenance: `cargo clean` + full rebuild, binary sha1 and timestamp
  recorded above, per the standing rule that a failed build silently reusing a
  previous binary has already produced one wrong refutation in this repository.
