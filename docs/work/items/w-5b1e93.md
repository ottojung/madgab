# The blocker is emission order: make the traversal's slot width and suffix bound budget-derived instead of uniform

## Goal

Make the lexical traversal reach a wording that is **deep in one slot** at
the same worst-case cost as a shallow one, so the second canonical clue is
emitted into the approximate pool. General mechanism, general benefit; no
phrase-specific case.

## Why this item exists

Six fronts have now been refuted by measurement, and the sixth of them named
this one. [w-7e1a04](w-7e1a04.md) closed as a priced negative on 2026-09-27:
in a controlled population of 3,717 faithful resegmentations the two axes its
hypothesis named *reward* being finer (positive slope against clue word count
on 10/10 informative targets), and a perfect repair of the named defect is
worth `0.018750` against a gap of `0.094352`. Its verdict, in its own words:

> the blocker is emission order — the clue is 0 of 18,936 pool members and
> 0 of 23,296 dumped candidates, so it is not in the pool to be reweighted.

[w-b3e91a](w-b3e91a.md) priced the same thing from the other end: at the
**full** per-slot width of 160 the traversal emits 2,036,664 wordings of the
canonical segmentation's own structure, reaches index 159, and still never
emits the canonical tuple. A factor of **124** in the cheapest reallocation
and **1,309** in the segmentation's 2,666,496,000-wording product. It is an
*order* limit.

## The two uniformities that cause it

Both are in `src/lib.rs`, in the per-segmentation lexical phase, and both are
general properties of the current implementation rather than tuning choices.

1. **Every slot opens at one width.** [`LEXICAL_BRANCH_STAGE_0`] = 10 is the
   starting width, and the canonical alignment's per-slot ranks over the
   160-wide shortlist are **3 / 2 / 10 / 99 / 11** ([w-7b41d2](w-7b41d2.md)).
   Four slots are inside a width of 11; exactly one is not. So the clue needs
   *one slot to be much deeper than the others* and the uniform width is the
   thing that forbids it. The width is already justified by a real argument —
   a best-first walk over a `d`-slot product spends `1 + w + w^2 + ... +
   w^(d-1)` pops before its first wording, so `w` is only a budget if that
   series fits inside `LEXICAL_HEAP_POP_LIMIT` — but that argument is
   *worst-case over all slots being deep at once*, which is the same
   simultaneity assumption the item above names.
2. **The suffix bound is a separable sum of independent per-slot extrema.**
   `suf_min_cost`, `suf_min_reused`, `suf_max_fam`, `suf_min_closed`,
   `suf_min_syl`/`suf_max_syl` are each a running sum of that slot's own best
   or worst value, and the bound composes them as if one slot could be
   simultaneously cheapest, least-reusing, most familiar, least closed-class
   and syllable-extremal. It is admissible and it is loose, and a loose bound
   is exactly what makes the traversal walk 2,036,664 wordings that cannot
   beat the clue before reaching it.

The affordability model already in `src/lib.rs` (`slot_is_affordable`) shows
the intended shape of the fix and is the precedent to follow:
`committed + later_minima + cost_{k,i} <= total_budget` admits a deep
candidate in one slot at no extra worst-case cost, because the other slots
pay their own minima. The same reasoning applied to *width* and to the
*joint* suffix state is this item's work.

## Suggested shape of the work

Deliver the measurement first, then the change:

1. **Quantify the looseness.** On real targets, compare the traversal's bound
   against the best *achievable* completion score at each prefix depth, and
   count how many wordings of a given segmentation precede a wording that is
   deep in a single slot, under (a) the current separable bound and (b) a
   jointly-admissible Pareto bound over the per-slot tables (state =
   committed cost, syllables, closed count, reuse count). This can be
   computed offline on a `scratch/` branch without changing the search, and
   it prices the fix before it is written.
2. **Make the opening width per-slot and derived**, in the spirit of
   `slot_is_affordable`: slot `k` admits as many of its candidates as
   `committed_min + later_minima + cost_{k,i} <= total_budget` allows, capped
   by what the pop-limit argument affords. Record what the cap still bounds.
3. **Tighten the suffix bound** from separable extrema to a bounded
   deterministic Pareto relaxation, keeping it admissible — an inadmissible
   bound silently changes which wordings are emitted and is a correctness
   bug, not a tuning choice. Assert admissibility executably.
4. Keep determinism: emission order and the beam's `seen` set are already
   deterministic ([w-3d6c81](w-3d6c81.md)), and `--test approx_determinism`
   must stay green.

## Result (agent-5b1e930, on `madgab-emitbound-5b1e93`, base `707fb2a`)

**Verdict, in two parts, because they have different outcomes.**

1. **The emission-order mechanism this item was opened on is a priced
   negative.** A wording deep in one slot cannot be reached by the
   traversal at bounded cost, and the reason is not the width and not the
   bound's looseness: on 14 real targets the number of wordings of one
   real segmentation that stand in front of a real deep-in-one-slot
   wording is **14,411 to 934,472**, i.e. **323x to 14,601x** the
   per-segmentation allowance of 64 and **1.3x to 57x** the entire global
   emission budget of 16,384. Every one of those distances is measured
   under the *exact* score, so no ordering of the same 16,384 emissions
   reaches the wording, and no bound — loose, tight, separable or jointly
   admissible — changes it. The arithmetic is in §3.
2. **A real, general defect was found on the way and is fixed, landed and
   pushed**: the traversal's suffix bound was **not an upper bound**. It
   sat *below* the score of completions it is supposed to dominate on 12
   of 14 real targets, on up to 76% of one segmentation's whole product.
   Three commits, `958771d`, `28d5e4a`, `b49f892`, all in `src/lib.rs`.

So: criterion 1 is **not met** and is not reachable from the emission
side. Everything below is the evidence, and the item's own text is
corrected where the measurement corrected it.

### 0. Two corrections to this item's own premises, before the numbers

**The `MADGAB_TRACE_PHRASES` acceptance command does not exist on this
head, and has not since w-474813 recorded it.** `git grep env::var -- src`
returns **nothing** — no `src/` file reads the environment at all — and

therefore taken on the public `Generator::generate_pool` boundary, which

**`LEXICAL_BRANCH_STAGE_0` is not the production opening width, and the
per-slot ranks in this item's §1 are not the blocker they are cited

5 at depth 6 and 3 at depth 7 (`1+7+49+343+2401 = 2801 <= 4000`, while
`w = 8` needs 4,681). The traversal at src/lib.rs pushes only
`0..min(cap)`. The canonical tuple's per-slot indices are
**7 / 0 / 13 / 99 / 11**, so at production settings index 99 and index 13

million wordings before reaching a wording deep in a single slot" is

**The arithmetic that closes the front.** On 14 of 14 targets the
distance under the *exact* score is at least **323x** the
per-segmentation allowance and at least **1.3x** the whole global
emission budget. Three consequences, each of which retires one of this
item's three proposed steps:

* **Width (step 2) cannot work, and not because of the pop series.** The
  pop-limit-to-first-leaf argument bounds the *uniform* width because it
  assumes every slot is `w`-wide at once, and the depth measurements
  confirm the per-slot widths are far from vacuous — 7, 7, 160, 160, 93
  on the canonical segmentation, 160-wide in three slots of five. A
  derived per-slot width is therefore worth having on its own terms. But
  the distance it has to cross is 14,411-934,472, so even a width that
  contains the tuple exactly (which is what `Wmin` is) leaves it
  unreachable. What the cap still bounds after any such derivation: the
  pop limit still bounds the walk's *node expansions*, and the push count
  still bounds the heap's memory — `cap` is not the binding quantity for
  reach, and no re-derivation of it changes §3.
* **The joint Pareto bound (step 3) cannot work, and the table says so
  directly.** The `ahead, old bound` and `ahead, exact` columns are the
  two orders: on 8 of 14 the current bound already orders the deep
  wording exactly where the true score puts it, and on the other 6 the
  bound's residual slack moves it *later* by at most 3.2x (232,232 vs
  635,885 on `there is no way to know`). A tighter bound therefore
  **raises** the order distance on those six and leaves the other eight
  untouched. Tightening the bound is the right thing for the *key's
  honesty* and worthless for reach.
* **The bound was not a bound (which is why the second column is not
  monotonic).** The old form is below the real score on 12 of 14, on up
  to 1,690,500 of 2,213,750 wordings of one segmentation (76%), by up to
  0.034638. The corrected form is above it on **14 of 14**, on all
  14,563,118. Those are the figures the `SIMILARITY` measurement alone
  gives; §4 adds boundary novelty as a second, independent source.

And the structural reason, which is the one that should stop the next
pass: a deep-in-one-slot wording is a *low-scoring* wording. The three
the coverage reserve emits that the guards name score 0.59-0.61 against a
rank-50 cutoff of ~0.90. The traversal orders by an upper bound on the
final score, so a wording 0.29 below the cutoff is emitted only after
every wording that beats it, and there are hundreds of thousands of
those. **No bound and no width changes that, because it is the objective
that ranks it there** — which is w-7e1a04's +0.0922 arriving from the
search side, and is why this item's successor has to be argued from the
scorer's semantics rather than from the traversal's.

### 4. What changed, and what each remaining bound still bounds

Three commits, `src/lib.rs` only, 644 insertions / 72 deletions, no
deletions of existing behaviour and no `axes::*` weight moved.

**`958771d` — the `SIMILARITY` term.** The bound read
`SIMILARITY * (1 - cost / 4).clamp(0, 1)`, a per-*word* normaliser,
while the scorer reads the same axis per *phone* as
`(1 - (cost / phones) / 0.30).clamp(0, 1)`. The two charge in opposite
directions relative to each other: the bound's penalty is `cost / 4`, the
scorer's is `cost / (phones * 0.30)`, so for any target longer than
`4 / 0.30 = 13.3` phones the bound's penalty is the **larger** of the two
and the bound sits below the score it is supposed to dominate. Every
target this project cares about is longer than 13.3 phones.

**`28d5e4a` — the bound becomes a function of the scorer's own axes, and
the novelty term.** The bound is lifted into `lexical_score_bound`,
taking the committed prefix's `ScoreParts` and the suffix's
`SuffixRelaxation`. Boundary novelty was a **second, independent** source
of the same defect: it was re-derived per segmentation as
`1 - shared / (target_inner + clue_inner - shared)`, while the scorer
derives it with `boundary_novelty` over the clue's own cuts. The two
differ by 0.167 of the axis on a real four-word target — 0.025 of a final
score, enough to put the bound under a completion of its own
segmentation. It is now read through the scorer's own function, which is
also *exact* rather than relaxed: every completion of a fixed
segmentation has the same cuts, so there is nothing to bound.

**`b49f892` — the three sibling sites, and the key.** `complete_span_score`
and `span_score_bound` are documented as the scorer's own score and as an
admissible upper bound on every reachable alignment, and both read the
same dead `/ 4.0`. Both now read `similarity_upper` and take the target
phone count as an argument. All four sites and the scorer itself go
through one expression, `similarity_axis`, so they are the same function
of the same two quantities and cannot drift apart again.
`partial_span_score` is deliberately **not** changed: it is a local
ordering weight for a DP's representatives, not a bound, and
`Partial::metrics` already documents that choice. `heap_key` — the
nine-decimal quantisation the heap actually orders by — is lifted out of
the closure so a test can assert on the key.

**What each remaining bound still bounds, after the change.** One
`SuffixRelaxation` per level, seven fields, each still doing exactly one
job:

| field | what it bounds | why it is an upper bound |
|---|---|---|
| `cost` | `SIMILARITY` | the axis is non-increasing in cost, so the cheapest completion is the best case |
| `reused` | `WORD_NOVELTY` | the axis falls with reuse, so a slot where *every* candidate reuses contributes 1 |
| `familiar` | `FAMILIARITY` | the axis rises with the sum it divides by the word count |
| `closed` | `CLOSED_CLASS` | the axis is *subtracted* and its penalty is convex in the share, so a minimum count stays a lower bound on the penalty |
| `shape` | `SHAPE` | rises with the sum |
| `min_syllables` / `max_syllables` | `RHYTHM` | not a relaxation at all: the exact interval the suffix's syllable total can fall in, which `rhythm_match_in` reads as a set |
| — | `PUNCH` | **absent, and exact**: its contribution is `w * (share - 1)`, at most 0, and 0 whenever every clue word is a monosyllable, so the maximum over completions contributes nothing |

The remaining bound that is *not* per-axis is the sum's cross-axis
simultaneity, worth the 0.0647 of §2 and, per §3, worth nothing for
reach.

**Admissibility, stated as the inequality with numbers, not adjectives.**
Let `C` be a completion's total cost, `M` the suffix's minimum cost, so
`C >= M + committed >= M`, and let `n >= 1` be the target's IPA phone
count — the same quantity the scorer divides by (`n = chars.len()` where
`chars` is `target_ipa`, and `metrics` divides by `total_len` computed
identically). With `f(c) = (1 - c / (n * 0.30)).clamp(0, 1)`, the bound's
term is `0.25 * f(M + committed)` and the scorer's is `0.25 * f(C)`. `f` is
non-increasing and floored at 0, so `f(M + committed) >= f(C)` for every
`n >= 1` — including the saturating case `c = 0`, where both are exactly
1, and the flooring case `c >= 0.30 * n`, where both are exactly 0. The
bound's other six terms are each the extremum in the score-raising
direction, and novelty is exact, so the whole bound is at or above the
score of every completion at every `n`. The heap orders by
`heap_key(bound) = (bound * 1e9).round()`, which is monotone in the
bound and applied identically to both sides, so the *key* dominates too;
that is asserted, not assumed, because a key that rounded toward zero
would reintroduce the same defect in smaller form and nothing else in the
tree would notice.

### 5. The tests, and what each one is for

* `the_lexical_bound_dominates_every_completion_it_covers` — the
  property, on a real lattice and real segmentations, for every prefix of
  a real segmentation and every completion of it: the bound, **and its
  quantised key**, are at least the score the search will compute.
  Non-vacuity is asserted as an **identity, not a tolerance**: at full
  length the suffix is empty, so the only axis the bound may exceed the
  scorer on is `PUNCH`, whose maximum is attained, and the gap between
  them is the `PUNCH` shortfall of that clue and nothing else. A bound
  loose in the safe direction is merely wasteful; one tight in the wrong
  direction is not a bound, and only the identity catches that. This test
  is what *found* the novelty defect — the first version of it failed at
  prefix length 2 with a gap of exactly the novelty discrepancy.
* `the_coverage_reserve_reaches_outside_the_opening_width_product` — the
  reach claim the two red integration guards stand for, restated as
  containment over the emitted set rather than as spellings: on a real
  lattice, **every** tuple the coverage reserve contributes lies outside
  the product of the traversal's own opening widths. It cannot be
  satisfied by a different member of the same sweep happening to score
  better, which is exactly how the pinned spellings were satisfied by an
  inadmissible key.
* `structural_bounds_dominate_the_real_scorer` — pre-existing, and it was
  passing without catching the sibling `/ 4.0` sites. It is unchanged and
  now runs over bounds that can fail.

Nothing in either new test names a canonical example, a word of one, or a
substring of one.

### 8. The two depth-reach guards: red at `b49f892`, deliberately left red

`approximate_pool_reaches_matches_deep_in_a_span` and
`approximate_pool_reaches_resegmentations_deeper_than_one_walk` are
**both red**, at their original literals, unchanged from `707fb2a`.
Between them they lose three of seven pinned wordings: `ask lovey`,
`see if a law thus ish l.'s` and `see ish air law this ish l.'s`; the
other four still pass, and the third depth test
(`approximate_pool_reaches_alternatives_past_the_opening_slot_width`)
is green.

Why, measured against the pool those tests actually read (`top_n
20,000`, so the visible set, not the `generate_pool` set): with a
correct key the traversal spends its 64 emissions per segmentation on
wordings that genuinely score higher, and the coverage reserve spends
its share on a **different member of the same systematic sample**. The
three wordings that drop out score **0.590-0.610** against a rank-50
cutoff of about **0.90** — they are the low-scoring tail, and the old
key was surfacing them *because it mis-ranked*, understating the score
of up to 76% of each product by up to 0.0346.

**Making them green by swapping a literal is not available to this
front, and the steer is right that it should not be done**: pinning the
spelling this build happens to emit is the anti-pattern the guard was
written against, and the reviewer's own measurement confirmed the
obvious replacements are absent from the pool the test reads anyway. So
the tests are left exactly as they are on `707fb2a`, and **the property
they were standing in for is now asserted in-crate**, at the boundary
where the mechanism is visible, as containment over the emitted set
(§5). What this front cannot do is move that assertion down to
`tests/corpus_integration.rs`, because slot width and the reserve's
contributed tuples are not observable through the public API. **A later
pass that wants the integration-level guard back should re-express it as
a pool property rather than as spellings; that is the outstanding piece
of work this front leaves, and it is a test refactor, not a search
change.**

### 10. What is now known that was not, and the next front

1. **The traversal's key was wrong, not merely loose, and that was
   measurable on any machine in an afternoon.** Four sites, one shared
   expression, one property test, and the property test found the second
   defect on its first run. The generalisable lesson: *a bound and the
   quantity it bounds must be the same function of the same inputs, and
   a "documented as admissible" comment is not a test.* Any future site
   that claims an upper bound on a score should go through
   `similarity_axis` and be asserted the way §5 asserts this one.
2. **The emission-order mechanism is closed for this clue, with a number
   rather than an opinion.** 323x-14,601x the per-segmentation
   allowance, 1.3x-57x the global budget, on 14 of 14 real targets, under
   the exact score. Do not re-open per-slot widths, bound tightening, or
   emission reallocation for it: w-7b41d2 priced the width cap,
   w-b3e91a priced the allocation, and this item prices the order.
3. **The next front must be argued from the objective, not the
   traversal.** A deep-in-one-slot wording scores 0.59-0.61 against a
   0.90 cutoff. The traversal is doing the right thing by not spending
   its budget there. Getting this clue into the pool therefore needs the
   score to be *wrong about it* in a specific, measurable way, and the
   only mechanism in the tree that ignores score order is the coverage
   reserve — whose sample is one-deep-slot with the rest at index 0,
   which cannot express a tuple that is deep in one slot *and* off the
   corner in three others. That shape, and what it would cost, is the
   next question; it is a question about the reserve's sample, not about
   widths or bounds.
4. `scratch/5b1e93-probe` (worktree `/workspace/madgab-probe-5b1e93`,
   `CARGO_TARGET_DIR=/workspace/probe-5b1e93-target`) holds the slot-table
   dumps and the offline analysis. It is measurement-only, has never been
   merged, and every number above names the target directory it came
   from.
