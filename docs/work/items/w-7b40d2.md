(`affordable_opening_width`, `LEXICAL_BRANCH_STAGE_0`) and the walk over
`slots[k]` belong to other fronts and are untouched here.

Recommendation: **HOLD**, on a different fence than the first cut of this
work found. The stratification does what it was asked to do and then some

there, and it costs one word — `hid` — its presence in the emitted pool.
That trade is real and is priced in §6; the fence is red and is **not**
re-pinned.

## 2. The change

`src/lib.rs`, no behaviour outside the shortlist rule:

* the six admission passes are lifted out of `generate_approximate` into
  `Generator::span_shortlist`, unchanged, and the production loop calls it;
* the **fill** is no longer a walk of the global `quality` order. Its slots
  are apportioned over the cost bands in proportion to band population
  (highest average per-slot weight first — the integral form of a
  population-proportional apportionment, ties by band index) and spent
  **within a band** by `quality`;
* a pick that is already in the list spends no slot, so the fill still tops
  the list up to `SPAN_SHORTLIST` rather than stopping short. (This was a
  bug in the first cut of this work, which is worth recording because it is
  invisible in every aggregate number: it left shortlists 118–140 wide
  instead of 160 and still *improved* the criterion-2 metric. See §6.1.)
* `SPAN_AXIS_KEEP`, `SPAN_BAND_KEEP`, `SPAN_RARITY_KEEP` move from
  function scope to module scope with their values untouched, so the rule
  can be reached from the new public API;
* new public API at the boundary: `SpanCandidate`, `span_candidates` (what
  a span offers) and `span_shortlists` (what it retains), both a pure
  function of `(target, per_word_budget)`.

Keyed only on measured properties — band population, cost, quality. No
clue, sentence or word list anywhere in the change, and
`git grep -i -E "wreck|beach|recognize|justice|stupid|dupe|came|hid" -- src`
returns only the pre-existing `#[cfg(test)]` fixtures, CLI usage examples
and ordinary English comments; the change adds no hit.

## 3. The regression test

`tests/span_shortlist_bands.rs`, three tests over twelve real targets, at
the public API boundary:

1. **every non-empty cost band contributes to the span shortlist** — the
   general property. Band edges are **re-derived inside the test** as the
   three interior cuts of four equal-width bands over *the span's own
   observed cost range*, not read from the rule's axis, so a future change
   to that axis cannot make the assertion vacuous. 885 spans, **3,234
   non-empty bands, 0 unrepresented.**
2. **every non-empty cost band keeps a word near the span cheapest** — the
   same partition, restricted to candidates within 1.5x of the span's
   cheapest: **1,371 populated competitive bands, 0 unrepresented.**
3. **the shortlist width is unchanged** — no span retains over 160, every
   span reaching 160 is one that offered more than 160, and no span retains
   a word it did not offer.

Tests 1 and 2 are **discriminating, not decorative**: with the new API and
the *old* fill restored, the same survey reports **1 unrepresented band of
3,234** (test 1) and **2 of 1,371** (test 2). With the stratified fill
both are 0.

One honest caveat, recorded rather than hidden: the property is stated on
the bands the *test* derives. Under a **population**-quartile partition of
the same costs instead of an equal-width one, 10 of 3,048 bands are
unrepresented **after** the change (1 of 3,048 before) — the thin tail
bands by population can sit wholly inside one slice a proportional fill
treats as thin. The equal-width partition is the one the test states and
the one the shortfall is argued against.

## 5. The DP-harming argument (argued, not asserted)

* **Branching is unchanged, exactly.** `SPAN_SHORTLIST` is untouched at
  160, the fill tops the list up to 160 exactly as the old walk did, and
  the `slots` walk reads `0..slots[k].len().min(cap)`. The per-span
  alternative count a segmentation branches over is the *same number*,
  `min(cap, |shortlist|)`, and measured on `taco cat` the number of spans
  that actually reach 160 is 19 of 34 both before and after. Pool 18,270 ->
  18,271 and 18,936 -> 18,933 is contents churn inside a fixed-width list.
* **Every `SpanExtremes` extremum is still an extremum over the same 160
  candidates.** The extremes are computed *after* the sort, by folding over
  `selected`, which is still exactly `selected`, still at most 160 entries,
  and still drawn only from the span's own candidate set (test 3 asserts
  the last half). Each extremum therefore remains an extremum over some
  160-subset of the span's candidates and stays **admissible**: a bound the
  enumeration below can attain or beat, never one it cannot reach.
  Admissibility depends on there being 160 real candidates, not on which
  160.
* **`min_cost` can only be tighter, and tighter is valid.** `min_cost` is a
  `min` over the retained set. A stratified fill admits costlier
  but-in-family words in exchange for cheaper ones, so the retained set's
  cost floor rises or stays. A **larger** `min_cost` is a strictly stronger
  statement about the optimum, and it is a bound as long as it is true of
  the retained set — which it is by construction, since it is a `min` over
  exactly the set the enumeration chooses from. The unsound direction for a
  relaxation is an *optimistic* bound, and this one moves toward the truth,
  never past it. It can move far enough that a span bound excludes a path
  the final scorer would have liked; that is a **quality** effect, not a
  soundness one, and §6 is exactly that effect being measured.
* **The suffix relaxation and the wall clock are untouched in structure.**
  No candidate is scored that the named passes had not already scored: the
  fill re-orders a per-band `quality` sort of buckets the rule had already
  built and sorted. The added work is a four-way apportionment over at most
  4 bands and at most 60 slots.

### 6.1 A bug of my own, recorded because the aggregate numbers hid it

The first cut of this work decremented the fill's remaining-slot counter on
every pick, including picks that were already in the list. The named passes
have already taken the head of each band, so a proportional walk re-picks
those, and the list stopped at **118–140 of 160**. Every headline number
still looked *better* (623 -> 4 dropped cheap candidates) and
`approximate_pool_reaches_matches_deep_in_a_span` went red, which read as
a principled trade. It was not: it was a short list. With the counter moved
inside the successful insert, the list reaches 160 exactly as before
(19 of 34 `taco cat` spans, unchanged), the pool-reach guard is green
again, and the criterion-2 number improves further, to **0**.

The lesson worth keeping: a fill that under-fills *shrinks the per-span
branching*, which is a change to the very bound the DP argument rests on,
and no criterion-2-style metric notices. `the_shortlist_width_is_unchanged`
in `tests/span_shortlist_bands.rs` is the control that catches it, and it
is now in the branch.

### 6.2 The question the next front has to answer, with the arithmetic

> Is the pool-reach guard's expectation compatible with cost stratification
> at all, or is it a counterexample that bounds how much the cheapest band
> must retain?

**It is compatible, and the general rule is a FLOOR, not a repartition.**
The arithmetic, on `taco cat`, span `[6,8)`, measured:

* band populations offered: `[3, 44, 152, 57]` for bands 0..3 under the
  rule's own axis (`cost / 0.5 * 4`), 256 candidates in all;
* `net` is a candidate of this span at cost **0.2649745686**, i.e. band 2,
  with **47 candidates cheaper than it**;
* under the old global fill, band 2 kept roughly its global-quality prefix
  (~113 of 152), which contains `net`;
* under a *pure* population-proportional fill of 60 slots over those
  populations, band 2's share is 60 * 152/256 = **35.6** and band 3's is
  **13.4** — so band 2 is thinned to a third of what the global order kept
  it, and any band-2 word outside band 2's own quality top ~36 is lost.

So a pure repartition is **not** compatible with "a deep-in-span cheap-ish
match must survive", and the guard is a genuine counterexample to it. But
the counterexample does not bound the *cheapest* band: it bounds the
**thickest** band, and the two are different objects. The cheap bands are
already safe — for this span band 0 (3 words) and band 1 (44) are retained
**in full**, and all 47 candidates cheaper than `net` are retained.

**Therefore the general rule that satisfies both is a floor on the cheap
end plus stratification of what is left over:**

> retain at least as many candidates at or below each cheap cost threshold
> as the global `quality` order would have retained, then apportion the
> remaining slots over the bands.

The floor is what the pool-reach guards need; the apportionment of the
remainder is what closes the 623. The two are not in conflict on any span
measured here — for `taco cat` `[6,8)` the floor is already satisfied
(3/3 and 44/44) and only the middle band is thinned — but the floor is what
has to be *stated* to keep a future change from re-breaking the guard by
accident, and this branch does not state it, which is why the guard went
red in its first cut and why the emission of `hid` is sensitive to which
160 a span keeps.
