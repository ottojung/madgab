## The hypothesis this item is opened on

A clue that is a genuine resegmentation of the target is, almost by
definition, made of *more, shorter* words than the target's own wording. The
score has several axes whose penalty grows with the clue's own length or
segment count while the thing being rewarded does not:

* `axes::NOVELTY` is a Jaccard distance over boundary cuts, and both
  `target_inner_count` and `clue_inner = depth - 1` grow with resegmentation,
  so a clue that re-cuts the target into more pieces is charged twice for the
  same evidence;
* `axes::WORD_NOVELTY` is `1 - reused / word_count`, so splitting the target's
  words up is charged as novelty even when the resegmentation is faithful;
* `axes::SIMILARITY` normalises cost by the target's phone count, which is
  length-neutral, while the per-word proxies normalise by word count, which
  is not.

The canonical alignment's recorded loss decomposition is consistent with this:
it loses on acoustic cost, boundary novelty and familiarity and wins only on
the weak axes. Whether that is a length artefact or a real quality gap is
exactly what this item must settle **by measurement on many targets, not on
the canonical pair alone**.

  to the axis ceiling: `0.15 × (1 - 0.875)` = **0.018750**.
* `WORD_NOVELTY` = `1 - 0/5` = **1.000**, the axis ceiling. Headroom
  **0.000000**.
* Total available from a perfect repair of this item's hypothesis:
  **0.018750**.
* Gap to the pool rank-50 cutoff: `0.915122 - 0.820770` = **0.094352**.
  Gap to the printed-50 cutoff: `0.912971 - 0.820770` = **0.092201**.
* 0.018750 / 0.094352 = **19.9%**; 0.018750 / 0.092201 = **20.3%**. Short by
  a factor of **4.9**.

The rest of the decomposition, and why none of it is a length term:
`SIMILARITY` 0.25 on a real edit cost (the clue is *not* an exact
resegmentation — it does not appear in the case-2 exact ladder at all, so
its cost is genuinely non-zero), `FAMILIARITY` 0.10, and `RHYTHM` 0.30.
The two named axes together are **0.30 of 1.15** total weight, and the
canonical clue already sits at the ceiling of one of them.

### 5. What `RHYTHM` actually charges a resegmentation for

Per the 06:2x steer, `RHYTHM` is the only length-coupled axis left, so the
question criterion 4 asks is: what does it charge, and what *should* it
reward?

**It does not reference word count at all.** `rhythm_match` reads
`|clue_syllables - target_syllables|` and nothing else: one syllable of
error costs half the axis (0.15 of score), two cost all of it. The measured
coupling is therefore not a length charge but a *sayability* charge, and
the controlled ladder separates the two cleanly:

* slope is **exactly 0.000000 on 5 of the 10** informative targets — a
  resegmentation that preserves the syllable total is charged **nothing**
  for being finer, at any word count;
* slope is **exactly −0.500000 on 4 of the 10**, which is precisely the
  one-extra-syllable step and nothing else;
* pooled slope is −0.127837/word, × 0.30 = **−0.038** of score per extra
  word, and it is not monotone, because more cuts do not monotonically break
  the syllable total.

The mechanism is visible in the case-2 ladder. Its best 5-word faithful
resegmentation scores **0.744492** with `RHYTHM` = 1.0, and its 6-word
siblings score **0.646906** with `RHYTHM` = 0.5 — the split is
`... stupid game` → `... stupid gay mm`, which turns one syllable into two
and halves the axis, costing 0.150, on top of 0.101 on `FAMILIARITY` for the
junk word. **The axis is charging for `gay mm` being unsayable, not for the
clue being cut finer**, and in that instance it is right to. A form that
made `RHYTHM` length-invariant would have to stop reading syllables, and
then the sharpest and most defensible term in the objective — 0.30, the
joint-largest weight — would stop distinguishing a sayable clue from
`gay mm`. So `RHYTHM` is **not** the defect, and no change to it is
proposed.

### 7. The quantity that is the blocker

For the second canonical clue, the score's form is not the blocker.
[w-b3e91a](w-b3e91a.md) priced the search side: the global emission ceiling
is saturated, and at the *full* per-slot width the traversal emits
2,036,664 wordings of the canonical segmentation's own structure — 124× the
whole ceiling — and still never emits the tuple, because 2,036,664
better-bound wordings stand in front of it. This front confirms the
consequence from the other end and independently: the alignment appears
**0 times in 18,936** pool members and **0 times in 23,296** dumped
candidates. It is not in the pool, so there is nothing for the score to
reweight, and reweighting the axes cannot put it there — the pool's
membership is decided by the search's per-span keys, which the traversal
orders by, and that order is 124 emissions deep in the wrong place.

Two honest caveats on that, because they are the live coupling:

* the score is not *only* a ranking key. `partial_span_score`, the
  single-word proxies and the traversal's `bound` are all built from the
  same axes, so a score-form change **would** reorder emissions. What is
  priced above is that a change confined to the length handling cannot
  produce a 0.0944 improvement in a candidate's score, so it cannot make the
  candidate outrank a field it is already 9,600 ranks behind — and the
  emission order would have to move by a factor of 124, which is
  [w-b3e91a](w-b3e91a.md)'s arithmetic, not this item's.
* a *general* improvement to the quality axes could in principle reorder
  emissions. That is not a length fix and it is not what this item was
  opened on, and [w-04f83f](w-04f83f.md) has already refuted the monotone
  reweighting of the current axes. It would be a new front with its own
  measurement, not an edit here.

### 11. Corrections to this item's own text, recorded rather than edited

* The hypothesis's second bullet has the sign of `WORD_NOVELTY` backwards.
  `1 - reused / word_count` entered with weight **+0.15** is a *reward* for
  clue words that are not target words; the clue that reuses everything
  scores 0 on it. It cannot "charge" a resegmentation.
* The hypothesis's first bullet is right that `NOVELTY` and `target_inner_count`
  both grow with resegmentation, but the consequence is the opposite of the
  one drawn: growing the union faster than the intersection *raises* a
  Jaccard distance. Measured pooled slope **+0.059179**/word, §2.
* The bullet on `SIMILARITY` normalising by phone count "while the per-word
  proxies normalise by word count, which is not [length-neutral]" describes
  a real asymmetry, and it is *already fixed* by
  [w-9d4e10](w-9d4e10.md)'s D6c, integrated as `33a46f1` and pinned by
  `similarity_is_charged_per_phone_not_per_word` and
  `similarity_axis_is_bounded_and_reaches_both_ends`. Measured here: in the
  controlled ladder, `SIMILARITY` is constant at 1.000000 across 3,717
  candidates, i.e. it is not a length-coupled term in that population at
  all.
