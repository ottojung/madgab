# The canonical clue's residual is a score deficit inside its own structure, not the share cap

## Goal

Establish, with measurement on the current accumulation head, whether the
canonical clue `hits justice dupe hid came` can be made visible by any change
to **selection**, or whether the residual is a **score** deficit. This item
exists because [w-7b2d40](w-7b2d40.md) measured that the answer is *score*, and
routed the remainder here rather than fixing it inside a fence that forbade it.

## The measurement, and why it is not a share-cap problem

Measured by agent `7b2d40` on accumulation head `3496b86`, release build, with
`--approximate --top 50`, on a scratch branch (`scratch/7b2d40-measure`,
commit `15396cf`) carrying an env-gated dump of the retained pool and the
selector's own decisions. Full table and method in [w-7b2d40](w-7b2d40.md).

For `It's just a stupid game` (pool 17,231, 333 distinct structures,
`cap = share_cap(50, 333) = 17`, visible cutoff 0.915692 at rank 50), with the
canonical clue injected at its **real** alignment `cuts [3, 10, 13, 15, 19]`
and its **real** score `0.8199012907736722`:

```text
ENUMERATED = yes (injected; the search does not reach it on its own)
RANKED     = 8683 / 17231          score gap to cutoff = -0.095790597
structure  = [3, 10, 13, 15]       49 members in the pool
within-structure rank = 42          (41 same-structure siblings score higher)
cap        = 17
VISIBLE    = no
```

Two results matter, and they point the same way.

**1. The score-vs-cutoff claim in [w-c1d3a7](w-c1d3a7.md) §8.3 is refuted as a
mechanism.** Being 0.0958 below the visible cutoff does **not** by itself
prevent visibility. On the same target, **27 pool candidates below the cutoff
are visible**, at global ranks 54-93, with within-structure ranks from 1 to 17.
Across the six measured targets the count of below-cutoff-but-visible
candidates is 0, 3, 3, 5, 16, 27. So "the clue is cut by 0.0958, therefore it
can never be shown" does not follow from the code.

**2. But the within-structure rank is *not* the binding constraint either, and
this is the part that redirects the work.** The clue fails the rank test
(42 > 17) — and when it is injected at a score that *would* put it at
within-structure rank 2, comfortably under the cap of 17, it is **still not
visible**:

```text
injected at 0.8802260016836312, cuts [3,10,13,15,19]:
  RANKED = 2294 / 17232   within-structure rank = 2   cap = 17   VISIBLE = no
```

The reason is a third quantity that neither prior record names: **the list is
already full long before the walk reaches the candidate.** The visible list
fills at global rank **93** on this target (49, 52, 52, 54, 73 on the other
five), and step 2 breaks out of its walk the moment `picked.len() == top_n`.
Measured directly: on all six targets there are **0** under-cap candidates
(rank < cap) that are invisible at or before the fill rank, and **4,598-4,869**
under-cap candidates that are invisible beyond it.

So the binding constraint is not "the score" and not "the within-structure
rank" as two alternatives. It is:

> **reachability** — a candidate is visible only if the descending-score walk
> has not already filled the list when it arrives, i.e. its global rank is at
> or below the *fill rank*, which is a small multiple of `top_n` (49-93 here),
> not 17,231.

The share cap and the within-structure rank are real gates, but they are
*inner* gates: they decide who wins the slots that exist. Neither can create a
slot for a candidate at global rank 2,294 or 8,683. Raising the cap, or
relaxing the within-structure rule, moves nothing here.

## Why the fill rank is small, which is the actual generalizable finding

The fill rank is small because the cap is reached almost immediately: with
`cap = 17` and a pool whose top ~90 candidates concentrate in 6 structures, the
first 93 candidates saturate 6 structures and the list is done. A structure
that is not represented within the first ~93 has **no path in at all**.

The canonical structure `[3, 10, 13, 15]` is not merely unrepresented, it is
**#84 of 333** structures when the pool's structures are ranked by their best
member's score (best member `each justice too add games` at 0.880226, itself
below the 0.915692 cutoff). Its first member sits at global rank 2,292. So even
a policy that "represents more structures" would have to reach the 84th-best
structure to get there, and the best member of that structure is 0.0355 below
the cutoff — it is a *score* problem wearing a structural costume.

## Completion criteria

1. Measure whether any **general, non-phrase-specific** selection change can
   move the fill rank high enough to matter. Concretely: the pool's structures
   ranked by best member put the canonical structure at #84, so a
   "represent the top-N structures by best member" policy needs N > 84 to reach
   it, and `top_n` is 50. State plainly whether the fill rank or the list size
   is the wall.
2. If the residual is a score deficit — which this item's evidence says it is —
   own it as a **scoring** question under [w-2e5b93](w-2e5b93.md)'s existing
   weight-ownership fence, with the numbers above. Do **not** re-run an
   `axes::*` sweep as a first move; one was refuted before
   ([w-04f83f](w-04f83f.md)).
3. Any change justified by a stated general property, never by the target
   example. No special case for any word, substring or word sequence of
   `hits justice dupe hid came`, `wreck a nice beach`, or either acceptance
   phrase, in `src/` or `tests/`. No test relaxed to make a change pass.

   `approximate_output_is_locked` re-baselining must name which structures
