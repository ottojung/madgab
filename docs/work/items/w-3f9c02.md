# The canonical clue's residual is a score deficit inside its own structure, not the share cap

## Goal

Establish, with measurement on the current accumulation head, whether the
canonical clue `hits justice dupe hid came` can be made visible by any change
to **selection**, or whether the residual is a **score** deficit. This item
exists because [w-7b2d40](w-7b2d40.md) measured that the answer is *score*, and
routed the remainder here rather than fixing it inside a fence that forbade it.

## The measurement, and why it is not a share-cap problem

Measured by agent `7b2d40` on accumulation head **`8cc71c4`** (post-adjacency
merge), release build, `--approximate --top 50`, on a scratch branch
(`scratch/7b2d40-measure`) carrying an env-gated dump of the retained pool and
the selector's own decisions. Full table and method in
[w-7b2d40](w-7b2d40.md).

For `It's just a stupid game` (pool 18,065, 333 distinct structures,
`cap = share_cap(50, 333) = 17`, visible cutoff 0.915692 at rank 50, **list
fills at rank 101**), with the canonical clue injected at its **real**
alignment `cuts [3, 10, 13, 15, 19]` and **real** score
`0.8199012907736722`:

```text
ENUMERATED = yes (injected; the search does not surface it on its own)
RANKED     = 9517 / 18066          score gap to cutoff = -0.095790597
structure  = [3, 10, 13, 15]       49 members in the pool
within-structure rank = 41          (40 same-structure siblings score higher)
cap        = 17
VISIBLE    = no
```

Three results matter, and they point the same way.

**1. The score-vs-cutoff claim in [w-c1d3a7](w-c1d3a7.md) §8.3 is refuted as a
mechanism.** Being 0.0958 below the visible cutoff does **not** by itself
prevent visibility. Across six targets the count of below-cutoff-but-visible
candidates is **3, 27, 0, 1, 2, 9**; on the canonical target 27 candidates below
the cutoff are visible, at global ranks 54-101, with within-structure ranks from
1 to 17. So "the clue is cut by 0.0958, therefore it can never be shown" does
not follow from the code.

**2. The within-structure cap is refuted as the binding constraint, by
control.** The clue fails the rank test (41 > 17) — and when the *same
alignment* is injected at a score that places it at **within-structure rank 2,
comfortably under the cap of 17**, it is **still not visible**:

```text
injected at 0.8781284673865422, cuts [3,10,13,15,19]:
  RANKED = 2968 / 18066   within-structure rank = 2   cap = 17   VISIBLE = no
```

So no change to the share cap, to `STRUCTURE_FLOOR`, or to the within-structure
rule can surface this clue. This is the refutation, and it is why
[w-7b2d40](w-7b2d40.md) closed without proposing a selection fix.

**3. What actually bounds visibility is the fill rank** — the global rank at
which the 50 slots are exhausted. `select_diverse` step 2 breaks the moment
`picked.len() == top_n`, and that happens at global rank **49-101** on these
targets, against pools of ~16,000. Measured directly: on all six targets there
are **0** under-cap candidates (rank < cap) that are invisible at or before the
fill rank, and **4,609-4,869** that are invisible beyond it. A candidate past
the fill rank is invisible *whatever* its within-structure rank, because no slot
remains to compete for.

The share cap and the within-structure rank are real gates, but they are *inner*
gates: they decide who wins the slots that exist. Neither can create one.

## Why selection cannot reach it either: the structure is #94 by best member

Ranking the canonical target's 333 structures by their best member's score puts
`[3, 10, 13, 15]` at **#94**, best member 0.878128 at global rank 2,966 — itself
0.0376 below the 0.915692 cutoff. A "represent more structures" policy would
have to reach the 94th-best structure to admit this one at all, and `top_n` is
50.

So even the most generous selection rule available cannot reach it: **the best
wording this resegmentation can produce does not clear the cutoff either.** That
is a scoring question in structural costume, and it is the reason this item
exists rather than a selection patch.

## Completion criteria

   re-derive them.** [w-7b2d40](w-7b2d40.md) established on `8cc71c4` that (a)
   below-cutoff candidates *are* visible, so the cutoff is not the gate, (b) the
   clue is invisible even at within-structure rank 2 under a cap of 17, so the
   cap is not the gate, and (c) the binding quantity is the fill rank, 49-101
   against pools of ~16k. Any future proposal that changes selection must
   explain how it moves the **fill rank**, because that is the only gate left.
2. Own the residual as a **scoring** question under [w-2e5b93](w-2e5b93.md)'s
   weight-ownership fence. The concrete target: the canonical structure
   `[3, 10, 13, 15]` is **#94 of 333** by best-member score, best member
   0.878128 vs a 0.915692 cutoff. For this clue to be reachable, a wording of
   that resegmentation has to score near 0.92, i.e. the axis weights have to

ordering — fix the axes that cannot discriminate at all *and* move a

is why a reweighting alone will not be enough.

re-verify any candidate fix on this head: inject the canonical alignment at a
stated score and read `RANKED` / within-structure rank / `VISIBLE` without
re-deriving the alignment by hand. It stays on the scratch branch, and the
useful, phrase-independent part (fill rank from the public API plus
`Clue::cuts`) should be re-derived as an ordinary test rather than merged as
scaffolding.
