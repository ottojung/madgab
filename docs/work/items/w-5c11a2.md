that rule, not by its cost and not by its rarity.  The alignment already holds

**src/lib.rs:2638** calls `rhythm_match(self.syllables, target_syllables)`,
and **src/lib.rs:3193-3195** is


so the clue is passed as **both** endpoints of the interval and the axis is a
function of the clue's total syllable count alone.  The one signature in the
file that could score a clue against a *band* is called with a degenerate band.

## Implementation-ready specification (recommended design: D6c)

### The defect, in general terms

[w-9c6f2b](w-9c6f2b.md) correctly removed the **word-count** term from the
similarity axis: a candidate's *total* cost was being charged once per
candidate for a per-word property.  What it left in place is the
**span-length** term.  A clue word that must cover six of the target's phones
is charged edit cost for all six, so at *equal per-phone phonetic quality* a
clue cut into longer words scores strictly below one cut into shorter words.
The axis is still a function of how the target's phones happen to be
distributed among the words rather than of how good those words are — which is
the same class of bias, one level down, and it is the bias that decides the
canonical alignment against the best five-word clue in the pool (0.7761 vs
0.8698, worth 0.022326).

### Exact lines

**src/lib.rs:2617-2620**, inside `Partial::metrics`:

```rust
let cost_per_word = self.sub_cost_total / words.max(1) as f64;
let similarity = (1.0
    - cost_per_word / axes::SIMILARITY_COST_PER_WORD)
    .clamp(0.0, 1.0);
```

becomes

```rust
let cost_per_phone = self.sub_cost_total / total_len.max(1) as f64;
let similarity =
    (1.0 - cost_per_phone / axes::SIMILARITY_COST_PER_PHONE).clamp(0.0, 1.0);
```

**src/lib.rs:2786**, in `mod axes`: replace

```rust
pub const SIMILARITY_COST_PER_WORD: f64 = 1.0;
```

with

```rust
pub const SIMILARITY_COST_PER_PHONE: f64 = 0.30;
```

Nothing else changes.  `total_len` is already a parameter of `metrics`
(**src/lib.rs:2584**) and is the target's phone count, so no signature
changes; `axes` is private, so the rename is contained; `words` stays in use by
four other axes.

### Why 0.30, and the band it may move in

The constant keeps the axis's documented meaning — "the mean cost that scores
this axis's full penalty" — on the new denominator.  The safe band is
**[0.15, 0.35]**, measured: at 0.15 `wreck a nice beach` is at printed rank 44,
at 0.20 rank 36, at 0.25 rank 27, at **0.30 rank 27**, at 0.35 rank 47; at
0.10 it is rank 60 (**out**) and at 0.40 rank 76 (**out**).  0.30 is
recommended because it is the value that maximises the alignment's score
(0.820770) among the designs that keep case 1 in the printed 50 without
touching `PUNCH` or `RHYTHM`, it leaves case 1 three ranks from where it is
(baseline 28) with 0.20 of headroom below it, and it is the midpoint of the
measured safe band in the direction that matters.  0.25 is the alternative if
breaking the case-2 monopoly harder is preferred: 12 of the printed 50 become
five-word at a cost of 0.001277 on the alignment's gap.

### Effect on pool size and on the relative cutoff

- **Pool size: unchanged — 18,917 (case 2) and 18,242 (case 1).**  A change to
  the score is a pure re-ordering of the candidates the search already emits;

  the whole table could be priced without a rebuild.
- **Case-2 relative cutoff: 0.917129 -> 0.915122**, i.e. **-0.002007**, and the

  The best five-word clue rises 0.913327 -> 0.918276 and **crosses the
  cutoff** for the first time.  This is the general property the design buys:
  a short target's proposal set stops being monopolised by one length.
- **Case-1 relative cutoff: 0.917026 -> 0.918796**, i.e. **+0.001771**, still
  50/50 four-word, and `wreck a nice beach` moves 0.918313 -> 0.919950 at

- **The alignment: 0.813896 -> 0.820770, hypothetical rank 9,249 -> 9,661,
  gap -0.103233 -> -0.094351.**  It is still out.  The design is worth landing
