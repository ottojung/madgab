count. No sentence, clue or word list. Nothing about `hid`, `net`, `taco cat` or any other literal

**thickest** band, not the cheapest, and the cheap end is where the floor must be stated.

least as many candidates at or below each cheap cost threshold as the global

public API boundary on the head, per span of the canonical target, with the
same 1.5×-of-cheapest threshold w-0f3a17 and w-7b40d2 use
(`Generator::span_shortlists` vs `span_candidates`, `per_word_budget = 0.5`):

**On all 108 spans of `It's just a stupid game`, `cheap1.5x_retained ==
cheap1.5x_offered`. Zero spans drop a candidate inside their own 1.5×-of-minimum
band.** That is w-7b40d2's 623 → 0, restated per span, and it means the floor
is **already satisfied everywhere on this head** — a rule that only constrains
the cheap end cannot change anything here, because the cheap end is already
complete. It is not that the floor is too weak; on this head it has no work to
do.

And it could not reach `hid` even if it did, by arithmetic. On the span that
matters, `[0,2)`:

* the span's `min_cost` is **0.0000000000**;
* `hid` is offered at cost **0.3500000000**;
* so the cheap-end *ratio* `0.35 / 0.0` is **not finite**: a multiplicative
  floor of any multiple `k` retains exactly the `⌊k · 0⌋ = 0`-band, i.e. the
  single zero-cost candidate, and `hid` is outside every `k`. The 1.5× band of
  this span is **1 of 256** offered candidates and `hid` is not in it.

The floor family is therefore wrong in *form* for this guard. What `hid` needs
is a floor on a **band that is not the cheapest one**, or an absolute/rank
floor — retaining some candidates at cost ≈ 0.35 in a span whose minimum is
0.0. Note this is the same shape as w-7b40d2 §6.2's own arithmetic on
`taco cat` `[6,8)`, where the counterexample bound the *thickest* band, not the
cheapest: `[0,2)` is the extreme case, a span whose floor sits at zero, so the
"cheap end" is a single word and everything else is unconstrained by it.

**So: FLOOR-SUFFICIENT is false, and the negative answer is the deliverable.**
It is recorded plainly rather than made to pass.

## 4. Is the 16,384-emission budget the binding constraint? — **No**

Measured, not argued:

* at the structure that emits `hid`, **7,312 of 16,384 emissions (55.4 % spent)
  are unspent**; the ceiling is reached only at schedule position **240 of 256**;
* the pop ceiling, the one that actually bounds wall clock, is at **17.2 %**
  (175,957 of 1,024,000) — `LEXICAL_GLOBAL_POP_BUDGET` is not close to binding;
* the per-structure depth account shows the real shape: **256 structures, 256
  schedules, one structure each**, `structure_depth_ceiling` giving each
  `LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION = 64`, of which the reserve takes
  14–16 and the traversal spends the remaining 48–50 and stops. The traversal
  is **allowance-bound, not budget-bound**;
* the traversal is also **width-bound**: `STAGE-DRAIN` fires 0 times in the

  raising any global ceiling would not reach a slot index of 119.

The number that decides it: **7,312 unspent emissions of 16,384 at the point of
loss** — a 1.86× headroom against the very budget the item offers as the
explanation. **BUDGET-BINDING is false as well.**

### 4.1 The property a successor would need instead, with arithmetic

If a successor wants a *general* statement that makes the pool-content guard
hold without naming a word, the honest candidate is **not** a cheap-end floor
and **not** a bigger global budget. It is a statement about the *pair*
(shortlist contents, reserve draws):

> For every span and every candidate `c` the shortlist retains, `c` must be
> reachable at the traversal's opening width **or** at an index the reserve
> sweep is guaranteed to draw — and a 16-draw systematic sample of a
> `160·160·7·160·160·93 = 4.266·10¹¹`-cell product grid is a cover of each
> *slot*, never of the *tuple*, so no property of the form "every retained
> candidate is drawn" exists at this budget.

The arithmetic that makes the pair necessary and the two halves individually
insufficient:

* **Width half.** For a `d`-slot structure the walk reads indices
  `0..min(cap, width)` with `cap = affordable_opening_width(d, 4_000)`, i.e.
  `1 + cap + … + cap^{d-1} ≤ 4_000`. Measured: `cap = 10` at `d ≤ 4`, `7` at
  `d = 5`, `5` at `d = 6`. A candidate at rank `r` in a slot is unreachable
  unless `r < cap`; the canonical alignment needs `r = 7, 0, 13, 119, 11`, so
  it is unreachable at `d = 5` and no budget change alters that — only
  `cap`, `SPAN_SHORTLIST` (the shortlist *width*, 160) or the stage-widening
  trigger can.
* **Sweep half.** The reserve's coverage argument
  (`sweep_index`, `w-c3f81a`) is that one sweep *tiles each slot's list*:
  `stride = span.div_ceil(per)` with a rotated start. That is a per-slot
  statement. It says nothing about a 5- or 6-dimensional tuple, and the
  measured draws show it: 15 of the 16 draws at the decisive structure are
  identical across the two heads while the *contents* at one index differ, and
  one draw moved (87→86, 100→93) purely because the sampled candidate is
  picked by the admissible `bound`, which the fill perturbs.
* Consequently the only rule that can restore this guard, given a fixed sweep
  and a fixed `cap`, is a rule on **which candidates a span retains and at what
  rank** — which is the w-5e2d41 surface — and it must be stated as a *rank*
  or *band* floor, not a cheap-end ratio (§3).

**Clean yes/no for the coordinator's split-or-merge question: the consumer is
reachable by a rule on which shortlist candidates are retained — the
**w-5e2d41** surface (agent-5e2d411) — and not only by a rule on the reserve
sweep placement (w-c3f81a).** The decisive reserve draw `[136,0,0,0,0,0]` at
schedule position 126 is *bit-identical* on the two heads; only the shortlist
of span `[0,2)` changed under it. w-c3f81a's surface is therefore **not** on
the critical path for this guard, and a successor should not spend a front on
it for this fence. (It *is* on the critical path for the **second** base
wording, via draw 11 and the 99→123 rank shift — recorded, not needed.)
