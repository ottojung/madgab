## The question, stated so it can be answered yes or no

Take the *same* pool, the *same* selector, and the *same* axes with their
*current* inputs and weights. Ask what happens if the axes are **combined
differently** — for example by per-axis rank or z-normalisation, by a
geometric/soft-min form instead of a weighted sum, by a lexicographic or
tiered form where a strong content-word signal dominates a saturated one, or by
any other general re-expression. For each form that is cheap to evaluate
offline against an existing pool:

1. does the requested wording become *reachable* (pool member and selectable),
   and at what `top_n`?
2. what is the price — visible top-50 membership churn per target, the count of
   full resegmentations, the count of function-word salads, wall clock,
   determinism — on at least six ordinary targets plus the two canonical ones?
3. does it survive the `Clue::score in [0,1]` contract, and can the
   `approximate_output_is_locked` values be re-baselined with side-by-side
   before/after evidence rather than silently?

The most likely useful answer is a **general** form that stops a saturated axis
from masking a real signal — a defect that would help many inputs, not just
this one. A form that only moves this clue is a rejection, on the same grounds
as a phrase-specific case.
