# The canonical "hits justice dupe hid came" clue is unreachable for its own target

## Goal

`tests/corpus_integration.rs::approximate_finds_classic_madgab_resegmentation`
fails on `post-milestone-acceptance` at `293d723` (and at `45419a7`
before it). This item exists to own the fix. The test is correct: the
resegmentation of *It's just a stupid game* as *hits justice dupe hid
came* is the canonical Mad Gab and it is the whole reason approximate
mode exists. Do not delete or relax the assertion to make the suite
green.

Found by the [w-3d6c81](w-3d6c81.md) determinism pass, which is
`src/`-read-only and so can only report this.

## Context and measurement

Measured on `post-milestone-acceptance` at `293d723` with the release
binary:

- `--approximate --top 50 "It's just a stupid game"`: the canonical
  clue is absent. Same at `--top 100`, `--top 200`, `--top 500` and
  `--top 2000`.
- The failure is **not** in ranking, selection or the diversity rule.
  `MADGAB_TRACE_PHRASES` reports
  `raw phrase="hits justice dupe hid came" missing candidates=17405`
  even at `--top 2000`, which is the maximum retention budget
  (`top_n * 128` clamped to `[1024, 8192]`, so `final_keep = 8192`
  there). The phrase is absent from the *deduplicated candidate pool
  itself*, so it is lost before the scorer ever ranks it.
- The alternative wordings that do survive are the same resegmentation
  with different clue words: `it justice too bad aim` (0.925),
  `hits justice too add aim` (rank ~1000 at `--top 2000`), and so on.
  Across the whole visible list at `--top 2000` the words `hits` and
  `came` occur, but **`dupe` and `hid` never occur at all**.

So the structural resegmentation survives and two of the four clue
words are reachable; `/dup/` and `/hɪd/` are not. That points at
lexical reachability in the approximate span match — the span
shortlist in `FuzzyLexicon::matches_at` (`src/approx.rs`), its edit
budget, or `min_word_ipa_chars` — rather than at scoring. Note
`"it's just a stupid game"` contains the exact substring *stupid*, so
`dupe` and `hid` are expected to be ordinary high-confidence matches
for the `/stʊp/` and `/ɪd/` spans; a correct `dupe` would be
`/dup/` against `/stʌp/` (a single `t`→`d` substitution plus the
`st` reduction).

Whether this is a regression introduced by the recent
scoring/selection/optimization work or a long-standing gap is not
established here; `git log` bisecting it is the natural first step and
is cheap.
