# Most of the clue score barely varies across the candidates it ranks

## Goal

`madgab --approximate` should order its proposals by a score that
actually discriminates between them. Measured over the 2000 best
candidates of ten real targets, several axes of the clue score have a
range of **0.0000** or near it, so the ranking is being decided by a
minority of the score while the majority is a constant offset that
inflates every candidate equally.

## Why this matters, stated generally

A score is useful in proportion to how much it varies over the
population it is ranking. An axis that is constant across every candidate
contributes the same amount to all of them, so it cannot change any
ordering — but it still consumes weight, which means the axes that *do*
discriminate are being under-weighted relative to what the author
intended. The visible symptom is a list whose proposals differ in ways
the score does not see.

This is a scorer-quality item, not a milestone item. It is deliberately
*not* on the critical path to
[../skills/itinerary-madgab.md](../skills/itinerary-madgab.md)'s primary
goal, and it must not be started while
[w-7b41d2](w-7b41d2.md) is still delivering the canonical clue: changing
weights before the alignment is even enumerated would move the
`approximate_output_is_locked` baseline for no measurable benefit and
would make that front's validation ambiguous.

## The question to answer

Stated without reference to any particular phrase: **which axes of the
clue score can distinguish a sayable, well-aligned clue from a
function-word salad that matches the same phones, and is the score
weighted toward the axes that can?** If the answer is that the
discriminating axes are the ones carrying the least weight, the fix is in
the combination — a normalisation, a per-axis rescaling to its observed
spread, or a weight change justified by a measurement over a spread of
targets. A flat rescale to observed spread is *not* automatically right:
an axis with a small range may be small because the population is
homogeneous, not because the axis is uninformative.

### How it was measured, and the sampling depth

Instrumentation lives on scratch branch **`scratch/2e5b93-measure`** in

private `Metrics` struct holding each axis' *weighted* contribution, recorded

cut vector. `ZZ_AXIS_DUMP=<path>` on the release binary. Axis order matches
the `axes` module: `SIMILARITY` 0.25, `NOVELTY` 0.15, `WORD_NOVELTY` 0.15,
`FAMILIARITY` 0.10, `RHYTHM` 0.30, `SHAPE` 0.05, `CLOSED_CLASS` −0.15

**Sampling depth: the entire retained, deduplicated pool — 100% of it, not a
prefix.** Pools measured: 10,707–13,623 candidates at the default
`--approximate` (`top_n` 10) and 12,415–18,065 at `--approximate --top 50`.
Three reasons for not sampling a prefix:

1. The claim under test is about the population the score ranks, and the
   earlier table's `0.0000` findings are a property of *where you look*, not
   of the axes. Only a full-population measurement can distinguish an axis
   that is constant from an axis that is *saturated near the top*. Those need
   opposite fixes.
2. There is no budget argument for a prefix. The dump is a pass over
   candidates the search has already retained and scored; it costs no search.
   The default approximate path was used throughout, with no widened budget
   and no relaxed shortlist, so nothing here is a hypothesis about a
   configuration the executable does not run.
3. The window columns are reported *alongside* the full-pool columns, so the
   `04f83f` table is reproduced rather than contradicted (see below).

**The population depends on `top_n`**, because the emission budget scales with

assurance that it is safe.

**The mean score of the most-sayable quartile and the most-salad quartile of
the retained population differs by 0.0005**, against a score range of ~0.6.
The score cannot tell a sayable clue from a function-word salad. Only two axes
move at all: `CLOSED_CLASS`, by 0.0139, and `FAMILIARITY`, by 0.0098 *in the
salad's favour* — a rare-word salad is more familiar to the corpus than an
ordinary content-word phrase, which is the pathology `src/lexical.rs` documents
and which the closed-class axis exists to counteract. They very nearly cancel.

**And the score is not weighted toward them.** The two axes that can see the
difference carry 0.25 of 1.15 (22%). The 0.90 that cannot see it is
`SIMILARITY`, `NOVELTY`, `WORD_NOVELTY`, `RHYTHM` and `SHAPE`, and 0.45 of the
1.15 is *exactly constant* across every candidate that can be shown on every
target measured. So the answer to the item's question is no on both halves,
and the fix is in the combination.

### The residual is the score, and the numbers go to `w-3f9c02`

Stated plainly: **the residual is the score, not selection.** Nothing here
opens a selection hypothesis — `w-7b2d40` and `w-3f9c02` criterion 1 have
already refuted the share cap, the within-structure rule and the cutoff, and
the family decomposition above is consistent with that: the family's best
wording is 0.0376 short *before* any selection question is asked, and it is
short on an axis that is a constant 0.15 offset for 83.4% of the deciding
population. `w-3f9c02` is filed, integrated and deliberately unclaimed, and
this is the table it is waiting for. A later pass should claim it for the fix.

**What the measurement justifies, and what it does not.** It does *not* justify
a small reweighting, and the table is explicit about why: the family's
advantage on the six non-novelty axes is +0.0124, so `NOVELTY` would have to
fall from 0.15 to about 0.05 (a 3x cut) for the family to tie. At 0.05 the axis
is worth at most 0.033 across the whole population, so it stops seeing
resegmentation at all — and the visible list changes on every target at once,
because 83.4% of it currently sits on the cliff's upper side. Raising the
weight instead deepens a cliff the family is already below. **A weight change
on an axis with two levels in the deciding region cannot create resolution
there.**

What the measurement does justify is a change to the axis's *form* — a
normalisation or rescale of `boundary_novelty`, not of its weight — so that
"keeps 2 of the target's 4 inner boundaries" stops being the same event as
"keeps none". The smallest version consistent with the table is to score
preserved and added inner boundaries as two separate monotone terms rather
than folding both into one Jaccard distance, which is what makes preservation
costly. Population effects to predict and then measure, not to assume:

1. the 16.6% of the top-2000 below novelty 1.000 rise by between 0.005 and
   0.050, and the 83.4% at 1.000 stay at the top if the transform is monotone
   and saturates there;
2. `approximate_output_is_locked` **must** be re-baselined, because the visible
   list on every target is currently 100% `NOVELTY` 1.000 and will gain
   wordings of boundary-preserving structures — the re-baseline is a scoring
   change this time, and the commit must say so, unlike the last one;
3. the first canonical condition's family (`[3, 4, 10, 14]`, novelty 1.000,
   rank 27 at `--top 50`) is in the region the axis does not currently tax, so
   it should be unaffected — measured, not assumed;
4. `RHYTHM` is a separate, larger problem that this table also measures and
   that the same pass should state: 0.30 weight, 3 levels, exactly constant
   across the entire visible list on 10 of 10 targets. It is not dead, it is
   saturated; the honest options are to give it resolution in the region where
   candidates differ or to move its weight to an axis that has some, and
   either is a re-baseline.

### Method notes for whoever re-measures this

* **A measurement harness can change the answer.** My first version computed
  `combined` as `axis_parts.iter().sum()` instead of the original left-to-right
  expression. The rounding differs, tie-breaking in the beam differs, and the
  canonical target's pool came out at **20,064 instead of 18,065** — a
  different population, silently. The instrumented binary now reproduces the
  pristine pool exactly on all six of `w-7b2d40`'s targets (18,065 / 16,027 /
  16,547 / 16,508 / 16,163 / 15,712) and the same cutoff score to nine
  decimals, and the dump's `POOL` line is cross-checked against the
  pre-existing `MADGAB_TRACE_PHRASES` `candidates=` field on every run. **Do
  that cross-check before believing any pool-level number from a scratch
  harness.** This is the same failure mode as the widened-budget harness in
  `w-04f83f`, in a new disguise: not a wider budget but a re-associated sum.
* Instrument first at the *weighted* contribution, next to the untouched
  expression that produces the score, so the measured quantity is the summed
  quantity by construction and cannot drift from it.
* The pool is deduplicated on a case- and punctuation-insensitive signature.
  Keying a per-candidate table on the raw phrase counts orthographic variants
  twice and silently inflates the population (this also produced a first
  20,064 reading). Key on phrase *and* score bits, and iterate the pool the
  selector was given, not the recorder's rows.
* `git bisect` was not used on this pass. Per the standing method note, the
  commits around here are docs-only and cargo reuses the previous binary; every
  number above comes from an explicitly rebuilt release binary, and the pool
  cross-check above is the guard that a stale binary would fail.
