# Spend the per-segmentation emission allowance on a *spread* of index
# tuples, not only on the cost-best ones

## Goal

The per-segmentation lexical enumeration emits a bounded number of wordings
(`LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION = 64`) chosen as the cost-best tuples
in traversal order. That is why the canonical alignment is unreachable: it is
locally bad in one slot, so it sits behind every cheaper tuple, and the
traversal cannot afford to reach it (see "Established" below). The emission
allowance should instead buy **coverage of the index-tuple space** — a bounded
spread that deliberately includes tuples the cost-best corner excludes — so
that a wording which is deep in exactly one slot is emitted without the search
having to prove, by enumeration, that nothing cheaper beats it.

## The mechanism this front owns

Which tuples the per-segmentation allowance emits.

The general property: **an emission allowance should buy coverage of the
candidate space, not just its cheapest corner.** A concrete shape worth
evaluating: classify tuples by *depth profile* — which slots are deep, i.e.
have a high index — and spend part of the allowance on representatives of
profiles the cost-best tuples never produce (one deep slot, two deep slots,
...), keeping the rest on the cost-best tuples so ordinary quality is not
regressed. Any variant is acceptable if it is general, bounded by the same
named budgets, and does not need an unbounded pool to work.

Success is the release-binary measurement, not a unit test:

```sh
MADGAB_TRACE_PHRASES="hits justice dupe hid came" \
    ./target/release/madgab --approximate --top 200 "It's just a stupid game"
```

Report whether the clue is found, its rank in the deduplicated pool, the pool
size, and the wall clock, on the current head and on your change.

### 3. Why the milestone clue is out of reach *by this mechanism*

Recorded so a later pass does not spend a pass rediscovering it. The
canonical alignment is segmentation 151 of 256, slot widths
`[160, 7, 160, 160, 93]`, and its index tuple in the enumeration's own
contribution order is **(7, 0, 13, 99, 11)** — measured, not copied. Four of
those five coordinates are off index 0 simultaneously.

Against the shipped rule that is **structural, not unlucky**:

- the rule places at most `EMIT_PROFILE_MAX_DEEP = 3` deep coordinates, and
  the canonical is a **4**-deviation tuple;
- the rule places them only at ladder rungs {10, 30, 80, 200}. The
  canonical's deep coordinates are 7, 13, 99 and 11. Three of the four are
  off-ladder, and 99 falls between rungs 80 and 200.

So the rule cannot emit this tuple by construction, and no choice of ladder or
of `EMIT_PROFILE_MAX_DEEP` inside a 16-emission reserve could: the class that
contains it is *one point* of a space of

    160 * 7 * 160 * 160 * 93 = 2,667,264,000

tuples, and the reserve buys 16 of them. Expected hit rate ~6e-9, and no
deterministic choice of 16 points beats chance against a specific target.

**The general statement, which is the part worth keeping:** a depth-profile
reserve buys **depth-magnitude** coverage — it guarantees wordings exist that
are far down some slot's list — and it demonstrably does. It cannot buy
**exact-index** coverage, which is what this milestone needs: four
simultaneous coordinates at specified indices in a 2.67e9-wide space. Even the
entire `LEXICAL_GLOBAL_EMISSION_BUDGET` of 16,384 is 6.1e-6 of that space. Any
front that reports "reached the canonical tuple" must have changed something
other than which 64 tuples a segmentation emits.

### 7. What landed

Three module-scope constants and one pure selection rule, all in `src/lib.rs`:

- `EMIT_PROFILE_RESERVE = 16` — carved out of `emit_allowance` *before* the
  traversal starts, and the traversal is handed only the remainder, so the
  per-segmentation total and both global budgets are unchanged;
- `EMIT_PROFILE_MAX_DEEP = 3` — how many slots a profile may deepen;
- `EMIT_DEEP_INDEX_LADDER = [10, 30, 80, 200]`, as multiples of
  `LEXICAL_BRANCH_KEEP`, so the first rung is the traversal's own width (a
  profile never re-spends the allowance on a tuple the traversal already
  emits) and the last is past `SPAN_SHORTLIST` (so it is always the deepest
  alternative a slot has);
- `profile_tuples(slot_widths, ladder, max_deep) -> Vec<Vec<usize>>` — ordered
  deepest rung first, then most slots deep, then slot order; every slot outside
  the profile keeps index 0, so a profile representative is the *cheapest*
  wording of its shape rather than a general-purpose regression. Pure function
  of the candidate-list lengths, bounded by
  `rungs * sum C(depth, 1..=max_deep)`, so it needs no unbounded pool.

Two tests: `depth_profile_reserve_is_bounded_by_named_arithmetic` (the ladder
ordering and top-rung invariant, the reserve being a strict minority of the
allowance, the profiles-plus-traversal sum never exceeding `emit_allowance`,
and the candidate list never exceeding the named bound) and
`depth_profile_emissions_reach_deeper_than_the_traversal` (the depth-80 bar on
real targets). Plus the traversal's emission body was factored into a shared
`build` closure so both halves use one construction path.
