# Spend the per-segmentation emission allowance on a *spread* of index
# tuples, not only on the cost-best ones

## Goal

The per-segmentation lexical enumeration emits a bounded number of wordings
(`LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION = 64`) chosen as the cost-best tuples
in traversal order. That is why the canonical alignment is unreachable: it is
locally bad in one slot, so it sits behind every cheaper tuple, and the
traversal cannot afford to reach it (see "Established" below). The emission
allowance should instead buy **coverage of the index-tuple space** — a bounded
spread that deliberately includes tuples the cost-best corner excludes — so
that a wording which is deep in exactly one slot is emitted without the search
having to prove, by enumeration, that nothing cheaper beats it.

  distinct structures. A candidate that is in the pool does not have to
  outscore anything.

## The mechanism this front owns

Which tuples the per-segmentation allowance emits.

The general property: **an emission allowance should buy coverage of the
candidate space, not just its cheapest corner.** A concrete shape worth
evaluating: classify tuples by *depth profile* — which slots are deep, i.e.
have a high index — and spend part of the allowance on representatives of
profiles the cost-best tuples never produce (one deep slot, two deep slots,
...), keeping the rest on the cost-best tuples so ordinary quality is not
regressed. Any variant is acceptable if it is general, bounded by the same
named budgets, and does not need an unbounded pool to work.

Success is the release-binary measurement, not a unit test:

```sh
MADGAB_TRACE_PHRASES="hits justice dupe hid came" \
    ./target/release/madgab --approximate --top 200 "It's just a stupid game"
```

Report whether the clue is found, its rank in the deduplicated pool, the pool
size, and the wall clock, on the current head and on your change.
