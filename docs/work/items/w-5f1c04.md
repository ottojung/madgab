# Make the global emission budget a real ceiling instead of the product of two per-segmentation constants

## Goal

The search's *global* emission budget is currently defined as
`SEGMENTATION_KEEP * LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION`, and the
per-segmentation allowance is clamped to `LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION`.
Those two facts together make the global budget **unreachable by
construction**: the whole search can never spend more than the sum of the
per-segmentation clamps, so the global constant is already at its maximum
reachable value. Decouple them — let the global budget be an independent
ceiling that a few boundary structures can be funded deeply into, while the
rest are funded at the current shallow allowance — so that a wording which is
jointly expensive but reachable inside a few thousand emissions of *one*
segmentation can enter the pool.

This is the "grow the budget ~8x" lever that
[w-8f3c61](w-8f3c61.md) §8.3 named, measured against and explicitly refused
to take, because it is a budget decision that belongs to the coordinator. This
item is that decision, taken on the evidence rather than on a ratio.

## Why this and not something else: the arithmetic, already measured

- [w-8f3c61](w-8f3c61.md) §8, from a confirmed build: the canonical
  resegmentation needs **>= 404,081 emissions** of its own segmentation to be
  emitted by cost-best-first order, and the cheapest depth-profile
  stratification is bounded below by **134,400 emissions**, against a
  per-segmentation allowance of **64** and a whole-search global emission
  budget of **16,384**.
- [w-3e5c7](w-3e5c7.md), integrated as `24f3cf7`: with the canonical
  alignment forced to index 0 of all five slots and that one segmentation's
  caps lifted, it sits at **emission 23,962 and pop 28,930 of its own
  segmentation**, at structural rank 151 of 256. The forced run *did* reach
  the clue. So a few tens of thousands of emissions inside one segmentation
  is sufficient, and it is ~375x the current per-segmentation allowance.
- `LEXICAL_GLOBAL_POP_BUDGET = 256 * 4_000 = 1,024,000` pops, and
  `28,930 < 1,024,000`. **The pop budget can already pay for it.** The only
  number that cannot is the emission budget, at 16,384, and it cannot be
  raised by any multiplier because it is defined as the product of the clamp
  that limits the spend.
- [w-be6d21](w-be6d21.md), integrated as `89c85ff`: the global
  emission/pop budget is **inert across 1x..256x** — the same pool, the same
  cutoff, the same verdict on five targets — and the pool saturates at
  177,029 by 64x. That sweep multiplied a number that could not be spent. Its
  handoff also records that the shipped constants are correct and provably
  non-binding *as they are written*, which is exactly the claim this item
  falsifies by changing the definition rather than the value.
- [w-6b2f04](w-6b2f04.md), integrated as `eaab259`, already put the
  structure-aware allocation machinery on the branch:
  `structure_wording_allowance(top_n)`, `structure_depth_ceiling(...)` and
  `funded: HashMap<structure, usize>`. This item reuses that machinery and
  changes what it is allowed to spend.

## Mechanism sketch (yours to refine, not to copy blindly)

`emit_allowance` is currently `depth_ceiling.clamp(1, LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION)`.
The clamp is what makes the global constant inert. A general replacement:

1. give the global emission budget its own value, no longer written as
   `SEGMENTATION_KEEP * LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION`, and state in a
   comment what the search's real worst case is;
2. allow a *small number* of boundary structures to be funded far deeper than
   64 emissions, taken from the same global ceiling, with the rest funded at
   the current allowance. Which structures earn depth must come from a
   general, observable property — the selector admits a candidate from
   anywhere in the pool when its structure is unrepresented or under its
   `share_cap`, so under-represented structures are the ones worth buying —
   and never from anything about a particular target or phrase;
3. keep every bound bounded and named, and keep the pop budget as the
   independent wall-clock guard it already is.

If the emission ceiling turns out not to be the binding wall after all, say so
with numbers and close the item; that is a first-class result here.

## 2. The definitional premise is confirmed, and it is two walls, not one

`LEXICAL_GLOBAL_EMISSION_BUDGET = SEGMENTATION_KEEP *
LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION = 256 * 64 = 16,384` is at its maximum
reachable value, and no multiplier can move it. Measured, on the canonical
target:

- `structures = 256` and `segmentations = 256` — **every** retained segmentation
  realizes a *distinct* boundary structure. So `structure_depth_ceiling(16_384,
  256, breadth) = 16_384 / 256 = 64` **exactly**.
- `emit_allowance = depth_ceiling.clamp(1, 64)` is therefore clamped to 64 by
  *two* independent quantities that coincide: the explicit clamp, and the equal
  share. Lifting only the clamp changes almost nothing (measured: whole-search
  emissions 14,239 → 14,839, clue still absent) because `depth_ceiling` is
  still 64. **Any fix has to move both.**
- Funding a *single* structure deeply exhausts the whole global budget by
  itself: at `ZZ_DEEPCAP` 40,000 with the pop cap lifted, whole-search
  emissions stop at exactly `16,384 = 256 * 64` with `structures=1`. This is
  the item's claim, demonstrated rather than argued.
- And the ceiling is **already slack today**: baseline whole-search emissions
  are `14,239` of `16,384`, i.e. 87% spent. The global constant is not what
  binds at 1x either.

So: the premise is right, and it is a genuine wart. §5 says why it is still not
worth landing.

## 3. The refutation: the emission ceiling is not the binding wall

The clue can only ever come from one segmentation — its own,
`[(0,3),(3,10),(10,13),(13,15),(15,19)]`, which is retained at **structural rank
151 of 256** and is enumerated at `emit_allowance = 64`. So funding *that one
structure* with the entire global budget is the **optimal possible allocation**
of this item's mechanism for this clue. There is no better allocation to try.
With the per-segmentation pop cap lifted for that structure only (4,000 → 8M)
and the global emission ceiling raised (`ZZ_GEMIT` 4,000,000):

| `ZZ_DEEPCAP` | whole-search emissions | the funded structure emitted | its popped | final `cap` | clue reached |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (baseline, no deep funding) | 14,239 | 48 (median) | — | 10 | no |
| 4,096 | 18,271 | 4,080 | 6,055 | 10 | no |
| 16,384 | 30,559 | 16,368 | 20,830 | 10 | no |
| 65,536 | 79,711 | 65,520 | 76,505 | 10 | no |
| 131,072 | 145,247 | 131,056 | 207,232 | 40 | no |
| 262,144 | 276,319 | 262,128 | 358,652 | 40 | no |
| 1,000,000 | 1,008,746 | 999,984 | 1,165,061 | 40 | no |
| 2,000,000 | 2,008,746 | 1,999,984 | 2,242,598 | 40 | no |

**The traversal does not saturate — it just never arrives.** It emits its full
deep allowance every single time, and at 2,000,000 it has covered
`2,000,000 / 2,666,496,000` = **0.075%** of that segmentation's own slot
product. The clue is absent at 31,250x the per-segmentation allowance of 64 and
at 122x the entire current global emission budget of 16,384.

And the reason is legible in the last column. That segmentation's slots are
`[160, 7, 160, 160, 93]`, product `2,666,496,000`. The per-slot walk opens at
`LEXICAL_BRANCH_STAGE_0 = 10` and widens 10 → 40 → 160, but **it may only widen
after the current stage's heap has drained.** The `cap = 40` stage alone has
`40 * 7 * 40 * 40 * 40 = 17,920,000` leaves, so the `cap = 160` stage — the only
one that can reach a slot index above 40 — cannot open before ~1.79e7 emissions
have been spent on that single segmentation. `hid` sits at **walk rank 99**.

The arithmetic, then:

- a candidate at slot rank > 40 is unreachable until **~17,920,000** emissions
  of its own segmentation;
- that is **1,094x** the whole current global emission budget (16,384) and
  **280,000x** the per-segmentation allowance (64);
- it needs ~20.1M pops, about **20x** `LEXICAL_GLOBAL_POP_BUDGET` (1,024,000);
- and at the measured rate (2,000,000 emissions of that one structure = 7,594 ms
  of lexical-phase wall clock, against a 611 ms baseline) ~17.92M emissions is
  **~68 s**, i.e. **~111x** the baseline search.

The wall is real, it is the traversal's stage-0 width and its drain-before-widen
rule, and it is not a budget. That is w-c1d3a7's object, not this item's.

**Why the item's motivating arithmetic pointed the other way.** The 23,962 /
pop 28,930 figure from w-3e5c7 is an **oracle** number: it was measured with
all five canonical words *forced to index 0 of their slots*, which turns the
required tuple from `(7, 0, 13, 99, 11)` into `(0, 0, 0, 0, 0)`. That is a
cap-10 tuple, reachable almost immediately — the oracle removes the need to
reach rank 99 altogether, so it converts a >40 problem into a ≤10 problem. Under
the real ordering the same clue is not at 23,962; it is not at 2,000,000 either.
The 23,962 is a property of the oracle, not of the budget, and no general
mechanism supplies that oracle — supplying it is exactly the per-span-shortlist
reordering that w-4b1e07 and w-3e5c7 already refuted by measurement.

was correct.** Scoring the current pool against the clue's own score

## 5. Why the decoupled emission ceiling was not landed

The definitional wart in §2 is real and the comment block at `src/lib.rs:581`
describing the constants as "NON-BINDING and cannot be made to bind" is now
demonstrably the wrong claim about the *per-segmentation* share. It was still
not worth landing, on measured grounds:

- **It changes nothing observable.** The global emission budget is already
  slack at 1x (14,239 of 16,384 spent). Decoupling the constant from
  `SEGMENTATION_KEEP * LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION` cannot alter a
  search that never reaches it, so it is a comment-level fix with no test that
  could distinguish it.
- **The version of it that *would* change behaviour cannot reach the clue.**
  §3: any real ceiling has to clear ~1.79e7 emissions of one segmentation, at
  ~111x the baseline wall clock and ~20x the pop budget.
- Landing a bound whose only justification is a number measured not to bind
  would put a *fourth* misleading "NON-BINDING, do not tune this" comment on a
  constant that a later pass would have to unpick.

So `src/lib.rs` is left byte-identical to `c4b6290`, and the claim transfers to
w-c1d3a7. The budget family is closed for good: this is the second and final
refutation of it, after w-be6d21.
