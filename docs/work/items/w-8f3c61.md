# Reach a deep word in one slot by enumeration *order*, not by budget

## Goal

Make the canonical-clue blocker reachable by changing the **order** in which
the per-segmentation lexical enumeration visits slot combinations, so that a
wording which is deep in exactly one slot costs O(that depth) instead of
O(the product of the fan-outs). This is the same general question as
[w-6b2f04](w-6b2f04.md) — how the enumeration spends its budget — but a
different mechanism, and it is opened because the budget mechanism has been
refuted twice and is currently implemented in a shape that, by the arithmetic
below, cannot reach the clue.

## The mechanism this front owns

The per-segmentation search is a best-first product search over prefixes.
Its frontier has to grow by roughly the per-slot fan-out at every level, so
a combination that is deep in one slot can sit behind a large number of
better-bound prefixes at each of the levels above it. The cost of depth is
multiplicative in the levels.

The general fix is to change the **visit order** so depth in one slot is
additive rather than multiplicative. Concretely, the space of index tuples
`(i_0, ..., i_{n-1})` over the per-slot candidate lists should be visited in
an order that enumerates by *sum of slot indices* (a diagonal, or any
max-index-first stratification) rather than by best-first prefix bound, with
the emission and pop budget spent on that order. Under such an order the
canonical tuple (3/2/10/99/11, i.e. max index 99) is reached after roughly
`sum of indices` rounded work rather than after 28930 pops, and the same
property holds for any other target: a clue that needs one unusual word
becomes affordable.

Variants are all in scope and all equally valid: diagonal/stratified order
over index tuples, per-slot index round-robin, or keeping best-first for the
cheap majority and switching to stratified order once the frontier exceeds a
budget. Pick one, justify it by measurement, and record why the others were
rejected.

### 3. Why the order still cannot deliver the clue: a closed-form bound

The deliverable is not met in any row above, and the reason is not a sweep
result that a third variant might escape. Every order in the stratified family
is *coordinate-wise monotone* — "how deep am I" is read off the indices
themselves — so it must emit the target's component-wise down-set before the
target. Measured slot widths and target indices on this head:

```text
MADGAB_DIAG slots width=[160, 7, 160, 160, 93]
              target=[Some(7), Some(0), Some(13), Some(99), Some(11)]
```

(confirming the established walk ranks 7/0/13/99/11 exactly), so

> **down-set = min(8,160) * min(1,7) * min(14,160) * min(100,160) *
> min(12,93) = 8 * 1 * 14 * 100 * 12 = 134,400 emissions** of that one
> segmentation, for **any** stratified order, at any budget, at any fan-out.

Against the integrated budgets: per-segmentation 64 is **2100x short**;
`structure_depth_ceiling` = `ceil(16384/137..180)` = 91..119 is **1130-1476x
short**; and the **entire** `LEXICAL_GLOBAL_EMISSION_BUDGET` of 16,384 is
**8.2x short**. Two direct measurements of how far the order actually gets:

- at 64 emissions per segmentation, max-index-first only ever reaches **max
  index 1-3** of any slot (per-segmentation traces, `deepest=1`/`2`/`3`); the
  target needs 99;
- at the whole global budget of 16,384 emissions, max-index-first reaches
  **max index 5** and the round-robin order spends all 16,384 on
  one-deviation tuples. `hits=0` in both.

So the steer's estimate that the target is reached "after work proportional to
the sum of the indices, roughly 135" is right about the *depth term* and wrong
about the total, and the gap is the whole finding: `7+0+13+99+11 = 130` is the
cost for a tuple whose other four coordinates are at their **best** (index 0).
This target's other four coordinates are 7, 0, 13 and 11, and every one of
them multiplies: `8 * 1 * 14 * 100 * 12`. The clue is a **four**-deviation
tuple, not a one-deviation one, and a `k`-deviation class costs the product of
`k+1` terms. Depth in one slot becomes additive; depth in four slots does not.

### 6. What the milestone needs instead

The blocker is that the enumeration's ordering key **is** the final score. To
enumerate a 0.82-scoring four-deviation tuple, a traversal must emit the
134,400 candidates of its down-set first, whatever the order. The mechanism
that works is therefore one that stops ranking candidates by final score during
enumeration — e.g. exhaustively enumerating one boundary structure's
plausible region and letting the display policy choose from it. That is a
scoring/objective change, which every live front is explicitly fenced out of
([w-6b2f04](w-6b2f04.md) no axes::* move, this item no scoring change,
[w-9d4e17](w-9d4e17) the truncation point only). The next coordinator has to
decide whether to open a front that is allowed to touch the ordering *key*;
no amount of reordering, re-widening or reallocating inside the current
objective gets there.
