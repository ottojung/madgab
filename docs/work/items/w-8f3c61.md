# Make enumeration depth cost additively: stratified frontier order

## Goal

The per-segmentation lexical enumeration in `src/lib.rs` is a best-first
product search over prefixes. Its frontier grows by the per-slot fan-out at
every level, so a combination that is deep in one slot sits behind every
better-bound prefix at each level above it, and depth in one slot costs
*multiplicatively* in the levels. This front changes the **order** so that
depth in one slot costs *additively*: visit the index tuples over the per-slot
candidate lists by max-index-first stratification (a diagonal / per-slot index
round-robin), spending the existing emission and pop budget on that order.

**Outcome: refuted by measurement, with a closed-form bound that refutes the
whole order family. No source change landed. `src/` is byte-identical to
`4111352`.** The item is closed `done` as a measurement-only result; §5 states
what the milestone actually needs instead.

## 2. The measurement this front turns on: how deep the clue actually is

All from an env-driven probe (no phrase literals in `src/`; reverted, patch
kept off-branch). The probe restricts the enumeration to one segmentation —
the canonical one — lifts the caps, and reports the slot widths, the target's
index in each slot, and whether/where the target is emitted.

```text
MADGAB_DIAG slots width=[160, 7, 160, 160, 93]
              target=[Some(7), Some(0), Some(13), Some(99), Some(11)]
              downset=134400
```

The per-slot walk ranks are exactly the established `7 / 0 / 13 / 99 / 11` on
this head. **`downset` is the decisive number:**

> **Coordinate-wise down-set bound.** Every order in the stratified family —
> pure diagonal, max-index-first, per-slot index round-robin, any hybrid of
> them — is *coordinate-wise monotone*: it emits every tuple that is
> componentwise `<=` a tuple it emits, because "how deep am I" is read off the
> indices themselves. The target's down-set in the real slot widths is
> `min(7+1,160) * min(0+1,7) * min(13+1,160) * min(99+1,160) * min(11+1,93)`
> `= 8 * 1 * 14 * 100 * 12` **`= 134400` emissions.** So **no stratified order
> can emit this clue in fewer than 134,400 emissions of this one
> segmentation**, whatever the budget and whatever the fan-out.

Against the budgets on the table:

| budget | value | vs 134,400 |
|--------|-------|-----------|
| `LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION` (today, per segmentation) | 64 | **2100x short** |
| `structure_depth_ceiling` = `ceil(16384/137..180)` (sibling, per structure) | 91..119 | **1130-1476x short** |
| `LEXICAL_GLOBAL_EMISSION_BUDGET` (sibling, **whole search**) | 16,384 | **8.2x short** |
| `LEXICAL_GLOBAL_POP_BUDGET` (sibling, **whole search**) | 1,024,000 | see §3 |

The last row is the sharpest statement available: **even handing the entire
global emission budget of the sibling's front to this single segmentation is
8x too little for any stratified order**, and the equal-share rule is 2100x
too little. The coordinator's arithmetic in the item header is therefore not
just confirmed but strengthened — the budget is not merely mis-distributed,
it is an order of magnitude too small for the *family*, not just for the
distribution.

The score-descending order it replaces is worse still, and that is measured
rather than argued. With the per-slot fan-out lifted to 110 so that it
admits the target's index of 99, and the emission/pop caps lifted, on that
one segmentation:

| order | budget | leaves emitted | target found |
|-------|--------|----------------|--------------|
| best-first (today) | pop 300,000 | 67,078 | no |
| best-first (today) | pop 1,000,000 | 404,081 | no |
| best-first (today) | branch 10 (real) | 64 | no (index 99 > 10) |

So under the real order the target is **more than 404,081 emissions and more
than 1,000,000 pops deep inside its own segmentation** — 6300x the emission
budget, and *on its own* more pops than the sibling's entire
`LEXICAL_GLOBAL_POP_BUDGET` of 1,024,000. This is also the correction the
milestone most needs: [w-3e5c7](w-3e5c7.md)'s "roughly 375x deeper than the
emission budget" was read off a **global** counter (the segmentation is
retained at rank 151, so a global emission index of 23962 is reached *before*
it is even enumerated). The correct per-segmentation statement is much
sharper and much worse: the clue is a low-scoring, four-slot-deviating corner
of a 2.7-billion-member product, and no order reaches it cheaply.

## 3. Both named variants, measured end to end

Implemented behind a temporary env switch on the real default path, all 256
retained segmentations, `--approximate --top 200`, pool size and search wall
clock, canonical phrase traced:

| target | best-first (today) | `strat` (max-index-first) | `deep` (one-slot round-robin) |
|--------|--------------------|---------------------------|--------------------------------|
| `It's just a stupid game` | 17405 / 1681 ms | 21351 / 670 ms | 21230 / 665 ms |
| `recognize speech` | 14423 / 1144 ms | 17880 / 582 ms | 21258 / 585 ms |
| `a whole lot of trouble` | 14978 / 1588 ms | 23138 / 700 ms | 23019 / 646 ms |
| `congratulations on your promotion` | 9230 / 2567 ms | 21284 / 1377 ms | 22112 / 1359 ms |
| `hello there how was your day` | 12494 / 1787 ms | 23418 / 778 ms | 23028 / 756 ms |
| `I love you` | 10351 / 393 ms | 10981 / 310 ms | 10611 / 271 ms |

**Canonical clue: still missing in every cell.** And the two variants behave
exactly as the arithmetic in §2 predicts:

- `strat` (max-index-first, the literal reading of "diagonal") is a
  *combinatorial* blow-up, not an additive one. Reaching max index `r` costs
  every tuple in the box `[0..r]^n`, so with the real widths the cost is
  `7 * (r+1)^4`. Measured: at 8,192 emissions it reaches **max index 6**; at
  200,000 emissions it reaches **max index 13** — and `7 * 14^4 = 268,912`,
  which is the right order of magnitude for where 200,000 emissions lands.
  Reaching the target's max index of 99 costs about `7 * 100^4 = 7 * 10^8`.
  So the *practical* max-index-first order is ~5200x worse than the 134,400
  down-set bound, and the down-set bound is the *idealised* best case for the
  family. Max-index-first is rejected: it is the worst member of the family,
  not a diagonal at all.
- `deep` (per-slot index round-robin, one slot's whole list at a time with
  the rest held at their best) is genuinely additive — depth `m` in one slot
  costs `m` emissions — and it is cheap. It is also **provably blind to this
  clue**, and the probe confirms it: at 200,000 emissions, `hits=0`. Its class
  is the tuples deviating in **one** slot, and the target deviates in **four**
  (`7, 0, 13, 99, 11`). The cheapest class containing the target is its
  down-set, 134,400. There is no cheap order because there is no cheap class:
  a `k`-deviation class costs `W^k`, and the target is a 4-deviation tuple.

## 4. Why nothing is landed, and the bound statement

A pure score-blind order at the *existing* budget is genuinely attractive on
cost and pool size — roughly **2x faster and 20-100% more candidates on every
one of the six targets** (the pool grows because score-blind orders produce
fewer near-duplicates) — and it is still not landable, for two measured
reasons:

1. **It does not deliver the deliverable.** Missing in all 6 targets at 64,
   1,024 and 8,192 emissions per segmentation, including a 1,578,714-candidate
   pool. A 128x budget increase on a score-blind order buys nothing.
2. **It is a net regression on the locked output.** Run with the variant
   active, `cargo test --release --test corpus_integration` goes from 8
   passed / 1 failed to **7 passed / 2 failed**, and the new failure is
   `approximate_output_is_locked`, on `I love you`:

   ```text
   left (strat):  ... "0.933655 isle uhh view" ...
   right (today): ... "0.936762 eye a view" ...
   ```

   A *better* clue is displaced by a worse one. That is the defining cost of
   a score-blind emission order, and it is exactly the re-baselining the item
   forbids. Reported as a finding, not accepted as a baseline: **the order
   cannot be replaced wholesale, and a hybrid cannot help, because the
   stratified phase is still bounded by 134,400 and the budget cannot reach
   it.**

So the bound this front was asked to state, stated for the record:

- **Before:** the frontier order bounds *what the enumeration explores* — a
  best-first walk with per-slot fan-out `LEXICAL_BRANCH_KEEP = 10` and
  per-segmentation caps of 64 emissions / 4,000 pops, so per segmentation the
  search explores a `10^5` corner of a 2.7e9 product in ~1.2 s, and the whole
  search is bounded by `256 * (64 emissions, 4000 pops)` = 16,384 emissions
  and 1,024,000 pops.
- **After:** unchanged, because nothing landed. What the measurement adds is
  the *other* end of the same bound: the clue this milestone needs is
  134,400 emissions deep in the best possible stratified order and
  >404,081 emissions / >1,000,000 pops deep in the order actually used, so
  the bound that matters is not the 64 but the **2.7e9-member product itself**,
  and the target sits at the 0.014% tail of it by score.

## 5. What the milestone actually needs (for the coordinator)

The blocker is **not** enumeration order and **not** budget distribution. It
is that the canonical clue is a *four-slot-deviating, 0.09-below-cutoff*
corner of its segmentation's product: it scores 0.819901 against a top-50
cutoff of 0.9063, and every order that reaches it must emit >= 134,400
candidates of that segmentation first. No amount of reordering, reallocating
or re-pruning inside a per-segmentation share of 64-119 emissions gets there.
The mechanism that would is a change to **what the enumeration optimises**:
the ordering key would have to stop being the final score — for example
enumerating a whole *boundary structure*'s plausible region exhaustively and
letting the display policy choose, which is a scoring/objective change and
therefore explicitly out of scope for every live front.

Consequences for the two fronts:

- **[w-6b2f04](w-6b2f04.md) should not spend more passes on distribution.**
  Its `LEXICAL_GLOBAL_EMISSION_BUDGET` of 16,384 is 8x short of the
  down-set bound for a single segmentation and 2100x short of the equal
  share, and `LEXICAL_GLOBAL_POP_BUDGET` of 1,024,000 is roughly *exactly*
  the >1,000,000 pops one segmentation's clue needs. No allocation rule over
  those totals enumerates it. The structure-aware direction (mechanism 2) is
  the right shape for *pool quality* but cannot be the milestone's fix, for
  this arithmetic reason.
- **The two fronts do not collide on any line**, so nothing needs merging: this
  front never needed a budget edit, and the sibling's budget edits never
  needed an order edit. The one line both would have needed is the
  per-segmentation emission break (`emitted >= ...`), and the measurements
  show neither front can use it: the order needs 134,400 through it and the
  budget front can only supply 64 through it.
