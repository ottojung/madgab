# Reach a deep word in one slot by enumeration *order*, not by budget

## Goal

Make the canonical-clue blocker reachable by changing the **order** in which
the per-segmentation lexical enumeration visits slot combinations, so that a
wording which is deep in exactly one slot costs O(that depth) instead of
O(the product of the fan-outs). This is the same general question as
[w-6b2f04](w-6b2f04.md) — how the enumeration spends its budget — but a
different mechanism, and it is opened because the budget mechanism has been
refuted twice and is currently implemented in a shape that, by the arithmetic
below, cannot reach the clue.

## The mechanism this front owns

The per-segmentation search is a best-first product search over prefixes.
Its frontier has to grow by roughly the per-slot fan-out at every level, so
a combination that is deep in one slot can sit behind a large number of
better-bound prefixes at each of the levels above it. The cost of depth is
multiplicative in the levels.

The general fix is to change the **visit order** so depth in one slot is
additive rather than multiplicative. Concretely, the space of index tuples
`(i_0, ..., i_{n-1})` over the per-slot candidate lists should be visited in
an order that enumerates by *sum of slot indices* (a diagonal, or any
max-index-first stratification) rather than by best-first prefix bound, with
the emission and pop budget spent on that order. Under such an order the
canonical tuple (3/2/10/99/11, i.e. max index 99) is reached after roughly
`sum of indices` rounded work rather than after 28930 pops, and the same
property holds for any other target: a clue that needs one unusual word
becomes affordable.

Variants are all in scope and all equally valid: diagonal/stratified order
over index tuples, per-slot index round-robin, or keeping best-first for the
cheap majority and switching to stratified order once the frontier exceeds a
budget. Pick one, justify it by measurement, and record why the others were
rejected.

### 3. Why the order still cannot deliver the clue: a closed-form bound

The deliverable is not met in any row above, and the reason is not a sweep
result that a third variant might escape. Every order in the stratified family
is *coordinate-wise monotone* — "how deep am I" is read off the indices
themselves — so it must emit the target's component-wise down-set before the
target. Measured slot widths and target indices on this head:

```text
MADGAB_DIAG slots width=[160, 7, 160, 160, 93]
              target=[Some(7), Some(0), Some(13), Some(99), Some(11)]
```

(confirming the established walk ranks 7/0/13/99/11 exactly), so

> **down-set = min(8,160) * min(1,7) * min(14,160) * min(100,160) *
> min(12,93) = 8 * 1 * 14 * 100 * 12 = 134,400 emissions** of that one
> segmentation, for **any** stratified order, at any budget, at any fan-out.

Against the integrated budgets: per-segmentation 64 is **2100x short**;
`structure_depth_ceiling` = `ceil(16384/137..180)` = 91..119 is **1130-1476x
short**; and the **entire** `LEXICAL_GLOBAL_EMISSION_BUDGET` of 16,384 is
**8.2x short**. Two direct measurements of how far the order actually gets:

- at 64 emissions per segmentation, max-index-first only ever reaches **max
  index 1-3** of any slot (per-segmentation traces, `deepest=1`/`2`/`3`); the
  target needs 99;
- at the whole global budget of 16,384 emissions, max-index-first reaches
  **max index 5** and the round-robin order spends all 16,384 on
  one-deviation tuples. `hits=0` in both.

So the steer's estimate that the target is reached "after work proportional to
the sum of the indices, roughly 135" is right about the *depth term* and wrong
about the total, and the gap is the whole finding: `7+0+13+99+11 = 130` is the
cost for a tuple whose other four coordinates are at their **best** (index 0).
This target's other four coordinates are 7, 0, 13 and 11, and every one of
them multiplies: `8 * 1 * 14 * 100 * 12`. The clue is a **four**-deviation
tuple, not a one-deviation one, and a `k`-deviation class costs the product of
`k+1` terms. Depth in one slot becomes additive; depth in four slots does not.

### 8. Bounded warning for [w-e07c42](w-e07c42.md) (emission selection), with
### the arithmetic

That front owns *what the 64-emission allowance is spent on*: instead of the
cost-best corner, buy coverage of the index-tuple space, e.g. reserve part of
the allowance for tuples classified by **depth profile** (which slots are
deep). This is the last untried mechanism family, so here is what my
closed-form result does and does not imply for it. **This is a warning about
the milestone clue only, not a claim that emission selection is useless** —
see the constructive part at the end.

**The warning, stated as a bound.** Let the target tuple be
`T = (7, 0, 13, 99, 11)` over slot widths `(160, 7, 160, 160, 93)`.

1. *A selection rule over the shipped stream cannot reach `T`, at any
   allowance below ~404,081.* The shipped traversal is an **admissible**
   best-first walk: the suffix bounds make the key a true upper bound on every
   completion, so the pop order is *exactly* descending in final score. The
   position of a leaf in that order is therefore a property of the stream, not
   of which leaves a rule chooses to keep. Measured on one segmentation with
   the fan-out lifted to 110 so `T`'s index 99 is admissible at all, and the
   caps lifted: **300,000 pops emitted 67,078 leaves without `T`;
   1,000,000 pops emitted 404,081 leaves without `T`.** So any rule that
   *emits* `T` from that stream has first paid >= 404,081 emissions of that
   segmentation, whatever it does with the ones it already has. Reserving `R`
   of the current 64 emissions changes which of the <= 64 emitted leaves
   survive; it does not move `T` up the stream. **64 vs 404,081 is 6300x, and
   this part of the bound is a property of the ordering key, so it is not
   reachable by anything that only re-selects within the stream.**
2. *If the rule also reorders — i.e. it is a depth-profile schedule — the
   cheapest possible such schedule still needs >= 134,400 emissions.* Note
   carefully that a "deviation count" stratification is **not** coordinate-wise
   monotone (it emits `(99,0,0,0,0)` in an early stratum and `(0,0,1,0,0)` in a
   later one, and the second is coordinate-wise *smaller*), so the 134,400
   down-set bound of §3 does not apply to it directly. It is worse, not
   better. `T` has **four** deviating coordinates (7, 13, 99, 11; slot 1 is 0),
   so it lives in the four-deviation stratum, and every tuple with <= 3
   deviating slots is emitted first: with width `W = 160` and `n = 5` that is
   `sum_{j=0..3} C(5,j) W^j = 1 + 800 + 128,000 + 4,096,000 ~= 4.2 million`
   emissions before the stratum containing `T` is entered. And 134,400 — the
   coordinate-wise down-set, i.e. the *best case over the whole space* of
   stratified schedules — is the floor. So a depth-profile selection rule is
   bounded below by **4.2 million**, and the best conceivable stratification by
   **134,400**, against a per-segmentation allowance of **64** (2100x) and a
   whole-search global budget of **16,384** (8.2x).
3. *Therefore the necessary condition is a change to the ordering key, not to
   the selection.* `T` is emitted only if the traversal stops ranking
   candidates by final score — it scores 0.819901 against a top-50 cutoff of
   0.9063 and sits at the ~0.015% tail of its segmentation's 2.7-billion-member
   product — or if that one segmentation's emission allowance is raised above
   134,400, which is 8.2x the entire global emission budget and a budget
   decision, not a selection one. Both are currently fenced: no `axes::*` move,
   no scoring change, no scoring-function change, and the budget belongs to
   [w-6b2f04](w-6b2f04.md) (closed, integrated).

**What emission selection *can* legitimately buy, which is not nothing.** The
reachable cheap class is the **one-deviation** tuples — deep in exactly one
slot, the rest at their best word — because that class costs `n * W = 800`
emissions and is a genuine "one unusual word" class, which is what this item
set out to make affordable. Measured baselines for that front to beat, on the
real path at the integrated allowance of 64 emissions per segmentation:

- per-slot round-robin order: 64 emissions reach slot depth **1-3**;
- at the whole global budget of 16,384 emissions, that order spends all of it
  on one-deviation tuples and reaches depth **~100** in one slot, `hits=0`.

So a depth-profile rule that demonstrably reaches **depth 20 or more within
64 emissions of a segmentation** — versus 1-3 today — would be a real,
measurable improvement in the class this front cares about, even though it
still will not surface the milestone clue. That is the honest bar for
w-e07c42: a defensible win on the one-deviation class, with the milestone clue
