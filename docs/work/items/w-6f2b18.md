## The arithmetic that closes the front

`SEGMENTATION_KEEP` looks like a hand-picked constant and is not one:

```text
LEXICAL_GLOBAL_EMISSION_BUDGET = SEGMENTATION_KEEP * LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION
                               = 256 * 64                            = 16384
emit_allowance                   = (structure_depth_ceiling - held).clamp(1, 64)
```

`LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION = 64` is the largest number of
wordings any one segmentation may be funded, and the global budget is
exactly `256 * 64`. So the retained count *is*
`LEXICAL_GLOBAL_EMISSION_BUDGET / LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION`, and
256 is the exact number of structures the budget can fund one ceiling deep.
`SEG_STATE_KEEP` and `SEGMENTATION_KEEP` are the two constants; neither is
independent of the emission budget, which is what hypothesis 2 asked to show
and which now holds in the *opposite* direction from the hypothesis: the
derivation already exists and it is tight.

### Hypothesis 1 — retention keyed on measured productivity: refuted

Productivity at `SEGMENTATION_KEEP = 256` is 256/256 = **100%**, so
"retain in order of what they actually emitted" is the identity function on
this target: there is no unproductive segmentation for such a rule to
displace. The rule can only act by *dropping* the target's own
segmentation or by re-ordering, and neither helps:

* The target's segmentation `[(0,3),(3,10),(10,13),(13,15),(15,19)]` is
  retained at index **151 of 256** (`MADGAB_TRACE_SPANS` reports
  `rank=Some(151)`) and is walked. It is not being crowded out.
* Retention order does not move the target's funding. The schedule is
  breadth-first over structures and each structure's ceiling is
  `structure_depth_ceiling(16384, structures, breadth)` — an equal share,
  independent of the order the structures were discovered in. Measured: the
  target's total emissions are **64** at every retained count from 160 to
  256, i.e. exactly `LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION`, the maximum any
  retention rule can hand it.
* The productive-population rule is not even well posed pre-traversal:
  productivity is only observable after the walk, and the walk is what
  retention feeds. A two-pass version (walk, re-retain) cannot help either,
  because at 256 retained nothing is unproductive and at any larger count
  the per-segmentation ceiling falls.

### Hypothesis 2 — retention scaled by the productive population: refuted, and measured harmful

Sweeping the retained count over the whole DP population (knob in the
worktree only, `SEGMENTATION_KEEP` replaced by a `ZZ_KEEP`-read value and
both global budgets rescaled by the same ratio, which is what the constants
already do):

| 1854 (all) | 1854 | **83** | walked (idx 151) | 3 of 64 | 31191 | 13715 ms |

* **Down** from 256 the target's own segmentation is released at 152 and
  above; the pool shrinks by 24%.
* **Up** from 256 the target's funding falls monotonically, 50 -> 28 -> 18
  -> 3 traversal emissions, because `structure_depth_ceiling = 16384/S`
  falls as the distinct-structure count `S` grows faster than the rescaled
  budget. The base is the maximum of the curve.
* Emptiness appears only at 1854 retained — 83 of 1854 = 4.5% — i.e. it is
  a *consequence* of over-retention, not a cause of a defect at 256.
* Wall clock grows 4.9x from 256 to 1854 for a pool that grows 1.75x and
  still does not contain the target.

### The binding constraint, named

The target's per-slot ranks on this head are `22 / 0 / 64 / 85 / 7` in slot
widths `[160, 7, 160, 160, 93]` (`MADGAB_TRACE_SPANS`/`_WORDS`). Its
segmentation is walked and funded to the ceiling, and the pooled index
tuples it produced reach slot indices `[0,111] / {0} / [0,118] / [0,123] /
[0,45]` — so **every one of the five coordinates is individually reached**,
and the wording is still absent from all 17827 pool candidates, because no
emitted tuple combines them. `cap_final` for that segmentation is 10: the
traversal's own prefix walk never widens past `LEXICAL_BRANCH_STAGE_0` for
it, and the deep indices it does reach come from the depth-profile reserve
and the adjacency operator, neither of which composes a joint tuple.

So the loss is localised to **joint per-slot coverage inside one
segmentation**, not to how many segmentations are retained and not to the
emission budget. Raising the per-segmentation emission clamp does not reach
it either, measured: at `LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION` raised to
128 / 256 / 512 / 1024 with retention at 256, the pool is **17827 in every
case** and the target tuple is emitted in none, because
`structure_depth_ceiling` (64 at 256 retained structures) is the binding
ceiling, not the clamp. At retained counts of 152 / 160 / 192 the target is
dropped or unfunded for the same reason. This is
[w-a7e2b3](w-a7e2b3.md)'s finding that "the only mechanism on the board
that can reach the far coordinate" is the per-member own-slot span in
[w-9c4d21](w-9c4d21.md), now with the retention family excluded by
measurement rather than by argument.
