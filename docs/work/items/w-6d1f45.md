# Keep alignments the search prunes for non-diversity reasons

## Goal

`madgab --approximate` should be able to *generate* alignments that are
individually excellent but are lost before the final scorer ever sees
them. Today at least one such alignment is silently unreachable: no
retention rule in the beam, the span shortlist, the segmentation DP or
the lexical enumeration keeps it, and the itinerary's primary milestone
depends on it.

## Context

Residual of [w-4b1e07](w-4b1e07.md); independent of the scoring front
([w-9c2d51](w-9c2d51.md)) and the selector front
([w-3c8e10](w-3c8e10.md)).

Measured on `post-milestone-acceptance` at 86357a4:

- `hits justice dupe hid came` for `It's just a stupid game` is **not in
  the generated pool at all** — this is a reachability failure, not a
  ranking one. Its per-word alignment costs are hits 0.20, justice 0.00,
  dupe 0.15, hid 0.37, came 0.40, total 1.12, inside the 1.5 default
  total budget, and `acceptance_sequences_are_reachable_in_fuzzy_lattice`
  in `src/lib.rs` already proves the words are in the lattice.
- Two retention stages were each identified as dropping it, and neither
  has been proven correct:
  1. the per-target-span shortlist in `generate_approximate`
     (`SPAN_AXIS_KEEP` / `SPAN_BAND_KEEP` / `SPAN_RARITY_KEEP` /
     `SPAN_SHORTLIST` = 16/4/4/160) drops `hid` for its span;
  2. the segmentation DP then drops the span structure, so even a
     recovered word would not be enumerated.
- The hypothesis worth testing first is an **admissibility** bug rather
  than a budget bug: the structural DP prunes with a `rank`/bound that
  mixes axes whose final-score contributions are not linear, so a
  structure can be pruned although its achievable final score is high. If
  the bound is not a true lower bound on the final objective, retention
  sizes are compensating for a wrong search. Establish which it is by
  measurement, not assumption.
- The general statement of the goal: for a spread of real targets, the
  top-scoring alignments in the fuzzy lattice (computed with the real
  final scorer, offline) should be present in the pool the search
  actually enumerates. That is a testable, general property and does not
  mention any particular phrase.

## Completion criteria

- The reason the alignment is lost is identified by measurement, and the
  fix is general: admissible bounds, retention rules derived from the
  objective, or equivalent. Not a bigger constant tuned until one phrase
  appears.
- A general test at the search or library boundary expresses the
  property "high-scoring lattice alignments survive into the enumerated
  pool" over at least a few real targets, or, if a full offline
  comparison is too slow for the test suite, a cheaper admissible-bound
  unit test plus a reproducible offline comparison recipe recorded here.
- The score for the recovered alignment is recorded so the ranking fronts
  know what they are working against.
- `cargo test --release --lib` and
  `cargo test --release --test corpus_integration` are green apart from
  the pre-existing acceptance regressions, and wall clock for
  `recognize speech` (5.2 s search) and `It's just a stupid game`
  (7.0 s search) does not regress. This front is explicitly allowed to
  cost a little time, but the cost must be measured and reported, since
  [w-7fa26c](w-7fa26c.md) owns the runtime budget.
- No phrase-specific hard-coding for the two canonical examples.
- Work is committed on a focused branch and integrated into
  `post-milestone-acceptance`, never into `main`.

### The premise of this item is wrong, and that is the main finding

The canonical alignment is lost, but **not** where the Context section
says, and it is **not** worth recovering. Measured on 7fa8dc6 with
`MADGAB_TRACE_SPANS` / `MADGAB_TRACE_WORDS` / `MADGAB_TRACE_PHRASES`:

- `hits justice dupe hid came` for `It's just a stupid game` occupies
  spans `0-3, 3-10, 10-13, 13-15, 15-19` at costs 0.20 / 0.00 / 0.15 /
  0.37 / 0.40, total 1.12, exactly as the item records.
- It **survives the segmentation DP**, at rank 150 of 256. The item's
  hypothesis 2 ("the segmentation DP then drops the span structure") is
  stale; the DP had already been repaired by an earlier front.
- All five words **survive the per-target-span shortlist**: their ranks
  inside their spans' 160-word shortlists are 26, 0, 48, 113 and 8. The
  item's hypothesis 1 ("the shortlist drops `hid` for its span") is also
  stale, for the same reason.
- It is lost in the **lexical enumeration**, and at no affordable budget.
  Measured by raising each cap in turn and re-tracing:
  - `LEXICAL_BRANCH_KEEP` 10 -> 200: still missing, 36 s search.
  - `LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION` 64 -> 100000: still missing,
    60 s search, pool grows 10764 -> 36478.
  So the binding constraint is neither the branch width nor the emission
  cap: it is that the best-first bound on a prefix is a very loose upper
  bound, and a state whose fourth slot is alternative 113 of 160 simply
  is not among the 4000 highest bounds. Reaching it means an unbounded
  search.
- **And it loses on the objective.** Scored with the real final scorer
  (`Partial::metrics`, `partial = false`): **0.819901**. The current
  top-10 cutoff for that target is **0.929514**. The alignment is 0.11
  below the cut, so no retention rule can put it in the output without
  the search being made arbitrarily more expensive. The ranking front
  ([w-9c2d51](w-9c2d51.md)) should treat 0.8199 as the number to beat,
  not the search's reachability.

### What was actually a bug, and is now fixed

The retention rules *were* miscalibrated against the objective, which is
the general defect the item was reaching for. In `generate_approximate`:

- `SegPath::rank`, the key that picks the `SEG_STATE_KEEP`
  representatives inside a DP state, weighted edit cost at **-0.1125**
  where `Partial::metrics` weights it at **-0.0625** (through
  `0.25 * (1 - cost / 4)`). It therefore punished exactly the
  moderately edited resegmentations approximate mode exists to find,
  about twice as hard as the scorer does. It also **omitted the shape
  axis entirely** and weighted reuse at -0.10 against the scorer's
  -0.15.
- The segmentation-ordering key replaced the scorer's
  `0.05 * mean(shape)` with a **flat 0.05**, so it could not tell a clue
  of short familiar words from one of long obscure ones.

Both are replaced by three keys built from one per-span summary
(`SpanExtremes`) and the final scorer's own coefficients:

- `partial_span_score` — ranks DP representatives. Boundary novelty is
  deliberately **excluded**: it is a function of where the path *ends*,
  so scoring an open path as if it had ended credits novelty it has not
  earned, and the shorter the path the worse the bias. (Measured: adding
  novelty here is not neutral, it collapses the pool from 10764 to 5000
  and drops the top-10 cutoff to 0.917487.)
- `complete_span_score` — orders the finished structures the lexical
  enumeration expands, where novelty is exact.
- `span_score_bound` — the **admissible** one. A suffix relaxation over
  the span DAG is folded in, so it is at least the final score of *every*
  alignment reachable through the path. It is the only key allowed to
  discard a path outright, and it discards against the incumbent the
  ordinary beam has already established (the `final_keep`-th best score
  in the completed pool — the same bar the search already commits to, not
  a new constant).

**Answer to the item's central question:** yes, the structural DP prune
was an admissibility bug, not a budget bug — the old key was not a
function of the objective at all in two of its six axes. But note the
limit of the fix: this pipeline has **no** bound-based prune, so
admissibility buys soundness, not reach. The remaining reachability
limits are all *work budgets* and are documented as such:
`SPAN_SHORTLIST` 160, `SEG_STATE_KEEP` 32, `SEGMENTATION_KEEP` 256,
`LEXICAL_COMBINATIONS_PER_SEGMENTATION` 64, `LEXICAL_BRANCH_KEEP` 10,
`LEXICAL_HEAP_POP_LIMIT` 4000, and `MATCHES_PER_SPAN` 256 in
`approx.rs`. Their *ordering* is now the objective's; their *sizes* are
unchanged from 7fa8dc6.

### Measured negative results (do not redo these)

- Removing `LEXICAL_BRANCH_KEEP` entirely (all 160 alternatives per slot
  expanded) leaves the two canonical top-1 scores **identical**
  (0.9324 and 0.9340) and the top-10 cutoffs identical, and costs
  **7x** the wall clock: 42 s and 47 s against 5-7 s. It is a runtime
  device, not the reachability bug. Not adopted.
- Making the emission cap global (one 16384 budget spent in descending
  bound order, instead of 64 per segmentation) costs ~5x wall clock and
  *lowers* the `It's just a stupid game` top-10 cutoff from 0.929514 to
  0.917487, because a purely score-ordered global budget concentrates on
  few structures and starves `select_diverse`, which is explicitly
  diversity-aware and will pick a lower-scoring but structurally
  different clue. A top_n-th-score incumbent threshold is **not** a sound
  cut for this pipeline. Not adopted.
- Narrow "lazy" best-first over the word product (each state offering
  only single-slot increments) is complete but starves: it spends all
  4000 pops on shallow prefixes and emits **5** candidates in total. Wide
  branching is load-bearing.

New tests, all at the library boundary in `src/lib.rs`:

- `structural_bounds_dominate_the_real_scorer` — for real targets and
  real alignments, the structure key and the admissible bound each
  dominate the real final scorer. **This test found two genuine errors in
  the bound while it was being written**: the suffix relaxation was
  joining alternative paths in the pessimistic direction (taking the
  minimum of the `max_*` fields), and the novelty relaxation assumed the
  shared-boundary count and the clue-inner-cut count could be chosen
  independently when every shared boundary is also a clue inner cut.
  Both are fixed; keep the test.
- `high_scoring_lattice_alignments_survive_into_the_enumerated_pool` —
  the general form of this item's goal, over three real targets. Runs an
  independent beam over the fuzzy lattice keyed on the *real* scorer
  (so it shares none of the search's span DP, per-span shortlist or
  lexical enumeration), then requires every alignment it finds at or
  above the top-10 cutoff to be in the pool the search enumerates, and
  that the search's best is at least as good as the reference's best.
- `novelty_upper_bound_dominates_every_reachable_jaccard` — exhaustive
  over reachable novelty triples.

### Residual

- The work budgets listed above still bound reachability, and the
  canonical alignment is outside all of them at an affordable price.
  Loosening them is a runtime decision, not a correctness one.
- `SpanExtremes::min_reused` is `1` only when *every* shortlisted word for
  a span reuses a target word, so a span whose shortlist happens to miss
  a fresh word contributes 1 to the bound. Sound, occasionally loose.
- The admissible bound is only exercised against a very low incumbent
  (the 1024th-to-8192th best beam clue), so in practice it prunes the
  long tail of hopeless structures and little else. It is correct, but
  it is not doing much work yet; a tighter relaxation is available if a
  future front wants one.
- The `rank` field on `SegPath` still carries the name from the old
  proxy; it is now `partial_span_score`. Renaming it is cosmetic.
- Expect a merge conflict in `src/lib.rs` with the other three open
  fronts. The performance front ([w-7fa26c](w-7fa26c.md)) edits
  `prune_partials`; this change does not, so the two are separable. The
  scoring front ([w-9c2d51](w-9c2d51.md)) edits `Partial::metrics`, which
  this change reads but does not modify — if it changes the scorer's
  weights, `partial_span_score`, `complete_span_score` and
  `span_score_bound` must be updated in the same commit, and
  `structural_bounds_dominate_the_real_scorer` is what will say so.
