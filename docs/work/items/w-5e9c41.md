# Calibration signal beyond total cost: the last unpriced route to canonical case 1

## Goal

Find and land, or price, a **scoring term that measures something the current objective does not
measure**, such that the canonical case-1 alignment separates from the near-tie band it currently
sits in. Never phrase-specific.

## Why this is the next front and not another reweighting

[w-c31a07](w-c31a07.md) closed as a priced negative and left the single most useful number on the
queue. The canonical `wreck a nice beach` for `recognize speech`:

* scores **0.919950**;
* the 10th displayed clue, `let egg nice pitch`, scores **0.921813**;
* the gap is therefore about **0.0019** — a near-tie;
* it is **present in the pool** (pool rank 26 of 13 801 at the time of measurement), not pruned,
  not shortlisted, and not demoted by a diversity decision. The selection and diversity layer is a
  measured pass-through at the shipped default;
* the stage that fixes its position is the **ordering key**, `src/lib.rs:4065`, fed by the pool sort
  at `src/lib.rs:2444`, which sorts on that one scalar;
* the canonical's score is *correct*. It is simply 27th in a descending order of scores that are
  themselves within 0.002 of each other.

So re-ordering equal scores cannot move it, and no selection policy can rescue a clue the cutoff
excludes. **The only remaining route is to change what the scalar measures.**

## Why it is not a repeat of w-e086cc

Objective weights and admissible weight vectors are already priced **empty** in
[w-e086cc](w-e086cc.md). This front must therefore NOT be a reweighting of the existing axes, and
must not re-run that analysis. The question this front answers is different: **is there a measurable
property of an alignment that the current total-cost objective is blind to, and that distinguishes
a within-budget match from another alignment that merely spends the same budget?**

## The lead hypothesis to start from

Total cost is the primary signal, so alignments at the same total cost are currently separated only
by whatever the remaining tie-breaks are. The obvious blind spot is *how the budget is spent
across the alignment*: an alignment that matches its budget cheaply in a few slots and matches
exactly in the rest, versus one that spends the budget uniformly, are equally good under a total
and are very different under a per-slot distribution. That is a **calibration / allocation-shape**
term, not a weight, and it should be expressible for other inputs too. This is a starting
hypothesis, not the assignment; the front's job is to find the term or to price its absence.
