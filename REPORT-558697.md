## 0. Headline

**No new axis computable from the data the scorer already holds closes the gap, and the
obstruction is measurable rather than rhetorical.** Four candidate axes were built,
measured on twelve ordinary targets plus the canonical one, and priced against the
production scorer. Three are rejected. The fourth (`PUNCH`, the share of clue words
that are monosyllabic) is the only one that keeps both named guards green, and it is
reported here as a real, cheap, general axis with a measured effect on the ordinary
twelve — **and it does not move the requested wording at all**, because on the canonical
target it is *constant across the entire visible top-50 band* (mean 0.8000, sd 0.0000),
so it is mathematically incapable of reordering that band.

The one axis that *does* have variance inside the canonical band (`FOOT`, metrical
footprint) is the one that pushes `wreck a nice beach` from raw rank 27 to rank 110 at
weight 0.05 — i.e. it **reddens `approximate_finds_recognize_speech_resegmentation` at
the smallest weight measured**, and even at weight 0.84 (absurd; score 1.53) it leaves
the requested wording 0.146 *below* the cutoff it moved.

**A structural finding that applies to every candidate, including any future one:** the
six existing weights sum to exactly 1.00 with `CLOSED_CLASS` at −0.15, so the objective's
maximum is exactly 1.0 and there is **zero headroom**. Any additive axis of positive
weight at any magnitude above the printed precision (6 dp) violates the documented
`Clue::score ∈ [0,1]` contract, reddens `generates_for_a_real_phrase`, and changes every
printed score, which reddens `approximate_output_is_locked` — the same blocker that put
front `9a41d3`'s M7a on HOLD. Measured, not asserted: at weight 1e-9 the locked test
passes (the delta is below print precision); at every weight measured at or above 0.05
all four candidates redden it. The ranking is unaffected by writing the axis as the
bounded penalty `+w·(v − 1)` instead of `+w·v`, so this is a *representation* problem,
not an ordering problem — but it is a coordinator decision (a re-baseline of a test this
front may not touch), not this front's to make.

**Targets.** Twelve ordinary multi-word targets, none of them named in the milestone and

**Census rule** (`c81e55` §6's metric, restated so it is checkable): a *full
resegmentation* is a visible proposal with **no target word retained**, where two words
count as shared if they are equal or if the shorter is a prefix of the longer and the
shorter is ≥ 3 letters (so `it` / `it justice` counts as shared with `It's`). Under this
rule the baseline census reproduces `c81e55` §6 on 12 of 13 targets; it differs on `the
other seven`, where this rule counts 15 of 50 full resegmentations (35 of the 50 share
`the`) against `c81e55`'s 50 of 50. The rule is stated above so the discrepancy is
attributable rather than silent.

**Additivity filter (hard, per the steer).** Every candidate is implemented as a single
additive term `+ w · v` with the six existing weights **untouched**, computed from
quantities the scorer already accumulates, with no rescaling of any axis and no
renormalisation of the total. A candidate that needed to rescale similarity would have
been rejected before measurement; none of the four did, and §6 records the one candidate
family (`RESEG`, §5) that would have needed exactly that.

## 2. The four candidates

Each is stated as: mechanism → definition → measured per-target effect → cost and
interaction → gap movement. Weights are in score units; 0.10 is comparable to the
existing `FAMILIARITY` (0.10) and half of `SIMILARITY` (0.25).

### C1 `PUNCH` — clue-word monosyllabic share (the only survivor)

**Mechanism.** A Mad Gab answer has to be *sayable at the pace of the target sentence*
and has to survive being read aloud by a person who has never heard the target. A clue
built from lookalike polysyllables (`consid`/`considerate`, `partic`/`particular`) is
phonetically excellent and humanly unusable: the solver cannot pronounce it in one pass,
so the wordplay never lands. The current objective has no sayability constraint at all —
`SIMILARITY` charges edit cost over the whole IPA stream, so a 12-phone
polysyllabic substitution that happens to be cheap scores like a 3-phone monosyllable
one, and `RHYTHM` reads only the *total* syllable count, which a redistribution across
words satisfies for free. `PUNCH` is the share of clue words with ≤ 1 syllable. It
applies to any sentence: for a five-word target of mostly monosyllables it asks the
clue to be monosyllabic too; for `It's just a stupid game` it asks the clue not to
borrow `stupid`'s three-syllable shape to cover `ˈstupəd`.

**Definition.** `v = #{clue words with ipa_syllables ≤ 1} / #{clue words} ∈ [0,1]`.
Term `+ 0.10 · v`.

**Measured per-target effect** (3 replicates each, ranges are `min-max` over replicates;
`turnover` = how many of the 50 visible phrases are not in the baseline visible 50):

| when the rain finally stopped | 31 | **50** | 21 |

there; the canonical target goes **0 → 30**; `when the rain finally stopped` 31 → 50;
`the other seven` 15 → 22; and it **costs 9** on `the cat sat on the mat`, the one target
whose every word is already monosyllabic. That asymmetry is the mechanism showing

**Cost and interaction.** One `f64` multiply-add per `metrics` call plus one `usize`
accumulator per word extension; no new traversal, no new pass, no new data — the

inside the within-arm spread on both targets: **no measurable wall-clock cost.** (My
implementation recomputes `ipa_syllables` on the gated path, which is the upper bound; a
landed version would reuse the value the extension already computes.)

Interaction with the other axes, Pearson *r* against the **baseline** combined score over
the whole pool (13 targets, 14 417–22 495 rows each): `r(PUNCH, score)` = 0.26, 0.48,
0.62, 0.63, 0.66, 0.70, 0.70, 0.75, 0.76, 0.76, 0.83, 0.86, −0.00 (median ≈ 0.70).
`r(PUNCH, similarity)` reaches 0.73. **This is the axis's main weakness and it is

### C2 `FOOT` — metrical footprint (REJECTED)

**Mechanism.** A Mad Gab is a syllable-for-syllable resegmentation: the listener has to
be able to lay the clue over the target's beat. `RHYTHM` (weight 0.30, the largest in the
objective) compares only the clue's *total* syllable count with the target's, so a clue
that puts three syllables where the target has one and none where the target has three
scores `RHYTHM = 1.0` for free. `FOOT` charges each clue word for the difference between
its own syllable count and the syllable mass the target carries in the span it consumes.
Stated for a sentence nobody here has measured: for `she told him that she was tired`
the target's stress feet are `she TOLD | him THAT | she WAS | TIRED`, and a clue that
concentrates all four of its polysyllables in the first two feet does not fit the line
even when the total is 8 either way.

**Definition.** `v = 1 − Σ_i |syl(clue word i) − expected_i| / max(target_syllables,
clue_syllables) ∈ [0,1]`, where `expected_i` is the target's syllable mass
proportional to the phones that clue word *i* consumes.

**Measured per-target effect** (3 replicates, all `X-X`): full resegmentations
`when the rain finally stopped` **31 → 0**, `there is no way to know` 50 → **36**,
`a whole lot of trouble` 50 → 45, `the cat sat on the mat` 29 → 30, `an old man in a big
hat` 35 → 40, `recognize speech` 44 → 46, `the other seven` 15 → 32, canonical 0 → 0.
Turnover 20–47 of 50. It **destroys** the census on one ordinary target outright and
degrades two more.

**Cost and interaction.** The only candidate with variance inside the canonical visible
band (top-50 `v`: mean 0.7284, sd 0.0335, range 0.632–0.825), so it is the only one with
leverage there; `r(FOOT, score)` is near zero on 11 of 13 targets (−0.22 … +0.21), i.e.
it is genuinely *orthogonal* to the existing six — which is exactly why it moves the
guard.

**Does it close the gap?** It is the only candidate that moves the requested wording
toward the band, and it still does not arrive:

| weight | rank | score | cutoff | margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 9865 | 0.819901291 | 0.915691888 | −0.0958 |
| 0.05 | 9339 | 0.862006554 | 0.953007687 | −0.0910 |
| 0.10 | 7927 | 0.904111817 | 0.991546466 | −0.0874 |
| 0.15 | 7075 | 0.946217080 | 1.033056179 | −0.0868 |
| 0.20 | 6602 | 0.988322343 | 1.078185962 | −0.0899 |
| 0.30 | 5386 | 1.072532870 | 1.171137450 | −0.0986 |
| 0.50 | 4484 | 1.240953922 | 1.357102362 | −0.1161 |
| 0.84 | 3376 | 1.527269712 | 1.673242713 | −0.1460 |

The margin improves by 0.009 and then **reverses**: the axis is self-defeating, because
the band is by construction the top of the same distribution, so weighting `FOOT` lifts
the band at least as fast as it lifts the clue. Meanwhile the guard: `wreck a nice
beach` raw rank 27 → **110 at weight 0.05**, 181 at 0.10, 307 at 0.15, 2311 at 0.84.
Suite at 0.05: `approximate_finds_recognize_speech_resegmentation` **FAILED**.

**REJECTED**, on the measured ground that it reddens a green guard at the smallest weight
measured, and on the independent ground that it does not close the gap at any weight.

### C3 `ECON` — clue-length economy (REJECTED)

**Mechanism.** The clue is read as the target, so its length is part of the fit: a clue
with materially more words than the target cannot be spoken over the target's line
without padding, and a Mad Gab solver discards it as babble. `WORD_NOVELTY` (0.15)
rewards *not reusing* target words, which pushes in the opposite direction — the more
resegmentation, the more words — so the objective currently pays for word-count
inflation twice and never prices it. `ECON` prices it once. It applies to any sentence:
for a four-word target, a six-word clue is a different joke.

**Definition.** `v = 1 − max(0, clue_words − target_words) / target_words ∈ [0,1]`
(penalises inflation only; shrinkage is already the target's own business).

**Measured per-target effect** (3 replicates, all `X-X`): `when the rain finally stopped`
**31 → 9**, `recognize speech` 44 → 50, `the other seven` 15 → 26, canonical 0 → 0, the
other eight unchanged. It buys two ordinary targets and destroys one.

**Cost and interaction.** On the canonical target it is **saturated on the visible band**
(top-50 `v` = 1.0000, sd 0.0000) *and on the requested wording* (1.000): five words for
five words. It is a constant on both sides of the comparison. On `recognize speech` it
is 0.000 for the entire top 50 while the guard clue is 0.000, so again no leverage. Its
only effect is on the mid-pool.

**Does it close the gap?** 9865 → 9553 (0.05) → 9087 (0.10) → 8447 (0.15) → 7873
(0.20), with the margin essentially flat (−0.0958 → −0.0958 → −0.0958 → −0.0958 →
−0.0958: econ contributes 0.100000000 to the clue and 0.100000000 to the cutoff, exactly,
at every weight). The guard: raw rank 27 → **194 at 0.05**, 763 at 0.10, 1189 at 0.15.
Suite at 0.05: `approximate_finds_recognize_speech_resegmentation` **FAILED**.

**REJECTED**: it reddens a green guard at the smallest weight measured, and it is
arithmetically incapable of moving the comparison it would have to move.

### C4 `LAND` — closing-word cadence (REJECTED)

**Mechanism, and why it is weak.** The last word of a Mad Gab is where the pun lands, and
an answer that ends on a function word or a three-syllable noun reads as a non-answer.
`LAND` scores 1 for a clue whose final word is a content word of ≤ 2 syllables and 0
otherwise. It applies to any sentence, and that is exactly the problem: the argument is
about *puzzle presentation*, not about the acoustic fit, and it singles out one position
for a reason the objective has no way to state. It is a positional prior wearing an axis's
clothes.

**Measured per-target effect** (3 replicates, all `X-X`): canonical **0 → 0**,
`the cat sat on the mat` 29 → **12**, `the other seven` 15 → 16, `she had a lot of
money` 50 → 50, the rest unchanged; turnover 0–49 of 50 (16 on `she had a lot of money`,
49 on `my brother has a red car`, 7 on `the other seven`). It is **saturated at 1.0000,
sd 0.0000 across the canonical top 50** and on the requested wording: the target's final
word is `game`, so every high-scoring candidate already ends on a short content word.

**Does it close the gap?** No movement at all in the margin: the clue and the cutoff both
gain exactly `w`, and the rank drifts 9865 → 10108 (0.05) → 10052 (0.10, 0.15, 0.20 —
constant, because the term is a constant on both sides). The guard is unharmed (rank 27,
margin +0.001267657 at every weight, identical to baseline).

**REJECTED**: zero effect on the metric that would justify it (the census is unmoved on
the canonical target and negative on one ordinary target), 100 % turnover churn on
targets where nothing else changes, and a mechanism that is a position prior rather than
a property of the sound.

## 5. Disqualified without measurement, one line each

* **Any axis whose justification is that it lifts `hits justice dupe hid came` or

* **A stress-mark axis** (the corpus IPA carries `ˈ`/`ˌ`, and `approx::ipa_syllables`
  discards them): the closest untested sibling of `FOOT`, not measured here — see §6.

## 6. What I did **not** measure

* **A stress/foot axis.** `FOOT` is a syllable-mass proxy, not a stress alignment: it
  cannot tell `REcord` from `reCORD`, and the corpus does carry stress marks that
  `approx::ipa_syllables` throws away by design. A stress axis would need the word-node
  path to carry per-syllable stress, which touches the traversal-visible quantity rather
  than only the scorer. It is the one untested member of the `FOOT` family, and it is the
  axis I would measure next if the guard problem had a solution.
* **Composition with `9a41d3`'s M7a.** Per the steer, M7a is a separate composable
  proposal and I measured nothing on top of it; every number here is on `3d520c0` with the
  similarity axis as `post-milestone-acceptance` has it.
* **Any interaction with `select_diverse`, the share cap or `STRUCTURE_FLOOR`.** The
  rank/cutoff numbers are the raw sorted deduplicated list, which is where `c81e55`
  measured and where the guards are decided, but I did not characterise the diversity
  policy's response to a reordered band.
* **Weights below 0.05** other than the 1e-9 structural check, and **weights between
  0.15 and 0.20** for the census. The census was measured at 0.10 only; the census is not
  claimed to be monotone in weight.
* **The `[0,1]`-preserving rewrite** (`+w·(v−1)` instead of `+w·v`). I argue in §0 that it
  is ranking-identical, but I did not run it, and it would still not fix
  `approximate_output_is_locked`, which locks score *strings*.
* **The six-axis reweighting family.** Closed by `c81e55` §5 and forbidden here; not
  re-measured, per the steer.
* **Runtime under a landed implementation**, and any runtime claim at weights other than
  `PUNCH` 0.10. `FOOT` and `ECON` compute one extra `abs` per extension and were not
  timed.
* **`d3b7c2`'s determinism fix and `9a41d3`'s branch** were not reviewed beyond reading
  them; I took `3d520c0` as given and re-took the floor on it rather than inheriting
  `b47d02`'s.

## 7. What would have to be true, and the single measurement that would falsify it

For **any** new axis to close the gap, two things must be true, and both are measurable
before any implementation:

1. **The requested wording must be in the top ~0.3 % of the pool on that axis, and the
   axis must have spread *inside* the visible band.** Three of my four candidates fail
   test 1 outright (constant on the band *and* on the clue: `PUNCH`, `ECON`, `LAND`), and
   the fourth passes it (`FOOT`: clue 0.8421 against a band max of 0.825) and still fails
   the second half, because the band reorders as the axis is weighted and overtakes the
   clue again. An axis that closes the gap must be one on which the *band* is
   near-homogeneous and the clue is far above it — a shape none of the per-word
   quantities I could reach has.
2. **It must not cost a green guard.** `wreck a nice beach` sits 0.00127 inside the band,
   so any axis with in-band variance is a coin-flip on it. `FOOT` lost that coin-flip at
   w=0.05; `ECON` at w=0.05; `PUNCH` and `LAND` had no in-band variance to lose with.

**Single measurement that would falsify `PUNCH` as a landable axis** (and it is cheap,
one run, no code): *if the coordinator declines a re-baseline of
`approximate_output_is_locked` and of the `[0,1]` score contract, `PUNCH` is
unlandable regardless of its census effect, and the answer is `NONE`.* The converse
falsifier, if the re-baseline is authorised: *if `PUNCH` at 0.10 does not hold
`approximate_finds_recognize_speech_resegmentation` green and keep
`wreck a nice beach` at raw rank 27 with a positive margin on **two more** ordinary
targets I did not measure, the census gain is target-specific and the axis is not worth
landing either.* Both are already half-answered by the numbers in §2.
